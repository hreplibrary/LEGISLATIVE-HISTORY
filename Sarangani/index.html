SARANGANI PROVINCE

The following text/information are lifted from the book of Lee W. Vance entitled Tracing your Philippine Ancestors which was published in Provo, Utah by Stevenson’s Genealogical Center in 1980 and Symbols of the State by the Department of Interior and Local Government of the Republic of the Philippines in 1998.

 

Sarangani is at the southern tip of the island of Mindanao. General Santos City cuts the province in half at the head of the Sarangani bay. The province is bounded on the north by South Cotabato and Davao del Sur, on the east also by Davao del Sur, on the west by Sultan Kudarat, and on the south by the Celebes Sea.

The province of Sarangani should not be confused with Sarangani Island, which is in the southern part of another province, Davao del Sur.

The old province of Cotabato had earlier been divided into 4 provinces, namely, North Cotabato, (also known simply as Cotabato), Maguindanao, Sultan Kudarat, and South Cotabato. With Sarangani, a 5th province has risen out of that territory. The province is divided into two parts, separated by the Sarangani Bay and General Santos City, and it used to be part of South Cotabato until it was made an independent province in 1992. Before its inception in 1992, Sarangani was part of South Cotabato and held its title as the Lone Third District of South Cotabato.

Sarangani Province was named after Sarangani Bay. The name itself is legendary -- it is about Saranganing, an adventurous son of a Sangil family from the coast of Celebes off Indonesia. His voyages often brought him to the Sultanate of Buayan (now General Santos City), the stronghold of Maguindanaoans. His outstanding character impressed the people that they named the bay in his honor.

Following is a time line1,2,3 of events that led to the creation and development of the province of Sarangani:

 

DATE

EVENT
  • 1942

Japanese troops occupied Sarangani

  • 1945

Filipino troops of the 6th, 101st and 102nd Infantry Division of the Philippine Commonwealth Army and 10th Infantry Regiment of the Philippine Constabulary entered and liberated in Southern Cotabato (now Sarangani) and fought against the Japanese Imperial Army forces during the Battle of Cotabato at the end of World War II under the Japanese Occupation in Cotabato.

  • 1966

Administered as part of the Cotabato Empire during the American period, the area (now Sarangani) was incorporated into the province of South Cotabato.

  • March 16, 1992

The province was created by virtue of REPUBLIC ACT No. 7228, penned by the late Congressman, James L. Chiongbian.

  • 2010
