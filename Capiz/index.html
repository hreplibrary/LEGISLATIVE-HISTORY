HISTORY

CAPIZ

The following text/information are lifted from the book of Lee W. Vance entitled Tracing your Philippine Ancestors which was published in Provo, Utah by Stevenson’s Genealogical Center in 1980 and Symbols of the State by the Department of Interior and Local Government of the Republic of the Philippines in 1998.

The province of Capiz is located on the northeastern part of Panay Island in the Western Visayas region of the Philippines. It is bounded on the north by the Sibuyan Sea, on the east and south by Iloilo on the west by Antique and on the northwest by Aklan.
Capiz has a land area of 2,594.64 square kilometers. In 2007, it had a population of 701,664.  

Following is a timeline 1,2 of events that led to the creation and development of the province of Capiz:

DATE

EVENT
  • February, 1213

The Bornean chieftains with about 1,000 followers arrived in Panay and bought the island from the Negrito inhabitants. They named the island Madia-as and divided them into three:

  • Hantik (Antique)
  • Aklan (Capiz and Aklan)
  • Irong-irong (Iloilo)
  • 1569

Don Miguel Lopez de Legaspi transferred his encampment to Panay island from Cebu and established the first Spanish settlement at the town of Panay. 
At the same time, many native settlements had already been well established in addition to Panay. These included Bulacale, Aclam (Aclan) Dumarao, Ibahay (Ibajay), and Dumalag, which were soon organized by the Spanish into encomiendas.
Batan and Mambusao were organized not long thereafter.
  • 1580

The first center of Christian faith was established in Panay (Capiz).

  • 1596

A center of Christian faith was established in Dumalag (Capiz).
  • 1707

A center of Christian faith was established in Capiz (now Roxas City, Capiz).
  • 1716

Capiz remained part of the old province of Panay when it was organized into a separate politico-military province.  Included in its territory were all of modern Capiz, Aklan, Romblon, and the islands of Maestre de Campo, Tablas and Sibuya, and their smaller dependent islands.
  • 1853

The islands comprising Romblon province, plus Maestre de Campo Island, were organized into a separate politico-military comandancia, administered from Capiz.
  • April 15, 1901

A civil government was established by the Americans for the province.
  • April 4,1903

By virtue of ACT 720, 34 municipalities of Capiz were reduced to 22 by consolidating Banga, Lezo and Numancia with Calibo; Loctugan with Capiz; Cuartero with Dao; Jagnaya with Jamindan; Madalag with Libacao; Batan, Balete, Jimeno, and the Barrio of Lagatic (formerly part of Calibo) to form New Washington; Casanayan with Pilar; Maayon with Pontevedra; and Macate and Tangalan with the new municipality of Taft.
  • July 2, 1907

By virtue of ACT 1665, the province of Romblon was annexed as a sub-province of Capiz except Maestre de Campo Island which was annexed to Mindoro.
  • December 7, 1917

With the approval of Legislative Act 2724, Romblon, together with Maestre de Campo Island, was re-established as a separate province from Capiz.
  • April 11, 1951

By virtue of Republic Act 603, the former municipality of Capiz was chartered as Roxas City.
  • April 25, 1956

By virtue of Republic Act 1414, Aklan achieved independent provincial status, separating it from Capiz.
  • As of 2010

Capiz constitutes two legislative districts. Listed below are the municipalities that comprise the districts:
1st District: Municipalities of Ma-ayon, Panay, Panitan, Pilar, Pontevedra, President Roxas.
2nd District: Municipalities of Cuartero, Dao, Dumalag, Dumarao, Ivisan, Jamindan, Mambusao, Sapi-an or Sapian, Sigma, and Tapaz.
