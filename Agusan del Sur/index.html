HISTORY

AGUSAN DEL SUR


The following text/information are lifted from the book of Lee W. Vance entitled Tracing your Philippine Ancestors which was published in Provo, Utah by Stevenson’s Genealogical Center in 1980 and Symbols of the State by the Department of Interior and Local Government of the Republic of the Philippines in 1998.

 

The province of Agusan del Sur is located in the southern Philippines, in the east-central portion of the island of Mindanao.  It is bounded on the north by Agusan del Norte, on the west by Bukidnon and a small portion of Misamis Oriental, on the south by Davao del Norte and Davao Oriental, and by Surigao del Sur on the east.
Agusan del Sur shares a common history with its sister province, Agusan del Norte.  Until 1967, these two provinces comprised the single province of Agusan. 

Agusan derived its name from the word agus, which refers to flowing water.  It probably refers to the Agusan River, which is navigable, and which in earlier days was the major means of transportation through the province.  When the rains cause it to swell, portions of the valley floor are flooded.  Early settlers minimized the resulting damage by living in houseboats or by building their homes on buoys or rafts which floated whenever the floodwaters came.  Thus, the Agusan River has played a prominent role in shaping not only the landscape, but also the culture and architecture of the province.

Following is a time line1, 2 of events that led to the creation and development of the province of Agusan del Sur:

DATE

E V E N T
  • Prior to 1521

When the Europeans first arrived in Agusan, portions of the province were already inhabited by natives trading with the Chinese and Portuguese.
  • 1521 - 1565

Magellan is believed to have passed Agusan del Sur on his way to Cebu.  He was followed by the Portuguese explorer De Castro in 1538, and the Spanish explorers Villalobos in 1543 and Legazpi in 1565.
  • 1596

The first permanent missions were established by Jesuit missionaries who arrived in Butuan in 1596.
  • 1622

The Jesuits were replaced by Augustinians Recollects who established missions in Linao, or what is now known as Bunawan.
  • 1860

The East District of Mindanao was established.  This politico-military government in Mindanao was made up of Agusan del Norte, Agusan del Sur, Surigao del Norte, Surigao del Sur, and part of Davao del Norte and Davao Oriental.

This district extended from Butuan Bay in the northwest to Caraga Bay, in modern Davao Oriental, in the southeast.  It included those inland settlements under Spanish influence, along an indefinite and slowly progressing boundary which moved as Spanish colonization proceeded slowly inland from the coast. 

  • 1870

The east district of Mindanao was called the District of Surigao.
  • 1898

Agusan had been formed into a politico-military commandancia under the administrative jurisdiction of the province of Surigao.  It was officially named “Butuan,” but was popularly known as the “Manobo Country,” after a Muslim tribe predominating in northeastern Mindanao.
  • 1901

Agusan was made a sub-province of Surigao.
  • 1907

Agusan was created as a regular province pursuant to Act 1693.  It included the sub-province of Butuan and Bukidnon which covered the Misamis province and the following Spanish pueblos: Butuan, Nasipit, Cabadbaran, Tolosa, Jabonga, Tubay, La Paz, Talacagon and Verbena.

The Spanish pueblos pueblos were organized into three municipalities. Nasipit was annexed to Butuan, Tolosa, Jabonga and Tubay to Cabarbaran (Cabadbaran) while Verbena and La Paz were annexed to Talacogon.
  • 1914

The sub-province of Bukidnon was made a separate province pursuant to Act 2408.  Agusan comprised what is now Agusan del Norte and Agusan del Sur.
  • 1935

Agusan was made up of the following municipalities: Butuan, Cabadbaran, Jabonga, Nasipit and Talacogon; municipal districts: Axpitia, Bahbah, Balete, Baquingquing,  Basa, Baylo, Borbon, Bunaguit,  Banawan, Concordia, Corinto, Cuevas, Ebro, Esperanza, Gracia, Guadalupe, Halapitan, Langasian, La Paz, Las Nieves, Libertad, Loreto, Los Arcos, Maasin, Mambalili, Mampinsahan, Maygatasan, Milagros, Novele, Nuevo Sibagat, Nuevo Trabajo, Patrocinio, Prosperidad, Remedios, Rosario,  Sagunto, Salvacion, San Ignacio, San Luis, San Isidro, Santa Ines, Santa Josefa, Santo Tomas, Trento, Tudela, Verdu, Verbena, Violanta, Waloe.
  • 1947

Two new municipalities were organized: Buenavista and Tubay while the municipality of Axpitia was dissolved.
  • 1967

The province of Agusan was divided into the two provinces of Agusan del Norte and Agusan del Sur with the passage of Republic Act 4979, approved June 17 1967, and proclaimed January 8, 1968.

At that time, Agusan del Sur was comprised of the municipalities of Bayugan, Bunawan, La Paz Loreto, San Francisco, Santa Josefa, Talacogon. Veruela and Prosperidad.
  • 2010

The lone legislative district of Agusan del Sur is now composed of the municipalities of Bayugan, Bunawan, Esperanza, La Paz, Loreto, Prosperidad, Rosario, San Francisco, San Luis, Santa Josefa, Sibagat, Talacogon, Trento, and Veruela.
