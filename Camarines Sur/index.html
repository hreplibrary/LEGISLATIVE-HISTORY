HISTORY

CAMARINES SUR

The following text/information are lifted from the book of Lee W. Vance entitled Tracing your Philippine Ancestors which was published in Provo, Utah by Stevenson’s Genealogical Center in 1980 and Symbols of the State by the Department of Interior and Local Government of the Republic of the Philippines in 1998.

Camarines Sur is located at the center of the Bicol Peninsula of southeastern Luzon. The province is bounded on the north by the Pacific Ocean, San Miguel Bay, its sister province of Camarines Norte, and the province of Quezon; by Lagonoy Gulf on the east; by Albay province on the south; and by Ragay Gulf on the west.

Camarines Sur has a land area of 5,380.78 square kilometers. In 2007, it had a population of 1,693,821.

Following is a timeline 1,2 of events that led to the creation and development of the province of Camarines Sur:

DATE

EVENT
  • 1573

The town of “Villa” of Santiago de Libon (or Libon) was accordingly established, inaugurated and renamed Nueva Caceres.
  • 1573

Camarines existed a single province, which originally included entire Bicol Peninsula (with modern Camarines Norte, Camarines Sur, Albay and Sorsogon) and Catanduanes, Masbate, Ticao and Burias islands and all their dependent islands.

  • 1578

Missionaries had established parishes at Naga, Nabua and Quipayo.
  • 1581

Daet and Indan (now Vinzons, Camarines Norte) were founded.
  • 1583

Minalabac parishes were founded.
  • 1585

Milaor parishes were founded.
  • 1586

Libmanan parishes were founded.
  • 1590

Baao parishes were founded.
  • 1595

The Diocese of Nueva Caceres was erected in and included the same territory as the old province of Camarines.
  • 1599

Canaman parishes were founded.
  • 1829

The province of Camarines was divided into two, Camarines Norte and Camarines Sur.
As then constituted, Camarines Sur had four main sections, namely: (a) the District of Nueva Caceres, consisting of the towns of Tabaco, Naga, Camaligan, Canaman, Magarao, Bombon, Quipayo, Calabanga, Libmanan, Milaor, San Fernando and Minalabac; (b) the District of Rinconada, consisting of the towns of Bula, Baao, Nabua, Iriga, Buhi and Bato; (c) the District of Iriga consisting of the towns of Libon, Polangui, Oas, Ligao, Camalig and Cagsawa (now Daraga); and (d) the District of Isarog, consisting of the town of Goa, Tigaon, Tinambac and the mission of Manguirin.
  • 1846

Governor-General Narciso Claveria realigned the boundaries of the Bicol Peninsula provinces.
The towns of Sipocot, Lupi and Ragay were annexed to Camarines Sur from Camarines Norte (comprising the area between the Bicol River and Tayabas and the Ragay Gulf), in exchange for Siruma, which was annexed to Camarines Norte from Camarines Sur.  Lagonoy, Caramoan, and Sagnay (comprising e Caramoan Peninsula), were transferred to Camarines Sur from Albay, and Camalig, Guinobatan, Ligao, Oas, Polangui, Libon, Mauraro, Quipia and Donzol were separated from Camarines Sur and annexed to Albay.
  • 1854

The two Camarines provinces were reunited into one province to form the Province of Ambos Camarines.
  • 1857

The two Camarines provinces were separated again.
  • 1893

The two Camarines provinces were reunited into a single province.
  • April 27, 1901

After the Philippine Revolution, the Americans established a civil government for what was termed the province of “Ambos Camarines”.

  • March, 1919

The province was divided again. Camarines Sur then consisting of 31 towns: Cagusao, Canaman, Calabanga, Camaligan, Gainza, San Fernando, Libmanan, Lupi, Magarao, Milaor, Minalabag, Naga, Pamplona, Pasacao, Ragay, Sipocot, Baao, Buhi, Bulo, Bato, Caramoan, Goa, Iriga, Lagonoy, Nabua, Pili, Sagnay, San Jose, Siruma, Tigaon, and Tinambac. 

  • 1948

The provincial capital was transferred from Naga City to Pili municipality.
  • June 18, 1948

Naga City was re-chartered.
  • December, 1948

Naga City was inaugurated as a chartered city.

  • June 21, 1959

By virtue of Republic Act (RA) 2599, the names of some barrios of the municipality of Goa were changed.
  • June 19, 1960

By virtue of RA 2778, certain barrios in the municipality of Sagnay were created.
  • June 18, 1961

By virtue of RA 3367, the barrio of Quinale in the municipality of Calabanga was created.

By virtue of RA 3377, certain barrios in the municipality of Lupi were declared as duly constituted barrios.

By virtue of RA 3421, the municipality of Parubcan was created.
  • June 18, 1964

By virtue of RA 4002, the barrio Tiltilan changed the name to San Jose in the municipality of Tinambac.

  • June 15, 1968

Iriga City was chartered.
  • October 12, 2009

By virtue of RA 9716, a new district was created through the reapportionment. As stated in Section 3, the result of the reapportionment described in this Act are summarized as follows:

1. First District – The remaining municipalities in the current First (1st) Legislative District shall continue to be designated as the First (1st) Legislative District, composed of the following municipalities: Del Gallego, Ragay, Lupi, Sipicot and Cabusao;

2. Second District – The new legislative district shall be composed of the municipalities enumerated in Section 2: the municipalities of Libmanan, Minalabac, Pamplona, Pasacao and San Fernando of the current First (1st) Legislative District are hereby consolidated with the municipalities of Gainza and Milaor of the current Second (2nd) Legislative District, to comprise the new legislative district authorized under this Act.

3. Third District – The current Second (2nd) Legislative District shall be renamed as the Third (3rd) Legislative District, composed of the following: Naga City and the municipalities of Pili, Ocampo, Camaligan, Canaman, Magarao, Bombon and Calabanga;

4. Fourth District – The current Third (3rd) Legislative District, without any change in its composition, shall be renamed as the Fourth (4th) Legislative District, composed of the following municipalities: Caramoan, Garchitorena, Goa, Lagonoy, Presentacion, Sangay, San Jose, Tigaon, Tinambac and Siruma; and

5. Fifth District – The current Fourth (4th) Legislative District, without any change in its composition, shall be renamed as the Fifth (5th) Legislative District, composed of the following: Iriga City and the municipalities of Baao, Balatan, Bato, Buhi, Bula and Nabua.
  • 2010

In 2010 (14th Congress), Camarines Sur was represented by four legislators from the four legislative districts. Listed below are the municipalities that comprise the districts:

1st District: Municipalities of Cabusao, Del Gallego, Libmanan, Lupi, Minalabac, Pamplona, Pasacao, Ragay, San Fernando and Sipocot.
2nd District: Municipalities of Bombon, Calabanga, Camaligan, Canaman, Gainza, Magarao, Milaor, Ocampo, Pili, and Naga City.
3rd District: Municipalities of Caramoan, Garchitorena, Goa,Lagonoy, Presentacion, Sagñay, San Jose, Siruma, Tigaon, and Tinambac.
4th District: Municipalities of Baao, Balatan, Bato, Buhi, Bula, Nabua, and Iriga City.
  • As of 2013

During the 15th Congress, Camarines Sur constitutes five (5) legislative districts represented by a legislator for each district. Listed below are the municipalities that comprise the districts:

1st District – Municipalities of Del Gallego, Ragay, Lupi, Sipicot and Cabusao.

2nd District – This the new legislative district which is composed of the municipalities enumerated in Section 2 of RA 9716: the municipalities of Libmanan, Minalabac, Pamplona, Pasacao, San Fernando, Gainza and Milaor.

3rd District – Naga City and the municipalities of Pili, Ocampo, Camaligan, Canaman, Magarao, Bombon and Calabanga.

4th District – Municipalities of Caramoan, Garchitorena, Goa, Lagonoy, Presentacion, Sangay, San Jose, Tigaon, Tinambac and Siruma; and

5th District – Iriga City and the municipalities of Baao, Balatan, Bato, Buhi, Bula and Nabua.
