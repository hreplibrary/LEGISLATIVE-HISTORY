HISTORY

CAVITE

The following text/information are lifted from the book of Lee W. Vance entitled Tracing your Philippine Ancestors which was published in Provo, Utah by Stevenson’s Genealogical Center in 1980 and Symbols of the State by the Department of Interior and Local Government of the Republic of the Philippines in 1998.

The province of Cavite lies south of the entrance to Manila Bay, and forms much of the southwest corner of Luzon Island.  It is bounded on the northeast by Rizal province, on the east by Laguna, on the south by Batangas, and it has a long shoreline fronting Manila Bay on the northwest. 

Cavite has a land area of 1,512.41 square kilometers. In 2007, it had a population of 2,856,765.  

Following is a timeline1,2 of events that led to the creation and development of the province of Cavite:

DATE

EVENT
  • 1571

The first Christian missionaries in the province arrived and early Christianization centers were established at Silang.
  • 1587

Christianization centers were established at Kawit.
  • 1614

Cavite was organized as a separate politico-military province.
  • 1627

Christianization centers were established at Maragondon.
  • 1700

Christianization centers were established at Ternate.
  • 1754

Maragondon and its neighboring territory was annexed to Cavite from the corregimiento of Mariveles (Bataan) to which it had formerly belonged. Corregidor Island was also transferred to the jurisdiction of Cavite at this time.
  • September 12, 1896

The Thirteen Martyrs of Cavite were executed by the Spaniards on suspicion of complicity to overthrow the Spanish government.
  • May 2, 1898

The Americans occupied the area following the Battle of Manila Bay.
  • June 12, 1898

Emilio Aguinaldo proclaimed Philippine Independence at his residence in Kawit Cavite. Bacoor became the revolutionary capital.
  • September 9, 1898

The seat of government was transferred to Malolos, Bulacan.
  • June 11, 1901

Under the Philippine Commission Act 138, the revolutionary government was displaced by an American military rule, followed by a civil government.

At this year, Lubang Islands, formerly of Mindoro, were annexed to Cavite.
  • June 23, 1902

The Lubang Island group was detached from Cavite and annexed to Marinduque, and at the same time Mindoro province was dissolved and annexed to Marinduque province.
  • November 10, 1902

By virtue of the Philippine Commission Act 423, Mindoro province was re-established, and the Lubang Islands remained with Mindoro.
  • March 28, 1903
 By virtue of ACT 707, the three municipalities of Cavite, San Roque, and La Caridad, in the Province of Cavite,were consolidated into one municipality under the name of Cavite.
  • 1903

By virtue of Philippine Act 947, the 23 municipalities of Cavite were reduced to 11 by consolidating Bailen and Mendez-Nunez with Alfonso; La Caridad and San Roque with Cavite; Bacoor and Perez Dasmarinas with Imus; Magallanes with Maragondong; Ternate with Naic; Cavite Viejo and Rosario with Noveleta; Santa Cruz de Malabon with San Francisco de Malabon; and Amadeo with Silang.

  • October 25, 1906
 By virtue of ACT 1551, the 11 municipalities of the province of cavite increased into 12, separating the former municipality of Bacoor from IMUS.
  • September 20, 1907

By virtue of ACT 1718, the 12 municipalities of the province of Cavite increased into 13, by separating the former municipality of Cavite Viejo from Noveleta and renamed it to Kawit.
  • 1909

By virtue of Executive Order 124, the municipal government on Corregidor Island that was formed during the Spanish rule was dissolved and annexed to Cavite municipality.

  • February 28, 1914

ACT 2390,the municipalities of San Francisco de Malabon and Santa Cruz de Malabon, Province of Cavite, were changed to Malabon and Tanza, respectively.
  • December 5, 1933
 ACT 4102 created a committe to fix the boundary line between the municipality of Talisay, Province of Batangas, and the adjacent municipalities of Silang, Amadeo, Mendez, and Alfonso, Province of Cavite.
  • June 21, 1938

By virtue of CA  338, Tagaytay became a chartered city.
  • September 6, 1938
 ACT 397 amended the certain sections of CA 338 known as the charter city of Tagaytay.
  • May 26, 1940
 By virtue of CA 547, the municipality of Cavite became a chartered city.
  • 1954

The provincial capital formerly at the municipality of Cavite was transferred to the new city of Trece Martires when it was chartered.
  • June 19, 1965

By virtue of Republic Act (RA) 4346, the municipality of Bailen was changed to the municipality of General Emilio Aguinaldo.

At the same time, by virtue of RA 4348, the name of barrio Tejero in the municipality of Rosario, was changed to barrio Tejeros Convention.
  • June 11, 1977

By virtue of RA 981, the provincial capital was moved to Imus municipality.
  • June 13, 1980
 By virtue of BP 75, Gen. Mariano Alvarez was created as a municipality.
  • October 10, 2009
 By virtue of RA 9723, the municipality of Dasmarinas was converted into a component city of the province of Cavite.
  • October 22, 2009
 By virtue of RA 9727,the province was reapportioned into seven (7) legislative districts.
  • April 10, 2012

By virtue of RA 10160 ,the municipality of Bacoor was converted into a component city of the province of Cavite.

At the same time, by virtue of RA 10161,the municipality of Imus was converted into a component city of the province of Cavite.
  • As of 2013

Cavite constitutes seven legislative districts. Listed below are the municipalities that comprise the districts:

1st District: Kawit, Noveleta, Rosario, and Cavite City

2nd District: Bacoor

3rd District:Imus City

4th District: Dasmarinas City

5th District: Carmona, Gen. Mariano Alvarez, Silang

6th District: Trece Martirez City, General Trias, Tanza, Amadeo

7th District: Tagaytay City, Alfonso, General Aguinaldo, Indang, Magallanes, Maragondon, Mendez, Naic, Ternate
