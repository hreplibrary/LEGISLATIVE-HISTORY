HISTORY

SIQUIJOR


The following text/information are lifted from the book of Lee W. Vance entitled Tracing your Philippine Ancestors which was published in Provo, Utah by Stevenson’s Genealogical Center in 1980 and Symbols of the State by the Department of Interior and Local Government of the Republic of the Philippines in 1998.

 

The province of Siquijor lies southeast of the Negros Island and Cebu Island, and southwest of Bohol Island, in the southern Visayas region of the central Philippines.  It is separated by the Mindanao Sea from the northern coast of Mindanao.

Siquijor’s early names include “Isla de Fuego”, or “Fire Island”, given by early Spaniards who saw hundreds of fireflies harbored in the Molave trees abounding on the island.  It was also called “Katugasan”, a native Visayan word meaning “a place where Tugas grows”, after the many tugas or molave, trees on the island.  Finally, the name “Siquijor” is thought to be a Spanish corruption of “Sikihod”, after the legendary ruler of the island, Datu Kihod.

Following is a time line 1,2 of events that led to the creation and development of the province of Siquijor:

DATE

E V E N T
  • 1565

Miguel Lopez de Legaspi first sighted Siquijor in 1565.
  • 1783

Siquijor was established in 1783 under the administration of the secular clergymen.
  • 1854 - 1892

From the earliest part of the Spanish conquest until 1854, Siquijor was administered from Cebu.  But in 1854, Bohol province was split off from Cebu, and it incorporated Siquijor within its jurisdiction until the end of the Spanish era.  Siquijor remained with Bohol for the first few years of the American Regime.

  • 1903

Philippine Commission Act 715, which was enacted in April 2, 1903, reduced the six existing municipalities in the sub-province to form three, by combining Canoan with Larena, Maria with Lazi, and San Juan with Siquijor municipality.
  • October 8, 1907

Siquijor was annexed as a sub-province to Negros Oriental by virtue of Philippine Commission Act 1753, enacted Oct 8, 1907. 

  • September 17, 1971

Republic Act 6398 separated Siquijor from Negros Oriental, and declared it to be a regular province.  By then its municipalities included Enrique Villanueva, Maria, Lazi, Larena (the capital), and San Juan.
  • September 12, 1972

Presidential Proclamation No 1075, dated Sept 12, 1972, transferred the provincial capital from Larena to Siquijor.
  • 2010

The lone district of Siquijor is made up of the municipalities of Enrique Villanueva, Larena, Lazi, Maria, San Juan, and Siquijor.
