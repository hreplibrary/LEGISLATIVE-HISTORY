HISTORY

QUEZON CITY

The following text/information are lifted from the book of Lee W. Vance entitled Tracing your Philippine Ancestors which was published in Provo, Utah by Stevenson’s Genealogical Center in 1980 and Symbols of the State by the Department of Interior and Local Government of the Republic of the Philippines in 1998.

Quezon City is bounded on the north, east and south by the province of Rizal, northwest by the City of Caloocan, and west by the City of Manila.

Quezon City has a land area of 166.20 square kilometers. In 2007, it had a population of 2,679,450.

Following is a timeline1, 2 of events that led to the creation and development of Quezon City:

DATE

EVENT
  • 13th Century

Ancient settlements were already thriving in Paranaque, Pasig, Cainta and Taytay.  These settlements were believed to be known to Chinese, Japanese and Indian traders.
  • 1571

Juan de Salcedo visited Taytay and Cainta and brought these towns under Spanish sovereignty.
  • 1572 - 1638

Christian missions established parishes in Pasig (1572), San Mateo (1572), Parañaque (1575), Antipolo (1578), Makati (1578), Morong (1586), Taguig (1587), Pililla (1590), Baras (1595), Taytay (1595), Tanay (1606), Binangonan (1621), and Marikina (1630).
  • 1639

A Chinese uprising caused great rioting throughout Rizal.  The Chinese burned the churches of Taytay, San Mateo and Pasig. The Spaniards were able to quell the uprising but not without great loss of life and property.
  • 1689

The town of Cainta was founded in 1689 while Marikina was founded the following year.
  • 1762

British troops under the command of Captain Thomas Backhouse invaded Pasig, defeated the Spaniards and occupied Taytay and Cainta.
  • 1764

The British withdrew from the islands.
  • 1815

The town of Pateros was founded.
  • 1853

“Distrito de los Montes de San Mateo”, or District of the San Mateo Mountains was formed.  Cainta, Taytay, Antipolo and Bosoboso were annexed to this new district from Tondo province.  Angono, Binangonan, Cardona, Morong, Baras, Tanay, Pililla and Jalajala were annexed to the district from Laguna province.  Morong was made the capital of the District.
  • 1859

The name Distrito de Los Montes de San Mateo was changed to Morong District.  At about the same time, Tondo province was renamed Manila province.  At about this time, Navotas was founded.
  • 1898

American military forces controlled the district.
  • June 11, 1901

Rizal province was created by fusing the former province of Manila, except the City of Manila, with the former district of Morong.  Pasig was declared the provincial capital.
  • 1903

Rizal’s various municipalities were combined as follows - Montalban with San Mateo; Taguig and Muntinglupa with Pateros; Malibay with Pasay; Las Pinas with Paranaque, Novaliches with Caloocan; Navotas with Malabon, San Felipe Neri (Mandaluyong) with San Juan del Monte, Cainta and Angono with Taytay; Bosoboso and Teresa with Antipolo, Binangonan, Baras and cardona with Morong; and Quisao and Jalajala with Pililla. 

A month later Binangonan was separated from Morong and Angono was annexed to it.  At the same time, Muntinglupa was separated from Pateros, and annexed as a barrio to Binan, Laguna and later annexed (still as a barrio) to Taguig, itself newly formed from the former pueblos of Pateros, Taguig and Muntinglupa.
  • October 12, 1939

The Philippine National Assembly enacted Commonwealth Act No. 502 which created the City of Quezon.
  • October 12, 1939- November 4, 1939

Quezon assumed the position of the first City Mayor. He appointed Tomas Morato as his successor.
  • January 2, 1947

The city was formally reconstituted and restored to its pre-war status by virtue of Republic Act (RA) No. 54.
  • July 17, 1947

By virtue of RA 333, the City of Quezon was made as the official capital of the Philippines.
  • 1948

Quezon City was declared the national capital of the new Republic of the Philippines, thus robbing Manila City of an honor it had held since 1595.
  • June 18, 1949
 Republic Act 392 redefined the territorial boundaries of Quezon City.
  • October 22, 1949

The city was inaugurated as the National Capital through the laying of a cornerstone at Constitution Hills.
  • June 16, 1950

By virtue of RA 537, the city charter was revised and the boundaries and limits increased to its present area, of which 62 percent are for residential and commercial purposes, 14 percent for thoroughfares and public utility terminals, 14 percent for the city government needs, 7 percent for public parks and playgrounds, and 3 percent for agricultural purposes.
  • June 18, 1952
 By virtue of Republic Act 727, Barrio Talipapa was renamed Barrio Tandang Sora.
  • June 16, 1956
 Republic Act 1575 amended and repealed certain sections of the Quezon City Charter.
  • June 22, 1957

Republic Act 1990 amended Republic Act 1162 by including Quezon City and its suburbs within its purview.
  • September 9, 1968
 Republic Act 5441 amended certain sections of the Quezon City Charter.
  • August 26, 1972
 Republic Act 6548 amended certain sections of the Quezon City Charter.
  • June 24, 1975

By virtue of Presidential Decree 940, the Capital of the Philippines and the permanent seat of the National Government were transferred to Manila.
  • February 25, 1983
 Batas Pambansa 343 created Barangay Batasan Hills in Quezon City.
  • June 24, 1983

By virtue of Batas Pambansa 546, the Barangay Holy Spirit in Quezon City, Metropolitan Manila was created.
  • March 7, 1984

By virtue of Batas Pambansa 687, Barangay Matalahib was renamed Barangay Sto. Domingo. Similarly, Batas Pambansa 688 renamed Barangay Gintong Silahis as Barangay S. Amoranto.
