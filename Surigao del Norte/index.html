HISTORY

SURIGAO DEL NORTE


The following text/information are lifted from the book of Lee W. Vance entitled Tracing your Philippine Ancestors which was published in Provo, Utah by Stevenson’s Genealogical Center in 1980 and Symbols of the State by the Department of Interior and Local Government of the Republic of the Philippines in 1998.

 

The province of Surigao del Norte is situated at the northernmost tip of the island of Mindanao. It includes several groups of offshore islands, the most important of which are the Dinagat, Siargao and Bucas Grande Islands.  It is bounded on the southeast by its Surigao del Sur, and on the southwest by Agusan del Norte.  It is separated from Southern Leyte to the northwest by the Surigao Strait.

Following is a time line1,2 of events that led to the creation and development of the province of Surigao Del Norte:

DATE

E V E N T
  • 1538

The Portugese explorer, Francisco de Castro first visited the eastern coast of Mindanao and found the area inhabited by the Caraga tribe.
  • 1543

The Spanish explorer Ruy Lopez de Villalobos visited the region.  His navigator, Bernardo de la Torre, named the place “Cesarea Caroli” in honor of King Carlos V, the reigning monarch of Spain.
  • 1609

The ancient province of Caraga, established in 1609, was comprised of what is now Surigao del Norte, Surigao del Sur, Agusan del Norte, Agusan del Sur, the northern part of Davao Oriental (far south as Caraga), and eastern Misamis Oriental (as far west as Gingoog).    
Juan de Vega led a military expedition to subdue the hostile natives and release some 1,500 Christian prisoners.He established a fort and garrison at Tandag which became the seat of Spanish authority in the region.
  • 1818

Misamis was split off and made a separate corregimiento.

  • 1849

The new province of “Nueva Guipuzcoa”, later named “Davao” was created. 
The towns annexed to the new province from Caraga included the former capital, Tandag, and also Tago, Lianga, Mission de San Juan, Bislig, Hinatuan, Lingig,  Quinablangan, Dapa and Baganga.  With the assignment of its capital to Davao, the provincial capital for Caraga was moved to Surigao town that same year.  Soon thereafter, the entire province was renamed Surigao after its new capital.
  • 1855

The province of Nueva Guipuzcoa was divided into the politico-military comandancias of Bislig and Davao.  Bislig comandancia comprised the territory formerly belonging to Caraga (Surigao) province.

The pueblos of Bislig included, Caraga, Baganga, Dapnan, Quinablangan, Cateel, Bislig, Ginatuan, San Juan, Lianga, ago Tandag, Linao, Mampanon, Tubud Manurigao and Manay.
  • 1860

The governments throughout Mindanao were reorganized into six politico-military districts.
Bislig comandancia, was annexed to Surigao District, thus retuning many of its former towns to its jurisdiction.  Surigao then comprised the Third, or East, District of Mindanao.  It included all the coastal territory of northeastern Mindanao under Spanish influence from Butuan Bay to Caraga Bay.
  • September 4, 1890

Loreto became a Spanish pueblo under the supervision of Gov. Gen. Manuel Sanchez by virtue of Direccion General No. 30. It was originally called "Mabua" because of the ever-foaming river that runs right into the center of the community. It was named “Loreto” in 1881 by the Spanish priest who frequents the town, in honor of the wife of the Alcalde Mayor of the Province of Surigao.
  • 1898

All Spanish citizens vacated the district, and the government was controlled briefly by the native officers sympathetic to the revolution.  Thereafter, the United States military forces took over the province’s administration.
  • 1901

Civil government was restored in the province. On May 15, 1901, Butuan became a sub-province of Surigao.  Butuan comprised what is now the two Agusan provinces.
  • 1903

The Moro Province was created in Mindanao, consisting of five districts including all of the pueblos of Bislig commandancia.
The 34 municipalities of Surigao were reduced to 29. The following year, they were further reduced to only 12.
  • 1907

Butuan became a separate province and named Agusan.
  • October 17, 1944

The Municipality of Loreto became the entry point of the American Liberation Forces. It was on this shore that the 6th Ranger Battalion of the 6th U.S. Army under Col. Mucci landed at Sitio Campinta of Barangay Panamaon, Loreto. It was during this landing that the first American flag flew on Philippine soil.  They named the place "Black Beach No. 2."
  • December 23, 1959

Cagdianao was created into a municipality under Executive Order No. 367. Its name came from the Spanish word "Cada dia linao" meaning everyday peaceful and calm. However some scholars say that its name came from the Visayan word "Taga Danaw" meaning the lake people.
  • February 29, 1960

Libjo was created into a municipality and named Albor under EXECUTIVE ORDER NO. 381. It was taken from the municipalities of Dinagat and Loreto. "Albor" came from the surname of the mayors of Loreto and Dinagat - Alfaro and Borja.
  • June 19, 1960

Surigao province was divided to form Surigao del Norte and Surigao del Sur by virtue of REPUBLIC ACT NO. 2786. 
At the time of its creation, Surigao del Norte comprised the municipalities of Surigao (the capital), Dinagat, Loreto, Dapa, Numancia, General Luna, Pilar, Sapao, Malimono, Anao-aon, Placer, Tagana-an, Mainit, Tubod, Bacuag, Gigaquit, Claver, Sison, San Isidro, Albor and Cagdianao.
  • June 18, 1966

By virtue of REPUBLIC ACT 4786, the name of the municipality of Numancia, province of Surigao Del Norte, was changed to Del Carmen
  • June 17, 1967

It was renamed as Libjo. The name came from the word "Liboo" which is a kind of mussel clam.
  • June 17, 1967

By virtue of REPUBLIC ACT 5093, the municipality of Burgos in the province of Surigao Del Norte was created.
  • June 17, 1967

Basilisa became a municipality under REPUBLIC ACT NO. 4986. It was then named as Rizal, after the country’s national hero.
  • June 15, 1968

By virtue of REPUBLIC ACT 5239, the municipality of Alegria in the province of Surigao Del Norte was created.
  • June 21, 1969

By virtue of REPUBLIC ACT 5641, the name of the municipality of Sapao, province of Surigao Del Norte was changed to Sta. Maria.
  • June 21, 1969

By virtue of REPUBLIC ACT 5643, the municipality of Tubajon in the province of Surigao Del Norte was created.
  • June 21, 1969

By virtue of REPUBLIC ACT 5775, the municipality of Basilisa in the province of Surigao Del Norte was created.
  • June 19, 1971

By virtue of REPUBLIC ACT 6218, the name of the municipality of Anao-aon, province of Surigao Del Norte, was changed to San Francisco.
  • September 17, 1971

By virtue of REPUBLIC ACT 6396, the municipality of San Benito in the province of Surigao Del Norte was created.
  • November 15, 1989

By virtue of REPUBLIC ACT 6769, the municipality of San Jose in the province of Surigao Del Norte was created.
  • October 2, 2006

By virtue of REPUBLIC ACT 9355, the Province of Dinagat Islands was created.
  • 2010

Surigao del Norte is now made up of the municipalities of Burgos, Dapa, Del Carmen, General Luna, Pilar, San Benito, San Isidro, Santa Monica, Socoro, Alegria, Bacuag, Claver, Gigaquit, Mainit, Malimono, Placer, san Francisco, Sison, Tagana-an, tubod, and Surigao City.
