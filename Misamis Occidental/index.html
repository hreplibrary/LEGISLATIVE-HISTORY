HISTORY

MISAMIS OCCIDENTAL

The following text/information are lifted from the book of Lee W. Vance entitled Tracing your Philippine Ancestors which was published in Provo, Utah by Stevenson’s Genealogical Center in 1980 and Symbols of the State by the Department of Interior and Local Government of the Republic of the Philippines in 1998.

The province of Misamis Occidental is the smallest of the three provinces occupying the Zamboanga Peninsula of northwestern Mindanao in the southern Philippines.  It is located on the eastern tip of the peninsula, near the narrow isthmus connecting Zamboanga del Sur to the Mindanao mainland.  The semi-circular coastline of Misamis Occidental faces the Mindanao Sea to the north and east, and the narrow Panquil Bay (with Lanao del Norte beyond it) to the southeast.  Zamboanga del Norte and Zamboanga del Sur share is nearly straight western border.

Until 1929, Misamis Occidental and MIsamis Oriental comprised the single province of Misamis. Therefore, much of their history is common to both.

The name “Misamis” is a Spanish corruption of the word “kayamis”, a variety of sweet coconut which was the staple food of the early inhabitants of the area.  The original inhabitants were the Bukidnons, but they migrated inland as droves of Cebuano-speaking Visayan immigrants settled along the coast.  Misamis was already well populated when the first Spaniard, Christian missionaries, explored it.

Following is a time line 1,2 of events that led to the creation and development of the province of Misamis Occidental:

DATE

EVENT
  • 1596

Jesuits first arrived in the area.
  • 1622

Augustinian Recollects arrived in the Misamis area and established missions in Cagayan (de Oro) and at Catarman on Camiguin Island.  At that time, these areas were part of the dominion of Sultan Corralat (Kudarat), the famous Mohammedan ruler of Mindanao and Sulu.  The ruler of the district they inhabited was a powerful and trusted subordinate of the sultan, named Datu Salanpang.
  • 1624

Jesuits returned to Misamis in 1624 and were given the territory from Punta Sulawan westward.
  • 1768

Jesuits were expelled from Spain and its dominions. They were succeeded by Augustinian Recollects, who erected 27 parishes in the area.
  • 1860

The Jesuits returned.
  • 1899

Spanish priests evacuated the province.
  • 1921

American Jesuits replaced the Spanish priests.
  • 1938

The Columban Fathers succeeded the Jesuits in Misamis Occidental.

  • 1818

Misamis became a corrigimiento, comprised of four partidos, or divisions:  Partido de Dapitan, including the towns of Lobungan, Dapitan, and several small adjacent villages long since renamed or destroyed.  Partido De Cagayan, including the settlements at Iponan, Salay, Hasaan (Jasaan), Molugan and Cagayan; and the Partido de Catarman, on the island of Camiguin, including the villages of Catarman, Guinsiliban, Mambajao and Sagay.
  • 1850

Misamis was made one of the four political divisions, or “districts”, into which the Christian settlements of Mindanao were organized.  Simultaneously, its boundaries were enlarged to encompass nearly the entire northern coast of Mindanao, including most of modern Zamboanga del Norte, Misamis Occidental, Lanao del Norte, Misamis Oriental, Camiguin and Bukidnon.  The district’s capital was at the pueblo of Misamis (Ozamiz).
  • 1898

By the end of the Spanish regime in 1898, there were seven such districts in Mindanao, and Misamis included dependent comandancias in Dapitan (to which Jose Rizal had earlier been exiled), Dipolog and Lobunga.  Its capital was then at Cagayan de MIsamis (Cagayan de Oro).
  • 1896-1898

The Spanish defeat in the Philippine Revolution of 1896 – 1898 forced the evacuation of all Spanish citizens, government leaders and priests from Misamis in December of 1898.
  • 1899

American troops arrived in Misamis, meeting armed resistance from the provincial militia, which was not fully overcome until April, 1901.

  • May 15, 1901

A civil government was established for the Christian towns of Misamis province, the non-Christians being largely left to themselves for several years more.  The province was then defined to include the territory of modern Misamis Occidental, Misamis Oriental, and those parts of Bukidnon (its northern half) and Lanao del Norte lying north of the 8th parallel of latitude by virtue of Philippine Commission Act 128.
  • June 1, 1902

Misamis became geographically divided by an intervening province for the first time.  The western portion corresponded to modern Misamis Occidental, and the eastern portion to Misamis Oriental, Camiguin and the northern half of Bukidnon, by virtue of Philippine Commission Act 787.
  • June 1, 1903

With the creation of the new Moro Province, the boundaries of Misamis were revised.  Iligan, formerly a pueblo of Misamis, together with the surrounding territory (which included part of Initao municipality), was annexed to the new Lanao District of Moro Province.
  • October 22, 1903

The remaining 24 municipalities of Misamis were reduced to 10 by means of consolidation by virtue of Philippine Commission Act 951. A system of local government for non-Christian tribes was also established, following the pattern used in Nueva Vizcaya.
  • March 20, 1907

By virtue of ACT NO. 1618, the number of municipalities in the province of Misamis was increased from 10 to 11, by separating from Talisayan the former municipality of Gingoog, giving the latter the territory which it comprised prior to the passage of ACT 951, and providing for the distribution of funds in the municipal treasury of Talisayan resulting from the separation from Talisayan of the former municipality of Gingoog.
  • August 20, 1907

The territory of Misamis inhabited by non-Christian tribes – essentially the same as the northern half of modern Bukidnon – was annexed as part of the new sub-province named Bukidnon, of Agusan Province by virtue of Philippine Commission Act 1693.

  • February 28, 1914

By virtue of ACT 2390, the name of the municipality of Langaran, province of Misamis was changed.
  • November 2, 1929

Since communication and administration proved difficult between the two widely separated halves of Misamis Province, they were split into the two independent provinces of Misamis Occidental and Misamis Oriental by virtue of Philippine Commission Act 3537. At that time, Misamis Occidental was comprised of the municipalities of Baliangao, Lopez-Jaena, Tudela, Clarin and Misamis (the capital town, now Ozamiz City).

  • January 6, 1930

Oroquieta was designated as the provincial capital.
  • November 20, 1930

By virtue of ACT 3701, the name of the municipality of Regidor, Occidental Misamis, was changed to Tangub.
  • June 19, 1948

Ozamiz City was chartered.
  • June 18, 1961

By virtue of REPUBLIC ACT 3369, certain barrios in the municipality of Bonifacio, province of Misamis Occidental were created.
  • June 18, 1966

By virtue of R.A. 4822, the municipality of Panaon in the province of Misamis Occidental was created
  • June 17, 1967

Tangub City was chartered.
  • June 21, 1969

Oroquieta City was chartered.
  • August 4, 1969

By virtue of R.A. 6022, the municipality of Pines in the province of Misamis Occidental was created.
  • June 19, 1971

By virtue of R.A. 6227, the barrio of Singalat in  the  municipality of Calamba, province of Misamis Occidental was created.
  • February 8, 1982

By virtue of BATAS PAMBANSA BILANG 171, the municipality of Don Mariano Marcos in the province of Misamis Occidental was created.
  • January 20, 1990

By virtue of R.A. 6845, the name of the municipality of Don Mariano Marcos, province of Misamis Occidental, was changed to municipality of Don Victoriano.
  • 2010

Misamis Occidental has 2 districts. The 1st district is composed of the municipalities of Aloran, Baliangao, Calamba, Concepcion, Jimenez, Lopez Jaena, Panaon, Plaridel, Sapang Dalaga, and Oroquieta City. The 2nd district is composed of the following municipalities: Bonifacio, Clarin, Don Victoriano Chiongbian, Sinacaban, Tudela, Ozamiz City and Tangub City.
