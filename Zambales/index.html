HISTORY

ZAMBALES
The following text/information are lifted from the book of Lee W. Vance entitled Tracing your Philippine Ancestors which was published in Provo, Utah by Stevenson’s Genealogical Center in 1980 and Symbols of the State by the Department of Interior and Local Government of the Republic of the Philippines in 1998.

The province of Zambales, situated along the western coast of central Luzon, is bounded on the north by Pangasinan, on the east by Tarlac and Pampanga, and on the south by Bataan.  Its long and irregular coastline faces the South China Sea to the west.  It derives its name from the Zambals, the principal tribe inhabiting the area in the sixteenth century.

Following is a timeline1,2 of events that led to the creation and development of the province of Zambales:

DATE

E V E N T
  • Prior to 1572

Zambals, the principal tribe inhabiting the area settled in several communities along the coast. 

  • 1572

Juan de Salcedo, a Spanish explorer first discovered the Zambals in Subic or Hubik in 1572.  He is mainly responsible for establishing Spanish sovereignty in Zambales.
Soon after, Zambales became the third province to be organized. 

  • 1607

The town of Masinloc was created. It became the first capital of the province.
Augustinian Recollects established centers in Subic and Masinloc.
  • 1611

The town of Iba was created. Subsequently, Augustinian Recollects established a center in the town.
  • 1612

The town of Santa Cruz or Sigayan was founded in 1612.  This was followed by the creation of a center by the Augustinian Recollects.
  • 1736

The town of Babayan was established.
  • 1819

Babayan was renamed Botolan.
  • 1825

The town of San Marcelino was established.
  • 1836

San Antonio was organized as a Spanish pueblo.
  • 1843

San Marcelino was organized as a Spanish pueblo.
  • 1853

San Felipe was organized as a Spanish pueblo.
  • 1872

The town of Candelaria also known as San Vicente was organized as a Spanish pueblo.
  • 1879

The town of Palauig was established as a Spanish pueblo.
  • 1898

The insurrectionists became active in the province and established a revolutionary government.
  • Between 1898 and 1901

The revolutionary government was displaced by the U.S. military which governed the province until 1901.
  • August 28, 1901

A civil government for Zambales province was organized under the Americans pursuant to Philippine Commission Act 211, enacted August 28, 1901.

  • October 15, 1903

By virtue of Act 945, the twenty-five municipalities of the province of Zambales were reduced to fifteen.
  • November 1903

Philippine Commission Act 1004, enacted on November 21, 1903, annexed the northern portion of the province, including the towns of Alaminos, Bolinao, San Isidro, Potot (Burgos) and Infanta, to Pangasinan.

  • December 1903

The towns of Bani, Anda and Agno were similarly added to Pangasinan from Zambales pursuant to Philippine Commission Act 1029, enacted December 19, 1903.
  • 1908

The towns of San Antonio and San Felipe were created.
  • 1910

The following towns were created Candelaria, Castillejos, and Palauig.
  • 1959

On December 7, 1959, President Carlos P. Garcia signed Executive Order No. 366, creating the Municipality of Olongapo in Zambales.
  • June 1, 1966

Olongapo became a chartered city pursuant to Republic Act No. 4645.
  • Sept. 3, 1966

Olongapo City was inaugurated by Vice-President Fernando Lopez.
  • 2013

Zambales now has two legislative districts.  The first district consists of Castillejos, San Marcelino, Subic and Olongapo City.  The second district is made up of Botolan, Cabangan, Candelaria, Iba, Masinloc, Palauig, San Antonio, San Felipe, San Narciso and Santa Cruz.
