HISTORY

CAMIGUIN

The following text/information are lifted from the book of Lee W. Vance entitled Tracing your Philippine Ancestors which was published in Provo, Utah by Stevenson’s Genealogical Center in 1980 and Symbols of the State by the Department of Interior and Local Government of the Republic of the Philippines in 1998.

The province of Camiguin is located at the northern tip of Misamis Oriental. It lies nestled in the Mindanao Sea of the southern Philippines. It is separated from the northern coast of Mindanao on the south by Gingoog Bay and Macajalar Bay and from Bohol on the north by the Mindanao Sea
Camiguin has a land area of 237.95 square kilometers. In 2007, it had a population of 81, 293.  

Following is a timeline 1,2 of events that led to the creation and development of the province of Camiguin:

DATE

EVENT
  • March 11, 1565

Miguel Lopez de Legazpi’s men found the people of Camiguin trading with neighboring Asian countries.
  • 1596

Earliest Jesuit missionaries started in northern Mindanao area.
  • 1622

Augustinian Recollects first establish missions in Camiguin Island.
  • 1818

Camiguin was included in the newly created province of Misamis as the “Partido de Catarman” and the villages of Guinsiliban.

  • 1855

Mambajao was created from Catarman.
  • 1869

Saganay and Mahinog were founded.
  • 1903

The towns of Catarman, Mahinog and Sagay were all combined with Mambajao to form a single municipality out of the entire island.
Guinsiliban started to be part of Sagay of this year until 1950.
  • 1929

The old province of Misamis was split and Camiguin was assigned to the jurisdiction of Misamis Oriental.

  • June 22, 1957

By virtue of REPUBLIC ACT 2021, Camiguin Island became a sub-province of Misamis Oriental.
  • June 18, 1966

By virtue of R.A. 4669 was approved and Camiguin was organized as a separate province with Mambajao as the capital.
  • As of 2010

Camiguin constitutes a lone district that includes the municipalities of Catarman, Guinsiliban, Mahinog, Mambajao, and Sagay.
