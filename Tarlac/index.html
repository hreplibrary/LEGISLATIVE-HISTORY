HISTORY

TARLAC
The following text/information are lifted from the book of Lee W. Vance entitled Tracing your Philippine Ancestors which was published in Provo, Utah by Stevenson’s Genealogical Center in 1980 and Symbols of the State by the Department of Interior and Local Government of the Republic of the Philippines in 1998.

The province of Tarlac is situated in the center of the central plains of Luzon Island in the northern Philippines, about half-way between Manila Bay and Lingayen Gulf.  It is bounded by Pangasinan on the north, Nueva Ecija on the east, Pampanga on the south, and Zambales on the west.  Because of its geographic location between these provinces, it has received settlers of different cultures and tongues from several different parts of Luzon.  Consequently, Tarlac is called the “melting pot of Luzon”.
Following is a timeline1,2 of events that led to the creation and development of the province of Tarlac:

DATE

E V E N T
  • Prior to 1500s

The sparsely populated old territory of Tarlac was originally divided between Pampanga and Pangasinan, and was inhabited by primitive tribes of Negritos. 

  • 1574 – 1754

The first town to be founded by Christian settlers were Paniqui (1574), Tarlac (1686), Bamban (1710), and Capas (1710). 

Paniqui was formerly a part of Guimba, Nueva Ecija.

  • 1762

Inhabitants of Paniqui took up arms against the Spaniards in support of the Palaris revolt in Pangasinan.
  • 1847

Dominicans and Augustinians evangelized the town of Camiling.

  • 1855

The town of Victoria was organized as a Spanish pueblo.
  • 1860

A politico-military comandancia was created out of the towns of Concepcion, Bamban, Capas, Mabalacat, Magalang, Porac, Floridablanca, Victoria and Tarlac.
  • 1874

Tarlac was formally organized as a province.  Its municipalities consisted of Bamban, Capas, Victoria and Tarlac.  It also included Camiling, Gerona, and Paniqui from Pangasinan.  The towns of Mabalacat, Porac, Floridablanca and Magalang were ceded to Pampanga.
  • 1896

Tarlac was one of the first eight provinces that rose up in arms against Spain. 

  • 1898

Mayantoc was organized as a Spanish pueblo under he Revolutionary government and named San Francisco de Mayantoc.  It was dissolved by the American until 1917.
  • 1899

U.S. forces drove the revolutionary forces out of Tarlac.

  • 1901

Tarlac province’s civil government was restored pursuant to Philippine Commission Act 87, enacted February 18, 1901.
  • 1903

Santa Ignacia and San Clemente were annexed as barrios to Camiling.

Gerona was annexed as barrio to Pura.

O’Donnell and barrios  Calynicuan and Taloga of the municipality of Taloga were annexed to  Capas.
Barrio Kaut of La Paz, barrios Santa Rosa, San Juan and San Agustin of Murcia were annexed to Concepcion.
  • 1906

By virtue of Act 1555, the municipalities in the province of Tarlac increased from nine to ten, separating the former municipality of La Paz and the barrio of Lauangcupang from the present municipality of Tarlac.

Also through this Act, Barrio Bantug was annexed to La Paz.
  • January 10, 1907

By virtue of Act 1584, Barrio Kaut was annexed to La Paz.
  • March 27, 1907

By virtue of Act 1623, the municipalities in the province of Tarlac increased from ten to eleven, reconstituting the municipality of Gerona.

Also through this Act,The barrios of San Roman, Legaspi, San Agustin, and Salcedo, now a part of the municipality of Paniqui, were separated from said municipality and annexed to the present municipality of Moncada.
  • 1942

Camp O’Donnell in Capas, Tarlac became the terminal point of the famous Death March of Filipino and American soldiers after the fall of Bataan.
  • 1945

Tarlac was liberated from the Japanese.
  • January 5, 1990
 By virtue of RA 6842,SAN  JOSE was created as a municipality.
  • 2013

Tarlac is currently made up of the following municipalities Anao, Camiling, Mayantoc, Moncada, Paniqui, Pura, Ramos, San Clemente, San Manuel, Santa Ignacia, Gerona, San Jose, Victoria Bamban, Capas, Concepcion, La Paz and the city of Tarlac.
