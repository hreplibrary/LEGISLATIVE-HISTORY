HISTORY

CAGAYAN

The following text/information are lifted from the book of Lee W. Vance entitled Tracing your Philippine Ancestors which was published in Provo, Utah by Stevenson’s Genealogical Center in 1980 and Symbols of the State by the Department of Interior and Local Government of the Republic of the Philippines in 1998.

Cagayan is located at the northern tip of Luzon. It is bounded in the north by the Babuyan Channel, east by the Pacific Ocean, south by the Provinces of Kalinga and Isabela, and the west by the Province of Apayao.

Cagayan has a land area of 9,295.75 square kilometres. In 2007, it had a population of 1,072,571.  

Following is a timeline1,2 of events that led to the creation and development of the province of Cagayan:

DATE

EVENT
  • 1572

Juan de Salcedo explored the Cagayan Valley, via the Cagayan River, and visited Aparri, Abulug, and Pamplona. The Spaniards called the region Catagayan which was shortened to Cagayan.
  • 1581

Capt. Juan P. Carreon’s expedition drove away the Japanese corsair Tayfusa in the coastal town of Cagayan, and then founded the town of Nueva Segovia (now Lal-lo) and built the first capital of the region. 
At this time, the first mission in Cagayan was established.

  • 1583

Cagayan (or Nueva Segovia, as it was often called), was already recognized as a political division with the pueblo of Nueva Segovia as its capital.
Cagayan comprised the territory east of the Cordillera Central mountains and north of the Caraballo del Sur range, and included most of Cagayan, Isabela, Nueva Vizcaya, Kalinga Apayao, Quirino, Aurora Sub-province of Quezon, and Batanes.
  • 1595

Missions were established in Abulug, Camaluniugan and Pata.
  • 1596

Nueva Segovia became the seat of a diocese of that name comprising the whole of northern Luzon.
  • 1596

Mission was established in Buguey.
  • 1598

Mission was established in Lobo (Fraire).
  • 1604

Missions were established in Tuguegarao and Nassiping in 1604.
  • 1607

Mission in Iguig was established.
  • 1610

Missions in in Piat and Tumauini (Isabela) were established.

  • 1612

Missions in Tuao and Ilagan (Isabela) were established.
  • 1617

Mission in Malueg was established.
  • 1619

Mission in Fuga was established.
  • 1631

Mission in Tabang (Fraire) was established.
  • 1680

Mission in Aparri was established.
  • 1758

The seat of the diocese was moved to Vigan, Ilocos Sur, but it retained the name of Cagayan town where it was first established.
  • 1839

Nueva Vizcaya was the first to split off from Cagayan.
At the same time, provincial capital was moved from Nueva Segovia to Tuguegarao.
  • 1856

Isabela was formed from parts of Nueva Vizcaya and Cagayan.
  • 1862

Batanes was made a sub-province of Cagayan.
  • 1885

Batanes was separated as a province from Cagayan.

  • 1889

Kalinga, under the name of “Partido de Itaves” was organized.
  • 1890

Apayao, north of the “Partido”, was organized into a comandancia.
  • August, 1898

The Philippine Revolutionary Forces occupied the province but were displaced by U.S. forces the year after.
  • August 22, 1901

A civil government for Cagayan province was established under the Americans, the capital being at Tuguegarao, as before the Spanish.
At the same time, the comandancia of Apayao and the province of Batanes were annexed to Cagayan.

  • 1903

The 33 towns then comprising Cagayan were condensed to 22.
This was accomplished by combining Cordoba with Amulung; the barrios of Mala, Buguey Poblacion, and Paddaya, all of Buguey municipality, with Aparri; Magatao (San Carlos), Ibanag (San Jose), Isbayat and Saptan (San Vicente), all of the Batanes island group, with Basco to form the single municipality of Basco (Santo Domingo); Calayan, Camiguin, Dalupiri, and Claro  Babuyan, all of the Babuyan island group, to form the single municipality of Calayan; the barrios of Balza, Dalaya, Fula, Mala Massi, Mision, and Pattao, all of Buguey municipality, with Camalaniugan; Nassiping on the east side of the Cagayan River with Gattaran; Nassiping on the west side of the Cagayan River with Santo Nino; and Malaueg with Manauanan.
At the same time, Kalinga was separated from Cagayan and annexed as a sub-province to the newly-created province of Lepanto-Bontoc.
Later, Apayao was annexed to Lepanto-Bontoc which became part of the large former Mountain Province. 

  • 1909

Batanes was separated from Cagayan as a “special” province.
  • February 28, 1914

By virtue of ACT No. 2390, Municipalities of Santo Nino and Mawanan were change into Faire and Rizal respectively.
  • 1918

Batanes became a regular province.
  • December 8, 1933

By virtue of ACT No. 4119, the sitios of Ziuanan and Zimigui, from the municipal district of Luna, subprovince of Apayao, and the sitios of Lattut, Capacuan, and Macatal, from the municipal district of Ripang, subprovince of Apayao were separated.
The sitios of Ziuanan and Zimigui with the municipality of Pamplona, and the sitios of Lattut, Capacuan, and Macatal, with the municipality of Rizal were merged.
  • October 15, 1945

By virtue of COMMONWEALTH ACT 692, the Municipal District of of Allacapan, Province of Cagayan was converted into a municipality, transferring a part of its territorial jurisdiction to the Municipality of Ballesteros, Cagayan.
  • June 13, 1950

By virtue of REPUBLIC ACT (RA) 507 , Municipality of Lasam was created.
  • June 22, 1963

By virtue of RA 3728, the municipality of Sta. Teresa in the province of Cagayan was created.
  • June 20, 1964

By virtue of RA 4149, the name of the municipality of Langangan was changed to Santa Praxedes.

  • June 21, 1969

By virtue of RA 5644, the name of the municipality of Faire was changed to Santo Niño.

By virtue of RA 5968, the Municipality of Santa Praxedes was converted into a regular municipality.
  • As of 2013

Cagayan constitutes three legislative districts. Listed below are the municipalities that comprise the districts:
1st District: Municipalities of Alcala, Aparri, Baggao, Buguey, Camalaniugan, Gattaran, Gonzaga, Lal-lo, Santa Ana, and Santa Teresita.
2nd District: Municipalities of Abulug, Allacapan, Ballesteros, Calayan, Claveria, Lasam, Pamplona, Piat, Rizal, Sanchez-Mira, Santa Praxedes, and Santo Niño (Faire).
3rd District: Municipalities of Amulung, Enrile, Iguig, Peñablanca, Solana, Tuao, and Tuguegarao City.
