HISTORY

GUIMARAS

The following text/information are lifted from the book of Lee W. Vance entitled Tracing your Philippine Ancestors which was published in Provo, Utah by Stevenson’s Genealogical Center in 1980 and Symbols of the State by the Department of Interior and Local Government of the Republic of the Philippines in 1998.

The province of Guimaras lies between the islands of Panay and Negros in the Western Visayas region of the central Philippines.  It is separated from Iloilo on the northwest by the Iloilo Strait and from Negros Occidental by the Guimaras Strait.

Guimaras has a land area of 604.6 square kilometers. In 2007, it had a population of 151,238.  

Following is a timeline 1,2 of events that led to the creation and development of the province of Guimaras:

DATE

EVENT
  • 1565

An expedition party dispatched by Legazpi from Bohol cruised along the eastern shores of Guimaras.
  • 1569

Panay Island was divided into two civil provinces, Panay on the north and Oton or Ogtong (later known as Arevalo) and finally Iloilo province on the south to which Guimaras was an integral part. Guimaras remained part of Iloilo until the end of the Spanish rule.
  • 1591

“Ymaral” was part of an encomienda administered from Oton.

Later, Guimaras Island became part of the original province of Oton (later called Arevalo, and finally Iloilo).
  • 1903

All the former Spanish pueblos or towns in Guimaras were combined into one municipality, Buenavista.
  • February 14, 1945

A contingent of Panay Forces under the command of Captain Luis Herrera attacked Buenavista.
  • June 18, 1966

By virtue of Republic Act (RA) 4667, Guimaras was created as a sub-province of Iloilo.
  • April 14, 1969

RA 4667 was amended by RA 5463. The municipality of Buenavista became the seat of the political administration.
  • As of 2010

Guimaras constitutes a lone district that includes the municipalities of Buenavista, Jordan, Nueva Valencia, San Lorenzo, and Sibunag.
