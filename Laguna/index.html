HISTORY

LAGUNA
The following text/information are lifted from the book of Lee W. Vance entitled Tracing your Philippine Ancestors which was published in Provo, Utah by Stevenson’s Genealogical Center in 1980 and Symbols of the State by the Department of Interior and Local Government of the Republic of the Philippines in 1998.

The crescent-shaped province of Laguna is located in south-central Luzon, in the north-central portion of the Philippine archipelago.  It nearly encircles Laguna Bay, the largest inland lake in the country.  It is bounded on the north b the bay and Rizal province, on the east by Quezon province, on the south by Batangas, and on the west by Cavite.

Before the arrival of the Spanish, Laguna was already well-settled by the Malays and Chinese.  It was probably one of the largest ancient Chinese settlements in the Philippines, the natives still bearing some resemblance to these forebears even today.

Laguna was originally bounded on the north by Manila and Nueva Ecija, on the east by the Pacific Ocean, on the south by Tayabas and Batangas, and on the west by Cavite.  But with the creation of the province of Morong (later renamed Rizal) in 1853, Laguna lost most of her territory north of Laguna Bay; namely, the towns of Jalajala, Pililla, Tanay, Baras, Morong, Binangonan, Angono and Cardona.

Laguna has 4 districts. The 1st district is composed of the municipalities of Biñan, San Pedro, and Santa Rosa City. The 2nd district is composed of the municipalities of Bay, Cabuyao City, Los Baños, and Calamba City. The 3rd district is composed of the municipalities of Alaminos, Calauan, Liliw, Nagcarlan, Rizal, Victoria, and San Pablo City. The 4th district is composed of the municipalities of Cavinti, Famy, Kalayaan, Luisiana, Lumban, Mabitac, Magdalena, Majayjay, Paete, Pagsanjan, Pakil, Pangil, Pila, Siniloan, Santa Cruz, and Santa Maria.
