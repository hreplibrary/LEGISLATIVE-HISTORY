HISTORY

MARINDUQUE
The following text/information are lifted from the book of Lee W. Vance entitled Tracing your Philippine Ancestors which was published in Provo, Utah by Stevenson’s Genealogical Center in 1980 and Symbols of the State by the Department of Interior and Local Government of the Republic of the Philippines in 1998.

The nearly circular island-province of Marinduque is located just south of Luzon Island in the central Philippines, between the Bondoc peninsula of southeastern Luzon and Mindoro Island to the southwest.  The province is surrounded by water;  Tayabas Bay to the north, with Quezon province beyond it; Mompog Pass to the east, separating it from the Bondoc peninsula of Quezon province; the Sibuyan Sea to the south, with Romblon beyond and Tablas Strait to the west, with Mindoro beyond it.

Marinduque has six municipalities, with its capital at Boac.

Following is a timeline1,2 of events that led to the creation and development of the province of Marinduque:

DATE

EVENT
  • 1570

Juan de Salcedo and Martin de Goiti were the first Spaniards to explore Marinduque
  • 1581

Marinduque was assigned by the Spanish government to be part of the new province of “Balayan” (also called “Bombon”, now known as Batangas), and placed under the jurisdiction of the new corrigimiento of Mindoro.
  • 1898

By the end of the Spanish regime, Marinduque was still part of  Mindoro province.
  • 1792

Jesuit missionaries were the first to bring Christianity to Marinduque’s natives.  They established early missions and parishes in Boac
  • 1798

early missions and parishes in Gasan
  • 1807

early missions and parishes in Mogpog
  • 1899

early missions and parishes in Torrijos

  • 1898

With the overthrow of the Spanish government in the Philippines by the revolutionaries, Marinduque was turned over to a revolutionary government, followed in turn by an American military government, and a civil provincial government.
  • May 1, 1901

The civil province of Marinduque was first created, with its capital at Boac by virtue of Philippine Commission Act 125
  • June 23, 1902

By virtue of ACT 423, the former province of Mindoro, together with the Lubang island group (newly separated from Cavite Province), were annexed to the province of Marinduque
  • November 10, 1902
  • Marinduque province was dissolved, and annexed to Tayabas (or Quezon) province by virtue of Philippine Commission Act 499
  • By virtue of Philippine Commission Act 500, Mindoro province was again created, with the Lubang Islands as part of its territory
  • August 10, 1907

Marinduque was made a sub-province of Tayabas (Quezon) province
  • February 28, 1914
 Act 2354 made the office of Lietenant-Governor elective in the subprovince of Marinduque, Tayabas.
  • February 21, 1920

By virtue of ACT NO. 2880, entitled “an act authorizing the separation of the subprovince of Marinduque from the province of Tayabas and the re-establishment of the former province of Marinduque, and for other purposes,” Marinduque was re-created as an independent province.
  • May 28, 1948

By virtue of Republic Act 20, the sitios of Angas, Biga, Kamandungan, Kilokilo and Makulapnit of the municipality of Santa Cruz, province of Marinduque, were converted into independent barrios
  • June 20, 1953

The barrio Baguidbirin in the municipality of Sta. Cruz, province of Marinduque, was created by virtue of Republic Act 960.
  • June 15, 1954

By virtue of Republic Act 1100, the sitio of Hinangayon, Mogpog, Marinduque, was converted into a barrio to be known as the Barrio of Hinangayon.
  • June 20, 1957

By virtue of Republic Act 1676, the Sitio of Kalangkang, barrio of Kasily, municipality of Sta. Cruz, province of Marinduque, was converted into a barrio to be known as the barrio of Kalangkang of said municipality.
  • June 21, 1957

By virtue of Republic Act 1738, the name of barrio Hinubuan, municipality of Gasan, province of Marinduque, was changed to Antipolo.
  • June 21, 1957

By virtue of Republic Act 1739, the name of barrio Banto-anin, Muncipality of Gasan, province of Marinduque, was changed to Barrio Bukal.
  • April 5, 1959

By virtue of REPUBLIC ACT 2120, the barrio of San Isidro in the municipality of Sta. Cruz was created.
  • April 24, 1959

REPUBLIC ACT 2148, the barrio of Bayakbakin in the municipality of Torrijos, was created

  • June 21, 1959

By virtue of REPUBLIC ACT 2581, the barrio of Tigwi constitute the sitios of Kay-poo, Nabiling, Kahawanan, Apartahan and Kalimpay, municipality of Torrijos, into a separate barrio to be known as the Barrio of Kawayanan
  • June 19, 1960

By virtue of REPUBLIC ACT 2917, the sitio of Malusak in the municipality of Mogpog, province of Marinduque, was converted into a barrio
  • June 18, 1961

By virtue of REPUBLIC ACT 3391, certain barrios in the province of Marinduque were created
  • 2013

Marinduque has a lone district which is composed of the following municipalities: Boac, Buenavista, Gasan, Mogpog, Santa Cruz, and Torrijos.
