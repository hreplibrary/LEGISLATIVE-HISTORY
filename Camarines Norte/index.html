HISTORY

CAMARINES NORTE

The following text/information are lifted from the book of Lee W. Vance entitled Tracing your Philippine Ancestors which was published in Provo, Utah by Stevenson’s Genealogical Center in 1980 and Symbols of the State by the Department of Interior and Local Government of the Republic of the Philippines in 1998.

Camarines Norte is located at the northwestern portion of the Bicol Peninsula. The province faces the Basiad Bay on the West, the Pacific Ocean on the north, and the San Miguel Bay on the east. It is bounded by the Province of Quezon on the southwest and Camarines Sur on the south.
Camarines Norte has a land area of 2,320.07 square kilometers. In 2007, it had a population of 513,785.  

Following is a timeline 1,2 of events that led to the creation and development of the province of Camarines Norte:

DATE

EVENT
  • 1571

Governor General Miguel Lopez de Legazpi sent Captain Juan de Salcedo explored the area of Mambulao and Paracale.
  • 1573

Camarines existed a single province, which originally included entire Bicol Peninsula (with modern Camarines Norte, Camarines Sur, Albay and Sorsogon) and Catanduanes, Masbate, Ticao and Burias islands and all their dependent islands. The capital was at Caceres.
  • 1829

The province was divided into two, Camarines Norte and Camarines Sur. Camarines Norte was assigned towns of Daet, Talisay, Indan, Labo, Paracale, Mambulao, Capalonga, Ragay, Lupi and Sipocot.
  • 1846

Camarines Norte lost the towns of Sipocot, Lupi and Ragay to Camarines Sur, in exchange for Simura.
  • 1854

The two provinces of Camarines were reunited to form the Province of Ambos Camarines.
  • 1857

The two provinces of Camarines were again separated.
  • 1893

The two provinces of Camarines were again reunited.
  • September 12, 1898

General Vicente Lukban took Daet from the Spaniards and organized and established a revolutionary government in the province.
  • January, 1900

American forces came and occupied the Bicol region.
  • April 27, 1901

A civil government under the Philippine Commission was established in Ambos Camarines.
  • March 3, 1919

The province was again divided into two, with Camarines Norte then consisting of towns of Capalonga, Mambulao, Paracale, Indan, Labo, San Vicente, Talisay, Daet, and Basud.

  • December 1, 1934

By virtue of ACT 4155, the name of the municipality of Mambulao was changed to that of the patriot Dr. Jose Maria Panganiban.
  • October 15, 1945
 By virtue of CA  690,the name of the municipality of Indan was changed to "Vinzons"
  • June 16, 1955

By virtue of RA 1314, Barrio Iraya Norte in the municipality of San Vicente was changed to San Jose of the same municipality.

Also on the same date,several sitios were converted to Barrio:Sitio Patag to Barrio San Isidro in the municipality of San Vicente, through RA 1315; Sitio Ilayang Basiad to Barrio San Pedro in the Municipality of Capalonga, through RA 1316; Sitios Lindong, Taba-taba, Manmontoy, Borabod, Taisan, Hinampacan, Manlimonsito, Polantuna, and Olivia, into Barrios with the same name in the municipality of Basud, through RA 1317.
  • June 21, 1969
 By virtue of RA 5480, Santa Elena was created as a municipality
  • November 9, 1970
 By virtue of RA 6144, Imelda was created as a municipality
  • February 10, 1989
 By virtue of RA 6708,the name of the municipality of Imelda was changed to "San Lorenzo Ruiz"
  • September 6, 1991
 By virtue of RA 7155, Tulay-na-Lupa was created as a municipality
  • October 22, 2009

By virtue of RA 9725, the province was divided into two districts.
  • As of 2013

Camarines Norte constitutes two legislative districts. Listed below are the municipalities that comprise the districts:
1st District:Capalonga, Jose Panganiban, Labo, Paracale and Sta. Elena

2nd District:Basud, Daet, Mercedes, San Lorenzo Ruiz, San Vicente, Talisay and Vinzons
