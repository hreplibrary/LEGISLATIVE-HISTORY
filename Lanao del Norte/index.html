HISTORY

LANAO DEL NORTE


The following text/information are lifted from the book of Lee W. Vance entitled Tracing your Philippine Ancestors which was published in Provo, Utah by Stevenson’s Genealogical Center in 1980 and Symbols of the State by the Department of Interior and Local Government of the Republic of the Philippines in 1998.

The province of Lanao del Norte is located in the southern Philippines, in that part of northwestern Mindanao where the Zamboanga peninsula joins the Mindanao mainland.  It is bounded by Misamis Oriental on the northeast, Bukidnon on the east, and Lanao del Sur on the south.  Where Zamboanga del Sur meets Lanao del Norte on the west, a narrow isthmus is formed between the waters of Iligan Bay to the north and Illana Bay to the south.

Since many centuries before the Spanish conquest of the Philippines, Lanao (including both Lanao del Norte and Lanao del Sur) had been the home of the Maranaos, a tribe of Malaysian-derived Muslims, who have remained defiantly independent for most of their history in spite of many efforts to subject them.

Following is a time line 1,2 of events that led to the creation and development of the province of Lanao del Norte:

DATE

EVENT
  • 1634

Jesuit priests first established Christian missions in Iligan (evidently a Spanish outpost by that time).
  • 1637

The first concerted effort of the Spanish government to control the Lanao region occurred, when Governor-General Hurtado de Corcuera led an expedition against Sultan Correlat (or Kudarat), Sultan of Mindanao and Sulu, at his Muslim stronghold at Lancitan on the Lanao coast. 

  • 1768

Jesuits were expelled from the Philippines and the Recollects took over these missions.
  • 1890

under the administration of Governor-General Valeriano Weyler, a series of campaigns was begun to bring the Lanao region under the Manila government’s control.
  • 1891

Spanish forces occupied Malabang and the other towns along the southern Lanao coast. 

  • 1894

Under Blanco’s orders, an army of 3,000 Spaniards captured the Muslim capital and stronghold at Marawi (among other Moro cottas), which was then reputed to be the strongest Muslim fort of its kind in Mindanao. 

  • October 8, 1895

Lanao was organized into the Seventh politico-military District of the province of Mindanao and Sulu. The true effectiveness of the District’s organization is questionable, as it was so short-lived.
  • 1896

the Philippine Revolution broke out.
  • 1899

the Spanish governadorcillo abandoned Iligan.  The District was left to the revolutionaries and the Moros.  Capitan Hilario Ramires (presumably an insurrecto), took over the reins of government for one year.
  • 1900

American forces landed at Iligan. They established their own military government in Iligan, which had influence mainly over the Christian town of Lanao (Iligan and Malabang), and practically none whatsoever over the Moros. 

  • 1903

Lanao (with its capital at Iligan) was made one of five district of the newly-created Moro Province, administered from Zamboanga City.
  • 1913

Control over the Muslims of Lanao was finally established by the Americans.
  • September 1, 1914

Lanao was organized as one of seven new provinces under the newly-created Department of Mindanao and Sulu (the successor to the then-dissolved Moro Province). Lanao Province then included all of the territory of both Lanao del Norte and Lanao del Sur, and had its capital at Iligan.
  • August 19, 1940
 COMMONWEALTH ACT NO. 592 created the City of Dinsalan.
  • October 17, 1946

REPUBLIC ACT NO. 58: an act creating the municipality of Tubod, province of Lanao.
  • June 16, 1948

R.A. 283 amended section one of R.A. 58.
  • June 16, 1950

Iligan City was chartered and became the provincial capital.
  • May 10, 1956

By virtue of R.A. 1415, the municipality of Bacolod in the province of Lanao was created.
  • June 16, 1956
 By virtue of R.A.. 1552, the act to amend the charter of the City of dansalan so as to change its name to Marawi and make elective its mayor, vice-mayor and councilors, and for other purposes was approved.
  • 1959

The Philippine Congress enacted Republic Act 2228 in 1959, which partitioned Lanao into two provinces, Lanao de Norte and Lanao del Sur. At that time Lanao del Norte included the municipalities of: Balo-I, Kauswagan, Bacolod, Maigo, Kolambugan, Ubod, Baroy, Lala, Kapatagan, and Caromatan; and the municipal district of Matungao, Pantao-Ragat, Munai, Tangcal and Nunungan. These have increased in number to the present total of 19 municipalities, 2 municipal districts, and one city.  

  • June 19, 1960

R.A. 2883 amended subparagraphs 22 and 23 for the provinces of Lanao and Leyte, respectively, pages 142 and 143, paragraph a, title d, section one of R.A. 2093.
  • June 18, 1961

By virtue of R.A. 3366, the barrio of Laparan in the municipality of Kauswagan.
  • June 21, 1969

By virtue of R.A. 5745, the municipality of Sapad was created.
  • June 21, 1969

The municipality of Tagoloan was created by virtue of R.A. 5822.
  • March 5, 1982

BATAS PAMBANSA BILANG 181 is an act transferring the seat of the provincial government of Lanao del Norte from Iligan City to the municipality of Tubod.
  • April 7, 1983

By virtue of BATAS PAMBANSA BLG. 365, the name of the municipality OF LINAMON was changed to TOMAS CABILI.
  • 2010

Lanao del Norte has 2 districts. The 1st district is composed of the municipalities of Bacolod, baroy, Kauswagan, Kolambugan, Linamon, Maigo, Tubod, and Iligan City. The 2nd district is composed of the municipalities Baloi, Kapatagan, Lala, Magsaysay, Matungao, Munai, Nunungan, Pantao-Ragat, Pantar, Poona Piagapo, Salvador, Sapad, Sultan Naga Dimaporo, Tagoloan, Tangcal and Tubod.
