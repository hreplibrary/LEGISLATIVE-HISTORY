HISTORY

NUEVA VIZCAYA
The following text/information are lifted from the book of Lee W. Vance entitled Tracing your Philippine Ancestors which was published in Provo, Utah by Stevenson’s Genealogical Center in 1980 and Symbols of the State by the Department of Interior and Local Government of the Republic of the Philippines in 1998.

The land-locked province of Nueva Vizcaya is located in north-central Luzon in the northern Philippines.  To the east lies the new province of Quirino.  To the south lie portions of Nueva Ecija and of Aurora, a sub-province of Quezon, separated from Nueva Vizcaya by the Caraballo and Sierra Madre Mountains.  To the west the Cordillera Central Mountains separate it from Pangasinan, Benguet, and from Ifugao to the north.

Before the Spanish conquest, Nueva Vizcaya’s original inhabitants were nomadic tribes of Igorots, Ifugaos, Ilongots, Aetas, Gaddanes and Malaats.  Early explorations into this region came from Cagayan to the north, of which it originally formed a part, and with which it shares its earliest history.

Following is a timeline1,2 of events that led to the creation and development of the province of Nueva Vizcaya:

DATE

EVENT
  • 1596

Nueva Vizcaya’s early settlement is largely due to the efforts of Christian missionaries, who came to the area
  • 1609

The Dominicans established the first mission at Ituy (or Ytuy), but did not stay very long
  • 1632

Evangelization was again commenced, this time at Burubur (now a part of Aritao), followed by the discovery of the already-settled towns of San Miguel, Dongla, Baxabax, Tujay, Negros, Alaguetes, Dagay, Buhay, San Fernando, Pahi-pahi, and Abiang.  Frequent interruptions in the work of evangelization and civilization continued, most missions being staffed only for brief periods until the next native rebellion to tribute collection.
  • 1716

the Augustinians took over missionary labors in the region. 

  • 1740

The Dominicans returned to help the Augustinians
  • 1762

When the British occupied Manila in 1762, the Dominican Order moved their vast riches from the city to their mission school in Dupax.
  • 1832

Galvey’s expedition
  • 1839

Nueva Vizcaya province was created. 3 great mission-regions had been organized: 1) Diffun, including Calanusian (now Reina Mercedes) Cauayan, Angadanan, Caramag and Carig, all now of Isabela province; 2) Paniki (or Panique), including Bayombong, Lumabang (now Solano) and Bagabag; and 3) Ituy (or Ytuy), including Bambang, Buhay (now Aritao), and Dupax.
  • May 21, 1839

Nueva Vizcaya was created as a politico-military province by the decree of Governor-General Luis Lardizabal
  • April 10, 1841

The decree of Governor-General Luis Lardizabal was confirmed by a royal Spanish decree. As originally created, Nueva Vizcaya included the three missions of Diffun, Pankiki and Ytuy, as well as the towns of Gamu, Furao, and Ilagan, and the sub-province of Ifugao.  In terms of area, this included not only modern Nueva Vizcaya, but also Quirino, Ifugao and the southwestern part of Isabela.  Its capital was originally at Camarag, now a barrio of Echague, Isabela.  Its name was derived from Lardizabal’s native province of Vizcaya in Spain.
  • 1847

Oscariz’s expedition
  • 1856

Nueva Vizcaya ceded a large portion of its territory to the newly created province of Isabela, including the old provincial capital of Camarag, and the towns of Carig (Santiago), Echague, Angadanan, Cauayan, Gamu, Reina Mercedes, Palanan and the new province’s capital of Ilagan.  Nueva Vizcaya retained Aritao, Bagabag, Bambang, Bayombong, Dupax and Solano, and the District of Ifugao, with its new provincial capital at Bayombong.
  • September 1898

Bayombong, to which the last Spanish governor had fled, fell to the insurgents.
  • November 1899

A short-lived revolutionary provincial government was organized, only to fall a year later to American Forces
  • January 28, 1902

The U.S. military ruled the province from then until a civil provincial government was established by the Americans by virtue of Philippine Commission Act 337
  • April 9, 1902

The Philippine Commission passed Act 387, providing for a simplified form of local government adapted to the special requirements of the many uncivilized tribes in the province.  This act became a prototype of the local government of “special” provinces of the Philippines for several decades.  As such, it fell under the administrative jurisdiction of the Secretary of the Interior, Bureau of Non-Christian tribes.  The former Spanish pueblos recognized as municipalities at this time were Aritao, Bagabag, Bambang, Bayombong, Dupax and Solano. Act 387 was amended by Act 388 striking out and inserting certain provisions.
  • August 1903

Aritao was annexed as a barrio to Dupax by virtue of Philippine Commission Act 850
  • August 24, 1903

ACT 850 the municipality of Aritao, province of Nueva Vizcaya, was incorporated as a barrio of the municipality of Dupax, province of Nueva Vizcaya
  • September 14, 1905

ACT NO. 1397 provided for the establishment of local civil governments in the townships in the province of Nueva Vizcaya
  • August 18, 1908

The territory comprising Ifugao province today (and corresponding to the former comandancia of Quiangan, organized as such in 1889 as a dependency of Nueva Vizcaya) was annexed as a sub-province to the newly created Mountain Province.  To compensate for this loss of territory, Nueva Vizcaya was given the territory of the former comandancia of Binatangan, or the “Ilongot District”, which had previously been part of Isabela.
  • 1914

A survey of the boundary resulted in the annexation of a rectangular area, now the site of Jones municipality, to Isabela.
  • February 3, 1916

ACT NO. 2562 amended Section 23 of ACT NO. 1396, entitled "The Special Provincial Government Act," by reorganizing the provincial board of Nueva Vizcaya, providing for the election of a 3rd member, and for other purposes
  • February 24, 1919

ACT NO. 2798 extended the laws and other legal provisions pertaining to the provinces and minor political subdivisions of the department of Mindanao and Sulu to the province of Nueva Vizcaya.
  • March 23, 1920

ACT NO. 2913 promoted the economic and political development of the Mountain Province and the province of Nueva Vizcaya, and amended accordingly ACT NO. 2798, entitled "An Act extending to the Mountain Province and the province of Nueva Vizcaya the laws and other legal provisions pertaining to the provinces and minor political subdivisions of the department of Mindanao and Sulu."
  • 1950

A boundary dispute between Isabela and Nueva Vizcaya was settled
  • 1950

6 municipalities and 5 municipal districts of Nueva Vizcaya were reorganized into 10 municipalities, defining their respective boundaries, abolishing the municipal districts.  The ten towns then existing were: Aglipay, Aritao, Bagabag, Bambang, Bayombong, Diffun, Dupax, Kayapa, Maddela and Solano.  The municipal districts of Imugan, Kasibu, Pinappagan and Kayapa were abolished by virtue of  EO 368 (series of 1950), dated Nov 11, 1950
  • June 22, 1957

The municipality of Ibung, province of Nueva Vizcaya was created by virtue of REPUBLIC ACT 1972
  • June 21, 1959

By virtue of REPUBLIC ACT 2519, the municipality of Saguday in the province of Nueva Vizcaya was created
  • June 18, 1961

By virtue of REPUBLIC ACT 3393, a certain barrio in the municipality of Diffun, province of Nueva Vizcaya was created.

By virtue of REPUBLIC ACT 3427, the Municipal District of Quezon in the province of Nueva Vizcaya was created.
  • June 19, 1965

By virtue of REPUBLIC ACT 4573, certain barrios in the municipalities of Kayapa and Maddela, province of Nueva Vizcaya were created
  • June 18, 1966

The sub-province of Quirino was created from the four towns of Diffun, Saguday, Aglipay and Maddela by virtue of Republic Act No. 4734
  • June 18, 1966

By virtue of REPUBLIC ACT 4735, the municipal district of Ambaguio in the province of Nueva Vizcaya was created.
  • June 17, 1967

By virtue of REPUBLIC ACT 4973, the municipal district of Diadi in the province of Nueva Vizcaya was created.
  • June 21, 1969

Cabarroguis municipality was formed from parts of Aglipay and Diffun, and designated the capital of the sub-province by virtue of Republic Act No. 5554
  • June 21, 1969

Republic Act 5554 amended REPUBLIC ACT NO. 4734, entitled, "an Act creating  the subprovince of Quirino in the province  of  Nueva  Vizcaya," and for other similar purposes.
  • August 4, 1969

REPUBLIC ACT 6068 converted the 4 districts outside  the  Poblacion Proper of the municipality  of  Solano,  province  of  Nueva Vizcaya, into  barrios,  defining  for  the  purposes  their respective boundaries and those of the Poblacion Proper.
  • August 16, 1971

The municipality of Dupax del Sur  from  the municipality of Dupax in the province of Nueva Vizcaya was created by virtue of REPUBLIC ACT 6372.
  • September 10, 1971

Finally, Republic Act No. 6394 separated Quirino sub-province from Nueva Vizcaya, and made it an independent province
  • September 10, 1971

The municipality of Alabel in  the  province of South Cotabato was created by virtue of REPUBLIC ACT 6393
  • April 20, 1979

The municipality of Alfonso Castañeda in the province of Nueva Vizcaya was created by virtue of BATAS PAMBANSA BILANG 27
  • As of 2013

The lone district of Nueva Vizcaya has 15 municipalities namely: Alfonso Castaneda, Ambaguio, Aritao, Bagabag, Bambang, Bayombong, Quezon, Santa Fe, Solano, and Villaverde.
