HISTORY

MASBATE


The following text/information are lifted from the book of Lee W. Vance entitled Tracing your Philippine Ancestors which was published in Provo, Utah by Stevenson’s Genealogical Center in 1980 and Symbols of the State by the Department of Interior and Local Government of the Republic of the Philippines in 1998.

The province of Masbate is located just south of Luzon’s Bicol peninsula, north of the Visayan Islands of the central Philippines.  It is comprised of the wedge-shaped main island of Masbate, the two major islands of Burias and Ticao, and fourteen other small islands.  It is separated from Luzon’s Bicol region on the northeast by the Burias and Ticao Passes; the Samar Sea separates it from Samar Island on the east and Leyte Island on the southeast; the Visayan Sea on the south separates I from Cebu and Negros islands; the Jintotolo Channel separates it from Panay Island on the southwest; and the Sibuyan Sea separates it from Romblon and Sibuyan Islands to the west.

Following is a time line 1,2of events that led to the creation and development of the province of Masbate:

DATE

EVENT

  • 1596

The first Spanish expedition to explore Masbate, Burias and Ticao was led by Luis Enriquez de Guzman
  • 1600’s

Masbate remained primitive and undeveloped for nearly three centuries of Spanish rule, in spite of an attempt to build its economy by establishing shipbuilding as a local industry there
  • 1750

Captain Andres de Ibarra subsequently continued the exploration of these islands.  Recollect missionaries also were early pioneers of the area, establishing eight mission stations
  • 1837

A gold strike changed all that, however, as hoards of fortune-seeking settlers arrived almost daily at the ore fields of Aroroy.
  • 1846

Masbate and Ticao were separated from Albay province and made a single politico-military comandancia, or district, with its capital at San Jacinto on Ticao island.
  • 1898

the area came under the revolutionary government’s control, being administered from Albay.
  • 1900

its government was turned over to the U.S. military forces.
  • March 18, 1901

By virtue of Philippine Legislative Act 2934, The former district of Burias and “Masbate y Ticao” were combined to form the new province of Masbate when civil government was restored to the area
  • January 1, 1906

Masbate was annexed as a sub-province to Sorsogon by virtue of Philippine Commission Act 1413
  • February 15, 1921

Masbate was re-inaugurated as a separate province, independent of Sorsogon by virtue of Philippine Legislative Act 2934, approved on Dec. 15, 1920, as implemented by Executive Order No. 3, dated Jan 19, 1921, and effective February 15, 1921
  • June 16, 1948

By virtue of Republic Act 292, the municipality of Placer in the province of Masbate, was created.
  • June 11, 1951

By virtue of Republic Act 639, the municipality of Palanas in the province of Masbate, was created.
  • June 11, 1951

By virtue of Republic Act 641, the municipality of Limbuhan in the province of Masbate, was created
  • June 11, 1951

By virtue of Republic Act 642, the municipality of Batuan in the province of Masbate, was created
  • June 15, 1954

By virtue of Republic Act 1116, the name of municipality of Limbuhan, province of Masbate was changed to Pio V. Corpus
  • June 10, 1955

By virtue of Republic Act 1258, the name of the barrio of Libertad, formerly known as Barrio Umabay Exterior, municipality of Mobo, province of Masbate, was changed to San Gregorio
  • June 10, 1955

By virtue of Republic Act 1259, the name of Barrio Bugsayon, municipality of Masbate, province of Masbate, was changed to Barrio B. Titong
  • June 20, 1957

By virtue of Republic Act 1672, the sitio of Bugang in the municipality of Pio V. Corpus, province of Masbate, was converted into a barrio to be known as Barrio Bugang.
  • June 21, 1957

By virtue of Republic Act 1737, the name of the barrio of Sawmill, municipality of Aroroy, was changed to Concepcion.
  • May 7, 1959

By virtue of REPUBLIC ACT 2187, the municipality of Claveria in the province of Masbate was created

  • June 22, 1963

By virtue of REPUBLIC ACT 3782, Surigao del Norte, Surigao del Sur and Masbate were transferred to Labor Regional Office No. 9, amending for this purpose REPUBLIC ACT 2646

  • June 17, 1967

By virtue of REPUBLIC ACT 4923, the name of a certain barrio in the municipality of Aroroy, province of Masbate, was changed to barrio San Isidro
  • 2010

Masbate has 3 districts. The 1st district is composed of the following municipalities: Batuan, Claveria, Monreal, San Fernando, San Jacinto, and San Pascual. The 2nd district is composed of the following municipalities: Aroroy, Baleno, Balud, Mandaon, Milagros, Mobo, and Masbate City. The 3rd district is composed of the following municipalities: Cataingan, Cawayan, Dimasalang, Esperanza, Palanas, Pio V. Corpuz, Placer, and Uson.
