HISTORY

BACOLOD CITY

The following text/information are lifted from the book of Lee W. Vance entitled Tracing your Philippine Ancestors which was published in Provo, Utah by Stevenson’s Genealogical Center in 1980 and Symbols of the State by the Department of Interior and Local Government of the Republic of the Philippines in 1998.

Bacolod City is situated on the northwestern part of the island of Negros, is bounded by the Guimaras Strait on the west, the Municipality of Talisay on the north, the Municipality of Murcia on the east, and Bago City on the south.

Bacolod City has a land area of 161.45 square kilometers. In 2007, it had a population of 499,497.

Following is a timeline 1,2 of events that led to the creation and development of the province of Bacolod City:

DATE

EVENT
  • 1521

The earliest westerners set foot on Negros and some of Magellan’s expedition were sent there to explore the island.
  • 1575

Early missions were established in Binalbagan.
  • 1584

Early missions were established in Ilog.
  • 1628

The Jesuits took over the Augustinians.
  • 1700

The village of Magsungay was placed by the early missionaries under the care and protection of Saint Sebastian.
  • 1734

Earlier, Negros Occidental originally fell under the provincial jurisdiction of Iloilo (or Arevalo).  But in 1734, it was combined with the eastern half of Negros to form a single politico-military comandancia, or district.  The capital of Negros was originally at Ilog, but was moved almost immediately to Himamaylan.
  • July 14, 1755

The inhabitants of coastal Magsungay were attacked by forces under Datu Bantilan of Sulu.
The townspeople transferred to a hilly area called Bacolod. Later on the town was established.
  • 1768

Expulsion of the Jesuits from the Philippines, the missions and parishes were turned over to the secular clergy.
  • 1787

The city was transferred towards the shoreline due to Muslim raids.
  • 1788

The new town of Bacolod was constituted a parish under the secular clergy.
  • 1849

By order of Governor General Claveria, through Negros Island Governor Manuel Valdeviseo Morquecho, Bacolod was made the capital of the Province of Negros.
  • 1856

Negros Island’s status was elevated to that of a province, when a heavy influx of settlers (mainly from Panay) arrived to work in the newly flourishing sugar industry in Negros.
  • 1890

Negros Province was split to form the two politico-military provinces of Negros Occidental and Negros Oriental.
  • November 7, 1898

Bacolod City was subjugated by the Spanish invaders but the natives of the city continued fighting until its people established its Provincial Revolutionary Junta and elected Aniceto Lacson as the President, and Juan Araneta as the war-delegate.
  • March, 1899

The American forces led by Colonel James G. Smith occupied Bacolod, the revolutionary capital of the Provincial Republic of Negros.
  • April 20, 1901

Civil government was restored. Negros was re-divided to form two separate provinces again, as under the last decade of the Spanish rule.  Negros Occidental capital remained at Bacolod, as before by virtue of Philippine Commission Act 119.
  • June 18, 1938

By virtue of Commonwealth Act No. 326, enacted by the National Assembly, the City of Bacolod was created with Alfredo Montelibano as the first City Mayor.

  • May 21, 1942

Bacolod City was occupied by the Japanese forces.
  • May 29, 1945

The city was liberated by the American forces.
  • June 18, 1964

By virtue of Republic Act 4088, certain sections of Commonwealth Act No. 326 were amended.
  • As of 2010

The City of Bacolod constitutes a lone district.
