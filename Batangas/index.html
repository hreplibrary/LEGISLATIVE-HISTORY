HISTORY

BATANGAS

The following text/information are lifted from the book of Lee W. Vance entitled Tracing your Philippine Ancestors which was published in Provo, Utah by Stevenson’s Genealogical Center in 1980 and Symbols of the State by the Department of Interior and Local Government of the Republic of the Philippines in 1998.

Batangas is located on the southwestern coast of Luzon Island. It is bounded on the north by Cavite, on the northeast by Laguna and on the east by Quezon province.

Batangas has a land area of 3,119.72 square kilometers. In 2007, it had a population of 2,245,869.

Following is a timeline1,2 of events that led to the creation and development of the province of Batangas:

  • Mid-1200

Batangas was settled by Bornean datus who founded earliest Malay villages at the mouth of Taal River.
  • 1570

Batangas was explored by the Spaniards Martin de Goiti and Juan de Salcedo. Prior to this, Nasugbu, Balayan, Batangas, Tagbakin (Lipa), and others along the Pansipit River.
  • 1581

The province of Batangas was created. Within its jurisdiction lay the vast territory of modern Batangas, Mindoro, Marinduque, and the southern part of Quezon province as far as the Camarines boundary.
  • 1591

Quezon’s southern portion was organized into a province of Kalilaya.
  • 1600

Masbate and Marinduque were separated to form jointly a corregimiento.

Batangas constitutes four legislative districts. Listed below are the municipalities that comprise the districts:

1st District: Municipalities of Balayan, Calaca, Calatagan, Lemery, Lian, Nasugbu, Taal, and Tuy.

2nd District: Municipalities of Bauan, Lobo, Mabini, San Luis, San Pascual, Tingloy and Batangas City.

3rd District: Municipalities of Agoncillo, Alitagtag, Baleti, Cuenca, Laurel, Malvar, Mataas na Kahoy, San Nicolos, San Teresita, Santo Tomas, Talisay, and Tanauan City.  

4th District: Municipalities of Ibaan, Padre Garcia, Rosario, San Jose, San Juan, Taysan, and Lipa City.
