HISTORY

QUEZON

The following text/information are lifted from the book of Lee W. Vance entitled Tracing your Philippine Ancestors which was published in Provo, Utah by Stevenson’s Genealogical Center in 1980 and Symbols of the State by the Department of Interior and Local Government of the Republic of the Philippines in 1998.

The province of Quezon, formerly called Tayabas, is the longest and the third largest province in the entire Philippines.  Situated along the eastern Pacific seacoast of Luzon Island, its long and narrow territory stretches from Isabela in the north to Camarines Sur in the south.  Inland, the province is bounded on the west by Quirino, Nueva Ecija, Bulacan, Rizal, Laguna and Btangas, and on the southeast by Camarines Norte and Camarines Sur.  It faces the Pacific Ocean to the east, and Tayabas Bay and Ragu Gulf to the south, with the Bondoc Peninsula between them.  The Mogpog Pass separates the peninsula from Marinduque Island to the west.  The province includes the small islands near its coast, the most important of which are the Polillo Island Group (Polillo, Palasan, Patnongan and Jamalig Islands) east of Infanta, and Alabat Island east of the Tayabas Isthmus.

Quezon also includes the sub-province of Aurora, which occupies the northern quarter of its territory.  Aurora extends from the northern tip of Quezon, bordering Isabela, southward to Dingali Bay in the south.  On the west, Aurora faces Quirino and Nueva Ecija.

the territory comprising roughly the same area as modern Quezon was made a separate province, called “Kalilaya” after its capital (now the town of Unisan, Quezon).

The northern part of Quezon, including Baler, Casiguran, Infanta and the Polillo Islands (ie., all the organized towns north of Mauban), was annexed to Nueva Ecija. 

  • 1853

The central portion of this territory, namely, the Infanta area and the Polillo Islands, was annexed to Laguna province.
  • Early 1880s

As part of the Spanish overnment’s effort to civilize and subject the wild tribes of Northern Luzon to their control, the politico-military comandancias of Infanta and Principe were organized. These comandancias eventually became districts, remaining dependent upon their mother provinces for fiancnial aid, but exercising considerable governmental autonomy.  Infanta’s capital was at Binangonan de Lampon (Infanta), and was dependent upon its mother province, Laguna, while Principe’s (Aurora’s) capital was at Baler, being dependent upon Nueva Ecija until the end of the Spanish regime.

Quezon Province has 2 districts. The 1st district is composed of the municipalities of Burdeos, General nakar, Infanta, Jomalig, Lucban, Mauban, Pagbilao, Panukulan, patnanungan, Polillo, Real, Sampaloc, and Tayabas. The 2nd district is composed of the municipalities of Candelaria, Dolores, San Antonio, Sariaya, Tiaong, and Lucena City. The 3rd district is composed of the municipalities of Agdangan, Buenavista, Catanauan, general Luna, Macalelon, Mulanay, Padre Burgos, Pitogo, San Andres, San Francisco (Aurora), San Narciso, and Unisan. The 4th district is composed of the municipalities of Alabat, Atimonan, Calauag, Guinayangan, Gumaca, Lopez, Perez, Plaridel, Quezon and Tagkawayan.
