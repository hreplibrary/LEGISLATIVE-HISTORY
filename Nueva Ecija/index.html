HISTORY

NUEVA ECIJA
The following text/information are lifted from the book of Lee W. Vance entitled Tracing your Philippine Ancestors which was published in Provo, Utah by Stevenson’s Genealogical Center in 1980 and Symbols of the State by the Department of Interior and Local Government of the Republic of the Philippines in 1998.

The province of Nueva Ecija lies in the northeastern section of Central Luzon in the northern Philippines.  This “rice bowl of the Philippines” is flanked by mountains on two sides, with the Caraballo Mountains separating it from Nueva Vizcaya on the northeast, and the Sierra Madre Mountains separating it from Quezon province on the east and south.  It is bounded on the south by Bulacan and a small portion of Pampanga, on the west by Tarlac, and on the northwest by Pangasinan.

Agustinian missionaries were among the first to explore and evangelize this territory.  They extended their outposts at an early period into what is now known as the province of Nueva Ecija by following the Rio Grande de la Pampanga.

Nueva Ecija’s earliest history is practically the same as that of Pampanga, of which it formed a part in early Spanish days.

Following is a timeline1,2 of events that led to the creation and development of the province of Nueva Ecija:

DATE

EVENT
  • 1595

Gapan mission was established
  • 1659

Santol (now barrio Santor in Bongabon) was established
  • 1701

Governor General Faust Cruzat y Gongora created Nueva Ecija into a politico-militar comandancia. He named it in honor of his native village of Ecija in Seville, Spain.  Its capital was probably originally located at Cabanatuan.
  • 1728

Some parts were turned over to the Franciscans, and all but nine existing parishes are now administered by either the secular clergy, or the Dutch Fathers of the Missionaries of the Sacred Heart, M.S.C.
  • 1760

Bongabon was established
  • 1816

a fire razed the entire town of Cabanatuan so the capital was transferred to San Isidro.
  • 1818

Nueva Ecija increased its size by annexing the northwestern part of Tayabas (Quezon) province, including Baler, Casiguran, Binangonan de Lambon (Infanta) and the Polillo Islands.  In addition, the town of Palanan, then part of Cagayan, was added to Nueva Ecija’s territory.
  • 1818

The area had originally been annexed from Tayabas (Quezon) province, and included Baler, Casiguran and two other towns. 

  • 1848

Nueva Ecija received additional territory from Pampanga, namely the towns of Gapan, San Isidro, Cabiao, San Antonio and Aliaga.
  • 1853

Principe District was organized from the eastern territory of Nueva Ecija facing the Pacific Ocean.
  • 1856

Isabela province was organized from portions of Nueva Vizcaya, Cagayan, and also from the town of Palanan, annexed to it from Nueva Ecija that year.
  • 1858

Binangonan de Lampon (Infanta) and the Polillo Island group were separated from Nueva Ecija to form part of the new military district of Infanta, created that year, and annexed as a dependency of Laguna province.
  • 1896

Nueva Ecija was one of the first eight provinces to join in the rebellion against Spain
  • 1898

The Spanish government was finally overthrown, and a revolutionary government was established
  • 1899

American military government was established
  • 1903

The 23 municipalities of Nueva Ecija were reduced to 15 by combining Nampicuan with Cuyapo, Zaragoza with Aliaga, Jaen with San Antonio, Cabiao with San Isidro, San Leonardo with Penaranda, Santa Rosa with Cabanatuan, Santo Domingo with Talavera, and Lupao with San Jose by virtue of Philippine Commission Act 933, enacted Oct 8, 1903, as amended by Phil. Commission Act 948, enacted Oct. 19, 1903.

  • February 16, 1901

The towns of Umingan, Rosales, San Quintin and Balungao were separated from Nueva Ecija’s jurisdiction and annexed to Pangasinan by virtue of Philippine Commission Act 86.
  • June 11, 1901

Civil government was restored to Nueva Ecija under the Americans when Nueva Ecija’s provincial government was re-established by virtue of Philippine Commission Act 139.
  • March 4, 1903

ACT 657 AMENDED SECTION 5 OF ACT NO. 139, extending the provisions of the provincial government act to the province of Nueva Ecija and ACT NO. 398 amendatory thereof.
  • October 8, 1903

ACT 933 reduced the 23 municipalities of the province of Nueva Ecija to 15.
  • October 19, 1903

ACT 948 amended PARAGRAPH 1 OF SECTION 1 OF ACT NO. 933, entitled "An act reducing the 23 municipalities of the province of Nueva Ecija to 15."
  • September 12, 1904

By virtue of Philippine Commission Act 1232, Puncan was consolidated with San Jose. Most of these municipalities have been reorganized, and new ones created, to bring the province’s present total to 29 municipalities and three cities.
  • September 12, 1904

ACTS NO. 1232 consolidated the present municipality of Puncan with the municipality of San Jose, both in the province of Nueva Ecija with the seat of municipal government at the present municipality of San Jose
  • August 17, 1907

By virtue of ACT NO. 1687, the number  of municipalities in the province of Nueva Ecija increased from 14 to 15, by separating from Cabanatuan the former municipality of Santa Rosa, reconstituting the latter as a municipality, and giving to each the territory which it comprised prior to the passage of ACT NO. 933.
  • September 20, 1907

By virtue of ACT NO. 1717, the number of municipalities in the province of Nueva Ecija increased from 15 to 21, by separating from the present municipalities of Cuyapo, Aliaga, San Antonio, San Isidro, Penaranda, and Talavera the former municipalities of Nampicuan, Zaragosa, Jaen, Cabiao, San Leonardo, and Santo Domingo, respectively, and giving to each the territory which it comprised prior to the passage of ACT NO. 933.
  • 1912

The provincial capital was transferred from San Isidro back to Cabanatuan.
  • February 28, 1914

By virtue of ACT NO. 2390, the names of the municipalities of San Juan, province of Nueva Ecija
  • September 8, 1936

By virtue of Commonwealth Act NO. 27, the name of the barrio of San Francisco, municipality of Zaragoza, province of Nueva Ecija, was changed to General Luna.
  • June 12, 1950

By virtue of REPUBLIC ACT 496, the municipality of Bitulok in the province of Nueva Ecija was created
  • June 20, 1953

By virtue of REPUBLIC ACT 949, the name of the municipality of Bitulok in the province of Nueva Ecija was changed to Sabani
  • May 11, 1955

By virtue of Republic Act 1221, the municipality of Llanera in the province of Nueva Ecija was created
  • June 16, 1955

By virtue of Republic Act 1318, the name of the municipality of Sabani, province of Nueva Ecija, was changed to Gabaldon
  • June 20, 1957

By virtue of Republic Act 1665, the name of the municipality of Papaya in the province of Nueva Ecija was changed to General Tinio.
  • June 22, 1957

By virtue of REPUBLIC ACT 1863, the municipality of General Mamerto Natividad in the province of Nueva Ecija was created
  • June 21, 1959

By virtue of REPUBLIC ACT 2475, the barrio of General Luna in the municipality of Rizal, province of Nueva Ecija was created
  • June 21, 1959

By virtue of REPUBLIC ACT 2476, the Sitio of Manlungay of the barrio of Canaan, municipality of Rizal, province of Nueva Ecija, was converted into a barrio of the said municipality to be known as barrio Villa Paraiso
  • 1965

Palayan became the chartered city, an action which took effect within the space of about four years.  Palayan City remains the provincial capital today.
  • June 19, 1965

REPUBLIC ACT 4475 established the new capital of the province of Nueva Ecija, creating the City of Palayan, providing a charter therefor, and for other purposes general provisions

  • June 19, 1971

By virtue of Republic Act 6221, a certain district was converted into a barrio in the municipality of Cuyapo, province of Nueva Ecija.
  • June 19, 1971

By virtue of Republic Act 6249, certain barrios in the municipality of Aliaga, province of Nueva Ecija were created
  • 2013

Nueva Ecija has 4 districts. The 1st district is composed of the following municipalities of Aliaga, Cuyapo, Guimba, Licab, Nampicuan, Quezon, Santo Domingo, Talavera and Zaragoza. The 2nd district is composed of the municipalities of Carranglan, Llanera, Lupao, Pantabangan, Rizal and Talugtug and the cities of San Jose and Science City of Muñoz. The 3rd district is composed of the municipalities of Bangabon, Gabaldon, General Mamerto Natividad, Laur, Santa Rosa, Cabanatuan City, and Palayan City. The 4th district is composed of the municipalities of Cabiao, General Tinio, Jaen, Peñaranda, San Antonio, San Isidro, San Leonardo and the City of Gapan.
