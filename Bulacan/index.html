HISTORY

BULACAN

The following text/information are lifted from the book of Lee W. Vance entitled Tracing your Philippine Ancestors which was published in Provo, Utah by Stevenson’s Genealogical Center in 1980 and Symbols of the State by the Department of Interior and Local Government of the Republic of the Philippines in 1998.

Bulacan is located in the Northeast of Manila Bay, stretching across the plains of central Luzon. It is surrounded by Nueva Ecija on the north and northeast, Quezon on the east, Rizal on the south, Manila Bay on the south and southwest and Pampanga on the west.

Bulacan has a land area of 2,774.85 square kilometers and in 2007, it had a population of 2,826,926.  

Following is a timeline1,2 of events that led to the creation and development of the province of Bulacan:

The town of San Rafael was organized.
  • 1848

Bulacan was expanded with the annexation of the town of San Miguel de Mayumo and the adjacent areas of Bulacan from Pampanga.
  • February 27, 1901

Americans established a civil provincial government in the province with Malolos as the provincial capital.
  • 1903

The 25 municipalities of Bulacan were reduced to 13 by consolidating Norzagaray with Angat, San Rafael and Bustos and Santa Isabel with Malolos, Marilao with Meycauayan, Obando with Polo, Pulilan with Quingua, San Ildefonso with San Miguel, and San Jose with Santa Maria.
  • 1907

By virtue of ACT 1588, the number of municipalities in the province of Bulacan was increased from thirteen to fourteen, by separating from Polo the former municipality of Obando and giving the latter the territory which it comprised prior to the passage of ACT 932.

By virtue of ACT 1619, the number of municipalities in the province of Bulacan was increased from fourteen to fifteen, by separating from Baliaug the former municipality of San Rafael and giving the latter the territory which it comprised prior to the passage of ACT 932.
  • March 10, 1917

The province of Bulacan was created under Act 2711.
  • June 19, 1960

By virtue of Republic Act (RA) 2851, the barrio of Bantog in the municipality of San miguel, province of Bulacan was created.
  • June 18, 1966

By virtue of RA 4702, the name of the municipality of Bigaa in the province of Bulacan to the municipality of Balagtas was changed.

  • October 2, 2006

By virtue of Republic Act (RA) 9356, Municipality of Meycauayan was converted into a component city.
  • As of 2013

Bulacan constitutes four legislative districts. Listed below are the municipalities that comprise the districts:
1st District: Municipalities of Bulacan, Calumpit, Hagonoy, Paombong, Pulilan, and Malolos City.
2nd District: Municipalities of Balagtas, Baliuag, Bocaue, Bustos, Guiguinto, Pandi and Plaridel.
3rd District: Municipalities of Angat, Doña Remedios Trinidad, Norzagaray, San Ildefonso, San Miguel and San Rafael.
4th District: Municipalities of Marilao, Obando, Santa Maria and Meycauayan City.
