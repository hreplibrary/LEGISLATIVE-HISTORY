BILIRAN

The following text/information are lifted from the book of Lee W. Vance entitled Tracing your Philippine Ancestors which was published in Provo, Utah by Stevenson’s Genealogical Center in 1980 and Symbols of the State by the Department of Interior and Local Government of the Republic of the Philippines in 1998.

Biliran is located north of Leyte Province in the Western Visayas region in the Central Philippines. It is surrounded by the Samar Sea on the north and east, the Leyte and Carigara Bays on the south, and the Visayan sea on the west.

Biliran has a land area of 536.01 square kilometers. In 2007, it had a population of 150,031.

Following is a timeline 1,2 of events that led to the creation and development of the province of Biliran:

DATE

EVENT
  • 1601

Franciso Vicente conducted the building of galleons on the island.

  • 1828

The earliest missions of Biliran were erected as parishes.
  • 1850

The parishes as well as others in western Leyte were turned over to the diocesan secular clergy.
  • April 8, 1959

By virtue of REPUBLIC ACT NO. 2141, Biliran was created as a province.
  • June 21, 1969

By virtue of R.A. 5977, R.A. 2141 was amended and changed the title of Lt. Governor to Governor, with the executive power of a provincial governor.
  • May 11, 1992

By virtue of R.A. 7160 known and cited as the "Local Government Code of 1991", Biliran became an independent province.
  • As of 2010

The province constitutes a lone legislative district that includes the municipalities of Almeria, Biliran, Cabucgayan, Caibiran, Culaba, Kawayan, Maripipi, and Naval municipalities.
