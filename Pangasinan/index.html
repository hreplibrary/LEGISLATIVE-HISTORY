HISTORY

PANGASINAN
The following text/information are lifted from the book of Lee W. Vance entitled Tracing your Philippine Ancestors which was published in Provo, Utah by Stevenson’s Genealogical Center in 1980 and Symbols of the State by the Department of Interior and Local Government of the Republic of the Philippines in 1998.

The province of Pangasinan occupies the north-central plains of region of central Luzon Island.  It is bounded by the provinces of La Union, Benguet and Nueva Vizcaya to the north; Nueva Ecija to the east; and Tarlac and Zambales to the south.  Its irregular coastline faces the South China Sea to the west, and Lingayen Gulf to the north.

Although the province’s ancient name was “Layug na Caboloan”, the early Spaniards named it “Pangasinan” (from the word “asin”, meaning “salt”) when they observed salt-making was a prominent occupation there.

  • As of 2013

Pangasinan has 5 districts. The 1st district is composed of the municipalities of Agno, Anda, Bani, Bolinao, Burgos, Dasol, Infanta, Mabini, Sual, and Alaminos City. The 2nd district is composed of the municipalities of Aguilar, Basista, Binmaley, Bugallon, Labrador, Lingayen, Mangatarem, and Urbiztondo. The 3rd district is composed of the municipalities of Bayambang, Calasiao, Malasiqui, Mapandan, Santa Barbara, and San Carlos City. The 4th district is composed of the municipalities of Manaoag, Mangaldan, San Fabian, San Jacinto, and Dagupan City. The 5th district is composed of the municipalities of Alcala, Bautista, Binalonan, Laoac, Pozorrubio, Santo Tomas, Sison, Villasis, and the City of Urdaneta. The 6th district is composed of the municipalities of Asingan, Balungao, Natividad, Rosales, San Manuel, San Nicolas, San Quintin, Santa Maria, Tayug, and Umingan.
