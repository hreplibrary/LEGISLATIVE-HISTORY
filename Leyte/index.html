HISTORY

LEYTE


The following text/information are lifted from the book of Lee W. Vance entitled Tracing your Philippine Ancestors which was published in Provo, Utah by Stevenson’s Genealogical Center in 1980 and Symbols of the State by the Department of Interior and Local Government of the Republic of the Philippines in 1998.

The province of Leyte comprises the northern three-quarters of Leyte Island, situated in the northeastern Visayas region of the central Philippines.  Its dependent islands include Maripipi, Gigantanga, and the island-subprovince of Biliran, among others.  Leyte is separated from Samar Island on the northeast by the narrow waters of the San Juanico Strait, now spanned b the famed San Juanico Bridge, the longest in the country.  Leyte’s daughter province of Southern Leyte lies to the south.  Water surrounds its coastline in all other directions, wit the Leyte Gulf to the east, the Camotes Sea (with Cebu and Bohol beyond) to the west, the Visayan Sea (with Masbate beyond) to the northwest, and Carigara Bay of the Samar Sea (with Samar beyond) to the north.

The early history of the provinces of Leyte and its daughter province, Southern Leyte, are essentially the same up to 1959, when the latter was created.

Before the Spanish discovered the Philippines, the island of Leyte, as well as the smaller islands along its coast (notably Limasawa to the south) were fairly well populated, and boasted some of the richest settlements in the Visayan Islands.  Fishing, agriculture, and the manufacture of weapons, jewelry and household utensils were well developed industries, while bartering with Chinese and Siamese traders had been carried on for untold centuries.

DATE

EVENT
  • Early Spanish times

Leyte (originally named “Tandaya”), together with Samar (named “Ibabao”), was under the jurisdiction of Cebu.
  • 1521

Magellan sailed from the island of Homonhon (south of Samar), to the island of Limasawa south of Leyte,

  • March 29, 1521

A blood compact of peace between Rajah Kolambu and Magellan was held.

  • March 31, 1521

In Lamasawa, on Easter Sunday, Fr. Pedro de Valderama, along with others, celebrated the first mass ever held on Philippine soil, which was attended by Rajah Siago and Magellan and their respective followers.
  • February 1543

the Spanish navigator, Villalobos, while searching for provisions, landed on the island of Leyte and named it “Felipina,” after Prince Philip of pain.  This name, in a slighty changed form “Filipinas,” was later given to the entire archipelago, and is the origin of their present name, the Philippines.
  • 1565

Miguel Lopez de Legazpi also visited the Leyte area, stopping at Abuyog and Limasawa.
  • 1580

Jesuit mission had been established at Barugo.
  • 1595

Jesuit mission had been established at Abuyog , followed by others at; Dulag, Carigara, Palo, Tanaoan, Alang-alang, and Ormoc.
  • By (or before) 1735

Leyte had already been made a politico-military province, which included Samar within its territory, and had its capital at Carigara.
  • 1768

Samar was separated from Leyte and organized into a new province.
  • 1768

With the expulsion of the Jesuits from the Philippines, these missions and parishes were taken over by Augustinians.
  • 1843

The parishes of eastern Leyte (namely, Leyte, Carigara, Barugo, Jaro, Babatngon, Tacloban, Palo, Alangalang, Tanauan, Tolosa, Dulag, Abuyog, Hinunangan and Hinundayan) were turned over to the Franciscans.
  • 1850

the rest of the parishes in western Leyte were turned over by the Augustinians to the diocesan secular clergy. 

  • 1959

Creation of Southern Leyte province.

  • Leyte province comprised the entire island of Leyte and its smaller dependent islands, including Biliran.
  • 1860

Tacloban became the capital of the province until today. Former capitals were the ff: Carigara, later transferred to Palo, then to Tanawan.

The Port of Tacloban was opened to foreign trade.

Leyte became involved in the Philippine Revolution.

Leyte has 5 districts. The 1st district is composed of the following municipalities: Alangalang, Babatngon, Palo, San Miguel, Santa Fe, Tanauan, Tolosa, and Tacloban City. The 2nd district is composed of the following municipalities: Barugo, Burauen, Capoocan, Carigara, Dagami, Dulag, Jaro, Julita, La Paz, Mayorga, MacArthur, Pastrana, Tabontabon, and Tunga. The 3rd district is composed of the following municipalities: Calubian, Leyte, San Isidro, Tabango, and Villaba. The 4th district is composed of the following municipalities: Albuera, Isabel, Kananga, Matag-ob, Merida, Palompon, and Ormoc City. The 5th district is composed of the following municipalities: Bato, Baybay, Hilongos, Hindang, Inopacan, Javier, Mahaplag, and Matalom.
