HISTORY

ANTIQUE

The following text/information are lifted from the book of Lee W. Vance entitled Tracing your Philippine Ancestors which was published in Provo, Utah by Stevenson’s Genealogical Center in 1980 and Symbols of the State by the Department of Interior and Local Government of the Republic of the Philippines in 1998.

Antique is one of the provinces occupying the island of Panay in the Western Visayas region of the Philippines. The other three provinces, with which it shares much of its early history, are Aklan, Capiz and Iloilo.

Antique has a land area of 2,729.17 square kilometers. In 2007, it had a population of 515,265. 

Following is a timeline 1,2 of events that led to the creation and development of the province Antique:

DATE

EVENT
  • February, 1213

The Bornean chieftains with about 1,000 followers arrived in Panay and bought the island from the Negrito inhabitants. They named the island Madia-as and divided them into three: Hantik (Antique), Aklan (Capiz and Aklan) and Irong-irong (Iloilo).
  • 1519

Panay Island had been divided into many encomiendas which included Bugason (Bugasong) and Hantik (Antique) in the territory of Modern Antique province.
  • 1581

Hamtic was established.
  • 1596

Nalupa (now Barbaza) was established.
  • 1733

San Jose was established.

San Pedro was established.
  • 1750

Bugasong was established.
  • 1753

Sibalom was established.
  • 1761

Lawa-an was established.

Pandan was established.

  • March 10, 1917

By virtue of Act 2711, the Province of Antique was created.

  • As of 2013

Antique constitutes a lone district that includes the municipalities of Anini-y, Barbaza, Belison, Bugasong, Caluya, Culasi, Hamtic, Laua-an, Libertad, Pandan, Patnongon, San Jose de Buenavista, San Remigio, Sebaste, Sibalom, Tibiao, Tobias Fornier (Dao), and Valderrama.
