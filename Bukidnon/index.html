HISTORY

BUKIDNON

The following text/information are lifted from the book of Lee W. Vance entitled Tracing your Philippine Ancestors which was published in Provo, Utah by Stevenson’s Genealogical Center in 1980 and Symbols of the State by the Department of Interior and Local Government of the Republic of the Philippines in 1998.

Bukidnon is a land-locked province in the Southern Philippines located in the north-central part of the large island of Mindanao. It is bound on the north and northwest by Misamis Oriental, on the east by Agusan del Sur and Davao del Norte, on the south by North Cotabato and on the west by Lanao del Norte and Lanao del Sur. 

Bukidnon has a land area of 10,498.59 square kilometres and in 2007, it had a population of 1,190,284.  

Following is a timeline 1,2 of events that led to the creation and development of the province of Bukidnon:

DATE

EVENT
  • 1849

The time of Governor Narciso Claveria about fifteen settlements were formed in the Tagoloan Valley and Plateu districts. Some of these are said to have been Malaybalay, Sumilao, Linabo, Malitbog and Impasugong. The entire district was called Malaybalay.
  • 1878

Churches had been blessed in Sumilao and Linabo, the visitas erected in Silipon, Bugkaon, Malaybalay, Kalasungay and Impasugong.
  • 1898

Sumilao was the only mission in Bukidnon.
  • 1899

Bukidnon, as part of Misamis, came under the control of Revolutionary government.
  • 1901

Bukidnon surrendered to the American forces.
  • May 15, 1901

Bukidnon became a sub-province of Misamis.
  • 1903

Under the Philippine Commission Act 787, the southern half of modern-day Bukidnon became a part of Cotabato district of the Moro province.
  • 1907

American civil government was established. The Province of Agusan was created, and Bukidnon became its subprovince by virtue of ACT NO. 1693.
  • 1909

The sub-province of Bukidnon was annexed as a sub-province to Agusan, which was made a province that year.
  • September, 1914

Bukidnon became an independent province with Fortich, Sr. As its governor.
  • December, 1941

Other parishes had been opened in Malaybalay, Linabo and Talakag.
  • June 18, 1966

By virtue of REPUBLIC ACT NO. 4787, the municipality of Lantapan in the province of Bukidnon was created.
By virtue of R.A. 4788, the municipality of Kalilangan in the province of Bukidnon was created.
By virtue of R.A. 4789, the municipality of San Fernando in the province of Bukidnon was created.
By virtue of R.A. 4800, the municipality of Don Carlos in the province of Bukidnon was created.
By virtue of R.A. 4801, the municipality of Kitao/Tao in the province of Bukidnon was created.
By virtue of R.A. 4802, the municipality of Quezon in the province of Bukidnon was created.

  • June 17, 1967
 By virtue of R.A. 5087, the name of Barrio Patpat in the Municipality of Malaybalay, Province of Bukidnon was changed to Lapu-Lapu.
  • June 15, 1968
 By virtue of R.A. 5289, the name of Barrio Cawayanon  in  the Municipality of Valencia, Province of Bukidnon was changed to Vintar.
  • June 21, 1969
 By virtue of R.A. 5924, the barrios of  Kisolon and  San Vicente of the Municipality of Impasugong and the Sitio of  Damay of the Municipality of Manolo Fortich  were transferred to  the  Municipality of Sumilao in the Province of Bukidnon.
  • August 16, 1971

By virtue of R.A. 6368, the Municipality of Kadingilan in the Province of Bukidnon was created.
By virtue of R.A. 6369, the Municipality of Damulog in the Province of Bukidnon was created.
  • June 17, 1972
 By virtue of R.A. 6489, the Municipality of Cabanglasan in the Province of Bukidnon was created.
  • 2010

Bukidnon has three legislative districts. Listed below are the municipalities that comprise the districts:
1st District: Municipalities of Baungon, Kalilangan, Libona, Malitbog, Manolo, Fortich, Pangantucan, Sumilao, Talakag
2nd District: Municipalities of Cabanglasan, Impasug-Ong, Lantapan, and San Fernando; City of Malaybalay, and Valencia City.
3rd District: Municipalities of Damulog, Dangcagan, Don Carlos, Kadingilan, Kibawe, Kitaotao, Maramag and Quezon.

  • As of 2012

By virtue of R.A. 10184, the Province of Bukidnon was reapportioned into four (4) Legislative Districts.
