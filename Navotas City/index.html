HISTORY

NAVOTAS CITY

The following text/information are lifted from the book of Lee W. Vance entitled Tracing your Philippine Ancestors which was published in Provo, Utah by Stevenson’s Genealogical Center in 1980 and Symbols of the State by the Department of Interior and Local Government of the Republic of the Philippines in 1998.

 

Malabon City is one of the cities that make up Metro Manila. It is part of the sub-region of Metro Manila informally called CAMANAVA which consists of Caloocan City, Malabon City, Navotas City, and Valenzuela City. Malabon and Navotas border the town of Obando in the province of Bulacan in the northwest. It is primarily a residential and industrial town.

Malabon City has a land area of 19.76 square kilometres while Navotas has a land area of 10.77 square kilometres and in 2007, the combined population of both is 363,681.

Following is a timeline1, 2 of events that led to the creation and development of Navotas City:

DATE

E V E N T
  • 13th Century

Ancient settlements were already thriving in Paranaque, Pasig, Cainta and Taytay.  These settlements were believed to be known to Chinese, Japanese and Indian traders.
  • 1571

Juan de Salcedo visited Taytay and Cainta and brought these towns under Spanish sovereignty.

  • May 21, 1599

Malabon was founded as a “Visita” of Tondo by the Augustinian friars.

  • 1627-1688

Malabon remained under the administrative jurisdiction of the province of Tondo.
  • 1689

The town of Cainta was founded in 1689 while Marikina was founded the following year.
  • 1762

British troops under the command of Captain Thomas Backhouse invaded Pasig, defeated the Spaniards and occupied Taytay and Cainta.
  • 1764

The British withdrew from the islands.
  • 1815

The town of Pateros was founded.
  • 1853

“Distrito de los Montes de San Mateo” or District of the San Mateo Mountains was formed.  Cainta, Taytay, Antipolo and Bosoboso were annexed to this new district from Tondo province.  Angono, Binangonan, Cardona, Morong, Baras, Tanay, Pililla and Jalajala were annexed to the district from Laguna province.  Morong was made the capital of the District.
  • 1859

The name Distrito de Los Montes de San Mateo was changed to Morong District.  At about the same time, Tondo province was renamed Manila province.

  • June 11, 1859

A “Superior Decreto” established a new parish and municipality under the supervision of Friar Matias Navoa.
At about this time, Navotas was founded.
  • 1882

Malabon’s Asilo de Huertanos housed orphans due to a plague.
  • 1898

American military forces controlled the district.
  • June 11, 1901

By virtue of Philippine Commission Act 137, Malabon was officially made a municipality of the newly-created province of Rizal.
  • 1903

Rizal’s various municipalities were combined as follows – Montalban with San Mateo; Taguig and Muntinglupa with Pateros; Malibay with Pasay; Las Pinas with Paranaque, Novaliches with Caloocan; Navotas with Malabon, San Felipe Neri (Mandaluyong) with San Juan del Monte, Cainta and Angono with Taytay; Bosoboso and Teresa with Antipolo, Binangonan, Baras and cardona with Morong; and Quisao and Jalajala with Pililla. 

A month later Binangonan was separated from Morong and Angono was annexed to it.  At the same time, Muntinglupa was separated from Pateros, and annexed as a barrio to Binan, Laguna and later annexed (still as a barrio) to Taguig, itself newly formed from the former pueblos of Pateros, Taguig and Muntinglupa.
  • January 16, 1906

By virtue of Act 1441, Malabon was separated from Navotas into two district municipalities of the Rizal province that revoked Act 942 merging Malabon and Navotas.

  • November 7, 1975

By virtue of Presidential Decree No. 824, Malabon became a part of Metropolitan Manila or the National Capital Region and was no longer a municipality of Rizal.

  • April 21, 2001

By virtue of Republic Act (RA) 9019, Malabon became a city.
  • March 10, 2007

RA 9387 was lapsed into law that converted the Municipality of Navotas into a highly urbanized city to be known as the city of Navotas.
  • June 24, 2007

Navotas became a city after a plebiscite.
  • As of 2010

Malabon City and Navotas City separated into two (2) legislative districts as per the provision of the city charter of Navotas (Republic Act 9387).

Navotas constitutes a lone district that includes the barangays Bagumbayan North, Bagumbayan South, Bangkulasi, Daanghari, Navotas East, Navotas West, North Bay Boulevard North, North Bay Boulevard South, San Jose (Poblacion), San Rafael Village, San Roque, Sipac-Almacen, Tangos and Tanza.
