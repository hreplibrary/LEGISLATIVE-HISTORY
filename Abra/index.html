HISTORY

ABRA
The following text/information are lifted from the book of Lee W. Vance entitled Tracing your Philippine Ancestors which was published in Provo, Utah by Stevenson’s Genealogical Center in 1980 and Symbols of the State by the Department of Interior and Local Government of the Republic of the Philippines in 1998.     

Abra is a land-locked province located in the northern part of Luzon. The province used to be part of the old Ilocos province.  It is bounded by Ilocos Norte in the north and Apayao in the northeast, Ilocos Sur in the south and southwest, while Isabela, Mountain Province and the Central Cordillera Mountain form its eastern boundary.

Following is a timeline1,2 of events that led to the creation and development of the province of Abra based from as far back as official records, researches and other resources provide:

DATE

EVENT
  • 1598

A Spanish garrison was stationed in the area later identified as Bangued.
  • 1611

Augustinian Fathers began their missionary work in the area.
  • 1613

The parish of Bangued was founded and Tayum and San Diego were made visitas of the parish of Bangued.
  • 1803

The parish of Tayum was founded.
  • Prior to 1818

Ilocos province was made up of the present–day Ilocos Norte, Ilocos Sur and Abra.

  • 1818

Ilocos province was divided into Ilocos Norte and Ilocos Sur.  The area of Abra was part of Ilocos Sur.
  • 1823

The parish of Pidigan was founded.
  • 1829

The parish of San Gregorio was founded.

  • 1832

The parish of La Paz was founded.

  • 1846

Abra was created and combined with Lepanto sub-province.  It included the territory of Lepanto sub-province and the towns of San Jose de Manobo, Bangued, Tayun, Pidigan, La Paz and San Gregorio.
  • 1847

The municipality of Bucay was founded and made the first capital of the province of Abra.
  • 1861

The capital of Abra was changed from Bucay to Bangued.
  • 1869

The town of Dolores was founded.
  • 1898

Don Blas Villamor established a provincial government.
  • 1899

Juan de Castro and Enrique del Rosario were designated as delegates of the revolutionary provincial government to the Assembly of Representatives in Malolos.
  • August 19, 1901

A civil government in the province was established under the Americans.

  • November 15, 1901

By virtue of Act 293, the Rancheria of Tubo was declared a part of the province of Abra.
  • August 8, 1902
 By virtue of Act 445, civil governments were established for the settlements of non-Christian tribes in Abra.
  • March 4, 1903
 By virtue of Act 658, the pueblo of Patoc in the province of Abra was renamed Peñarrubia.
  • November 20, 1903

By virtue of Act 1001, the eleven municipalities comprising Abra were reorganized to form only five municipalities.  Bangued absorbed Tayum and Pidigan. Pilar absorbed Villavieja.  Bucay absorbed San Jose.  La Paz absorbed San Gregorio and Dolores absorbed San Juan.
  • July 26, 1904
 By virtue of Act 1205, Act 1001 was amended, reiterating that the seat of municipal government was "at the present municipality of La Paz."
  • April 26, 1906
 By virtue of Act 1481, Act 1001 was amended, transferring the seat of the municipal government of Pilar from Barrio San Juan to the town of Pilar.
  • February 28, 1905 – March 19, 1917

Abra was annexed to Ilocos Sur as a sub-province.
  • March 27, 1907

By virtue of Act 1624, Abra was reorganized into six municipalities by separating the former municipality of Tayum from Bangued, reinstating Tayum's territory prior to the passage of Act 1001 and appropriating funds from the Bangued municipal treasury.
  • February 4, 1916
 By virtue of Act 2618, the townships in Abra were converted to municipalities.
  • April 2, 1917

By virtue of Act 2683, the re-establishment of Abra as an independent province became effective.
  • 1918

Abra consisted of at least 17 municipalities and municipal districts.
  • December 8, 1928

By virtue of Act 3497, the municipality of Dolores, Abra, consisting of Barrios San Juan, Guimba, Daoida, Pattawig, Colabaoan, Lomubang, Lam-ag, Baug, Coliong, Abualan, Silet, Nangobungan, Lupa, Gaang, and Badas, was converted into a regular municipality.
  • June 18, 1952

By virtue of Republic Act 735, Sitios Anoling, Binandirahan, Cabraran, Ilawod, Mabugna, Magogon, Quinuartilan, Solong, and Sumlang, in Camalig, Abra were converted to barrios of the same municipality.
  • 1952

Abra had developed into 18 municipalities and 19 municipal districts. The municipalities included Bangued (provincial capital), Bucay, Danglas, Dolores, Lagayan, Lagangilang, Langiden,  La Paz, Luba, Manabo,  Penarrubia, Pidigan, Pilar, Sal-lapadan, San Juan, San Quintin, Tayum, and Villaviciosa.  The municipal districts included Alaca (Alaoa), Anayan, Ba-ay, Bangilo, Boliney, Bucloc, Buneg, Caganayan, Daguioman, Danac, Lacub, Lanec, Licuan, Malibcong, Nataragan, Naglibacan, Tiempo, Tineg and Tubo. 

  • 1960

By 1960 Abra had 19 municipalities and eight municipal districts.
  • June 17, 1957
 By virtue of Republic Act 5161, the municipality of Tubo, Abra was created.
  • 1970

The 19 municipalities were expanded to 22 municipalities while the eight municipal districts were condensed to five municipal districts.
  • 2010

The lone legislative district of Abra is made up of the municipalities of Bangued, Boliney, Bucay, Bucloc, Daguioman, Danglas, Dolores, La Paz, Lacub, Lagangilang, Lagayan, Langiden, Licuan-Baay, (Licuan), Luba, Malibcong, Manabo, Peñarrubia, Pidigan, Pilar, Sallapadan, San Isidro, San Juan, San Quintin, Tayum, Tineg, Tubo, and Villaviciosa.
