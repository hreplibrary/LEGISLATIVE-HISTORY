HISTORY

DAVAO ORIENTAL

The following text/information are lifted from the book of Lee W. Vance entitled Tracing your Philippine Ancestors which was published in Provo, Utah by Stevenson’s Genealogical Center in 1980 and Symbols of the State by the Department of Interior and Local Government of the Republic of the Philippines in 1998.

 

The province of Davao Oriental stretches along the southeastern coast of Mindanao Island in the southern Philippines.  It is bounded by Agusan del Sur and Surigao del Sur on the north, Davao del Norte on the west, the Davao Gulf and the Celebes Sea on the south, and by the vast Pacific Ocean on the east. 

Davao del Oriental has a land area of 5, 670.07 square kilometers. In 2007, it had a population 486,104.  

Following is a timeline1, 2 of events that led to the creation and development of the province of Davao Oriental:

TIME

EVENT
  • 1528

Alvaro de Saavedre visited the Sarangani islands at the southern tip of Davao del Sur.
  • 1591

Caraga (Davao Oriental) was an encomienda of Cebu province.
  • 1844

Governor Figuiroa of Zamboanga succeeded in persuading the Sultan of Mindanao to transfer control over the vast Davao region of Mindanao to the Spanish government.
  • 1847

Davao town or pueblo was established although it was settled earlier by natives.
  • 1849

Oyanguren was installed as the first governor of the new province, named “Nueva Guipuzcoa” in honor of his home province in Spain. The capital was at Davao town, renamed “Vergara” in honor of the governor’s home town.  Nueva Guipuzcoa included the three modern Davao provinces and most of the eastern coast of Mindanao northward to Cauit Point, just north of Tigao in modern Surigao del Sur.
  • 1855

The province of Nueva Guipuzcoa was short-lived. It was divided into the two politico-military comandancias of Bislig and Davao.  Essentially, all the former territory of Nueva Guipuzcoa lying along the eastern Mindanao coast fell under the jurisdiction of the new comandancias of Bislig; and that surrounding Davao Gulf was under the jurisdiction of the Davao comandancia.
  • 1860

When the governments throughout Mindanano were reorganized into six politico-military districts, Bislig  comandancias was annexed to Surigao, thus returning many towns to is jurisdiction which formerly had belonged to it, to form the Third, or East, District of Mindanao.  Davao, including most of southeastern Mindanao, remained to form the Fourth, or Southeastern, District.
  • 1898

After the Philippine Revolution, the Spanish evacuated from Davao, and were replaced by a short-lived revolutionary government.
  • June 1, 1903

A civil government for Davao province was established by the Americans.

At this time, under Philippine Commission Act 787, Davao became one of the districts of the newly-created Moro Province, which included the Sulu Archipelago and the whole island of Mindanao, with the exception of Misamis and Surigao, within its five districts.
  • December 20, 1913

The Moro Province was renamed the Department of Mindanao and Sulu.
  • September 1, 1914

The five districts of Davao became seven provinces, under the Department’s supervisory jurisdiction.

One of these new provinces was Davao by virtue of Philippine Commission Act 2309.
  • 1920

The Department of Mindanao and Sulu was abolished, and part of its duties was taken over by the Bureau of Non-Christian Tribes.
  • 1922

Davao was freed from the supervisory control of the national government, and became a regular province.
  • 1958

The Maryknoll Fathers started operating the area of Davao Oriental and Davao del Norte (the Tagum Prelature).
  • May 8, 1967
By virtue of Republic Act 4867, the province of Davao was divided, and Davao Oriental assigned the following towns; Mati (the capital), Lupon Governor Generoso, Manay, Caraga, Baganga, and Cateel.
Since then, four more municipalities have been added; Banay-Banay, Boston, San Isidro and Tarragona.
  • 2010

Davao Oriental constitutes two legislative districts. Listed below are the municipalities that comprise the districts:
1st District: Municipalities of Baganga, Boston, Caraga, Cateel, Manay, and Tarragona.    
2nd District: Municipalities of Baybanay, Governor Generoso, Lupon, Mati, and San Isidro.
