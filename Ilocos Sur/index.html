HISTORY

ILOCOS SUR
The following text/information are lifted from the book of Lee W. Vance entitled Tracing your Philippine Ancestors which was published in Provo, Utah by Stevenson’s Genealogical Center in 1980 and Symbols of the State by the Department of Interior and Local Government of the Republic of the Philippines in 1998.

The province of Ilocos Sur can be found at the northwestern corner of Luzon Island in the northern part of the Philippines. It is bounded on the north by Ilocos Norte, its sister province;  Abra and Mountain Province on the east, on the south by Benguet and La Union, and by the South China Sea on the west.

Following is a timeline1,2 of events that led to the creation and development of the province of Ilocos Sur:

DATE

EVENT
  • 1572

The Spanish explorer Juan de Salcedo and his expedition arrived in the Ilocos region, a place with fairly advanced cluster of towns and settlements  familiar to Chinese and Japanese traders
Salcedo established Vigan as the capital of the new province of Ilocos, or “Ylocos,” which he renamed “Villa Fernandina”, one of the early Christian parishes erected in this region.
  • 16th century

The province of Ilocos included all of modern Ilocos Norte, Ilocos Sur, Abra, and parts of La Union and Mountain Province.
Governor-General Legazpi gave the Vigan encomienda to Salcedo to reward his successful conquest of northern Luzon. 
Towns established by the end of the 16th Century: Santa Maria, Narvacan, Bantay, Candon, and Sinait

Diocese of Nueva Segovia was established in lal-lo, Cagayan however it was vulnerable to floods
  • 1758

The Diocese of Nueva Segovia was transferred to Vigan, Ilocos but retained its former name, i.e., after an extinct town of Cagayan 

  • 1611

Pangasinan was created where part of modern La Union (then Ilocos) was annexed to it

  • February 2, 1818
  • the ancient province of Ilocos was divided into two: Ilocos Norte with its capital at Laoag, and Ilocos Sur, retaining the old capital of Vigan.
  • Ilocos Sur then included its present territory, plus the northeastern part of modern La Union and all of modern Abra.
  • 1846

Abra province was separated from Ilocos Sur and was organized as a new province.
  • 1854

La Union province was created from the union of parts of Ilocos Sur and Pangasinan
  • 1898

A revolutionary government was established in Candon
  • August 16, 1901

a civil government established by virtue of an act of the Philippine Commission Act 205 but government was actually organized effective Sept 1,1901.

  • October 8, 1903

Under the Philippine Commission Act 934, approved on Oct. 8, 1903, 10 towns of Ilocos Sur were dissolved and annexed to adjacent towns by consolidating:

  • San Jose and Sevilla with Santa Cruz;
  • Salcedo with Santa Lucia;
  • San Esteban and Nueva Coveta with Santiago;
  • San Ildefonso with Santo Domingo; and
  • Banta, Caoayan, Santa Catalina and San Vicente with Vigan
  • April 1, 1905

To further aid in the financial solvency of the region, Abra province was annexed as a sub-province to Ilocos Sur, under the Philippine Commission Act 1306 enacted on Feb. 28, 1905, but only took effect on this date
  • April 26, 1907

By virtue of ACT 1633, the former Municipality of Santa Catalina was separated from the Municipality of Vigan, reestablishing it as a new municipality and giving it the territory of which it was constituted prior to the passage of ACT No. 934
  • February 28, 1914
 By virtue of ACT 2390, the township of Barrit-Luluno, Province of Ilocos Sur was changed to Luba.
  • March 9, 1917

Under the Philippine Legislative Act 2683 which was enacted on March 9, 1917, Abra was reorganized as a separate province
  • December 10, 1941

At the outset of World War II, the Japanese landed in Ilocos Sur. Six days later, they invaded Santa, Vigan, and neighboring towns, destroyed the churches and rectories of Bantay, Lapog, Santiago and Cervantes, and the municipal offices of Narvacan and Candon, thus accounting for the lack of records in many of these places for preceding periods of time

  • June 10, 1955

By virtue of REPUBLIC ACT 1246, the Municipality of Concepcion was renamed to Gregorio del Pilar.
  • June 20, 1957

By virtue of REPUBLIC ACT 1627, the Municipality of Bauguen was renamed to Salcedo.
  • June 18, 1961

By virtue of REPUBLIC ACT 3386, the Municipality of Lapog was renamed to San Juan.
  • June 18, 1964

By virtue of REPUBLIC ACT 4035, the Municipality of Angaki was renamed to Quirino.
  • March 24, 2007

By virtue of REPUBLIC ACT 9411, January 22 of each year was declared as a special nonworking holiday in the City of Vigan, Ilocos Sur to commemorate its anniversary as a city.
  • As of 2013

Ilocos Sur has 2 districts. The 1st district is composed of the municipalities of Bantay, Cabugao, Caoayan, Magsingal, San Ildefonso, San Juan, San Vicente, Santa Catalina, Santo Domingo, Sinait, and Vigan. The 2nd district is composed of the municipalities of Alilem, Banayoyo, Burgos, Cervantes, Galimuyod, Gregorio del Pilar, Lidlidda, nagbukel, Narvacan, Quirino, Salcedo, San Emilio, San Esteban, Santa, Santa Cruz, Santa Lucia, Santa Maria, Santiago, Sigay, Sugpon, Suyo, Tagudin, and Candon City.
