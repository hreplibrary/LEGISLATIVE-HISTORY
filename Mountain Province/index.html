HISTORY

MOUNTAIN PROVINCE

The following text/information are lifted from the book of Lee W. Vance entitled Tracing your Philippine Ancestors which was published in Provo, Utah by Stevenson’s Genealogical Center in 1980 and Symbols of the State by the Department of Interior and Local Government of the Republic of the Philippines in 1998.

The Mountain Province is located in the central part of northern Luzon Island, in the northern Philippines.  True to its name, it is comprised of a broad range of high and massive mountain, among which arise the tributaries of the Cagayan River.  The Mountain Province is bounded on the north by Kalinga-Apayao, on the east by Isabela, on the south by Ifugao, and on the west by Benguet, Ilocos Sur, and a small portion of Abra.  It is presently composed of 10 municipalities, with Bontoc as the capital.

Now only a fraction of its former size, Mountain Province has included at some point during its existence the provinces of Kalinga-Apayao, Ifugao, Benguet, and parts of Ilocos Sur, La Union, Abra, Isabela and Nueva Vizcaya.  Consequently, any complete discussion of its history must treat the entire mountain region as one.

Although it is one of the most interesting areas of the country, probably less is known of the ancient history of Mountain Province than any other part of the Philippines.  It was so inaccessible and presented so few inviting prospects to colonizers seeking easy wealth, that it remained largely uncivilized and undeveloped in the 300-year Spanish rule of the country. 

Following is a timeline1,2 of events that led to the creation and development of the province of Mountain Province:

DATE

EVENT
  • 1623

The conquistadores came to the area of Baguio in Benguet to evaluate the extent of the gold deposits there
  • 1663

Governor-General Salcedo sent an expedition led by Pedro Duran de Monforte to the Palidan area of Lepanto.  It is said that they penetrated as far as Kayan (now Tanidan) in the Mountain Province

  • 1785

Governor-General sent the first really successful military expedition into the area, to subdue an uprising of the Kalingas.
  • 1829

Guillermo Galvey led an expedition to the District of Benguet
  • 1846

Galvey established a comandancia at La Trinidad, the principal town of the new politico-military province of Benguet
  • 1859

Bontoc was created
  • 1852

The politico-military province of Lepanto was created
  • 1880

The Spanish authorities began a concerted effort to subjugate and Christianize the people of those parts of the mountainous regions of Luzon which and not as yet been organized into provinces
  • 1890

Politico-military comandancias of “Apayos” (Apayao) and Amburayan were organized
  • 1891

Kayapa (Cayapa) and Kabugaoan (Cabugaoan) were established
  • 1898

end of the Spanish rule of the Philippines
  • 1668

a mission was established by two Augustinians at Kayan, but it was abandoned five years later when the soldiers of the adjacent garrison were forced by the natives to leave.
  • 1881

The Kayan mission was re-opened, as well as new missions or visitas in Banaao, Sabangan, Bontoc, and Sacasacan (which was soon transferred to Sadanga), as well as others.
  • 1907

Missions were established in Bontoc and Bauko
  • November 23 1900

The first province to be re-established was Benguet, civilly organized with its new capital at Baguio
  • 1903

The former commandancia of Cayapa was annexed as a township to Benguet by virtue of Philippine Commission Act 48
  • February 6, 1901

Americans organized a civil provincial government for Cagayan on February 6, 1901, Kalinga and Apayao became part of that province by virtue of Philippine Commission Act 83 and Philippine Commission Act 209 enacted on August 22, 1901.
  • May 28, 1902

Act No. 410 of the Philippine Commission organized the former comandancias of Lepanto, Bontoc and Amburayan, together with all the remaining territory lying between Abra, Cagayan and Bontoc into the new provisions of Lepanto-Bontoc.  This new province comprised three sub-provinces – Lepanto, Bontoc and Amburayan – and had its capital at Cervantes (now Ilocos Sur).
  • May 9, 1907

Kalinga was separated from Cagayan and made a new sub-province of Lepanto-Bontoc province, with its seat at Tauit by virtue of Philippine Commission Act 1642
  • August 18, 1908

Philippine Legislature changed the mountain region’s administration. They dissolved Lepanto-Bontoc Province and combined its four sub-provinces (Lepanto, Bontoc, Amburayan, and Kalinga) with the three sub-provinces of Apayao (formerly part of Cagayan), Ifugao (formerly named the comandancia of Quiangan, a part of Nueva Vizcaya), and Benguet ( dissolved as an independent province and annexed as a sub-province).  This immense new “super-province”, now comprised of seven sub-provinces, they renamed “Mountain Province”. Bontoc was declared the capital by virtue of Legislative Act No. 1876.
  • February 1920

The boundaries of Benguet Sub-province, Ilocos Sur and La Union were changed, with the abolition of the sub-provinces of Amburayan and Lepanto. Benguet received Bakun from Amburayan, and Mankayan from Lepanto.  Ilocos Sur received Tagudin, Alilem, Sigay, Sugpon and Suyo from Amburayan, and Angaki (Quirino), Baugen (Salcedo), Cervantes, Concepcion (Gregorio del Pilar), and San Emilio from Lepanto.  La Union received San Gabriel, Santol and Sudipen from Amburayan.
  • June 12, 1950

An act to transfer certain barrios from the territorial jurisdiction of the municipal district of Bontoc to that of the municipal district of Sadanga, sub-province of Bontoc, Mountain Province by virtue of Republic Act 500
  • June 16, 1950

The municipal district of La Trinidad and Tabuk was converted into a regular municipality known by the same name by virtue of REPUBLIC ACTS 531 and 533, respectively.
  • May 15, 1951

By virtue of Republic Act 616 the municipal district of Itogon, sub-province of Benguet, Mountain Province, was converted into a regular municipality known as the municipality of Itogon.
  • June 16, 1955

By virtue of Republic Act 1302, the municipal district of Mankayan, sub-province of Benguet, Mountain Province, was converted into a municipality
  • March 28, 1959

By virtue of REPUBLIC ACT 2101, the name of the municipality of Kayan, Mountain Province, was changed to Tadian municipality

  • April 5, 1959

By virtue of REPUBLIC ACT 2111, the Municipal District of Lamut was created.

By virtue of REPUBLIC ACT 2116, the name of the barrio of Cabicalan in the municipal district of Potia, Ifugao, Mountain Province, was changed to barrio San Domingo

  • June 18, 1961

By virtue of REPUBLIC ACT 3380, the name of the municipal district of Burnay in Mountain Province was changed to Lagawe
  • June 16, 1962

By virtue of REPUBLIC ACT 3488, the municipality of Paracales in Mountain Province was created
  • June 22, 1963

By virtue of REPUBLIC ACT 3672, the municipality of Flora in the subprovince of Apayao, Mountain Province was created
  • June 19, 1965

By virtue of REPUBLIC ACT 4396, the municipality of liwan in mountain province was created
  • June 18, 1966

The former large Mountain Province was divided into four provinces: Benguet, Ifugao, Kalinga-Apayao and Bontoc (later renamed Mountain Province).  The new Mountain Province was comprised only of the territory of the former sub-province of Bontoc.  Its towns then included Bontoc (the capital), Barlig, Bauko, Besao, Natonin, Sabangan, Sadanga, Sagada, Tadian and Paracales by virtue of Republic Act 4695
  • June 18, 1966

By virtue of REPUBLIC ACT 4738, the name of the municipality of Paracales, Mountain Province, was changed to Paracelis
  • June 18, 1966

By virtue of REPUBLIC ACT 4739, certain barrios from the municipality of Natonin were transferred to the municipality of Barlig, Mountain Province

  • June 18, 1966

By virtue of REPUBLIC ACT 4741, the municipality of Pasil in Mountain Province was created
  • June 18, 1966

By virtue of REPUBLIC ACT 4763, Barrio Kiling in the municipality of Paracales, Mountain Province, was transferred to the municipality of Potia, same province

  • June 18, 1966

By virtue of REPUBLIC ACT 4778, Barrio Balantoy, municipality of Balbalan, Mountain Province was created
  • As of 2013

Mountain Province has a lone district composed of the following municipalities: Barlig, Bauko, Besao, Bontoc, Natonin, Paracelis, Sabangan, Sadanga, Sagada, and Tadian.
