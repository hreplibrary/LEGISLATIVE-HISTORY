BAGUIO CITY

The following text/information are lifted from the book of Lee W. Vance entitled Tracing your Philippine Ancestors which was published in Provo, Utah by Stevenson’s Genealogical Center in 1980 and Symbols of the State by the Department of Interior and Local Government of the Republic of the Philippines in 1998.

The City is landlocked within the province of Benguet, thus bounding it on all sides by its different municipalities; on the North by the capital town of La Trinidad, on the East by Itogon and to the South and West by Tuba. With City Hall as reference point, it extends 8.2 kilometers from East to West and 7.2 kilometers from North to South. It has a perimeter of 30.98 kilometers.

Baguio was named after bigyiw, meaning, moss, a green plant, which the first Igorot found around the lake (now Burnham lake).

Following is a timeline1,2,3 of events that led to the creation and development of the province of Baguio City:

DATE

EVENT
  • 1642

The “land of the Igorots” was visited by Alonso Martin Quirante, Comandant of Pangasinan.
  • 1755

An Augustinian friar held the first mass at La Trinidad.
  • 1756

Agustinians established a mission in Tondo, near Tuba, making occasional visits from parent parish at Agoo (La Union) but only lasted for three years.
  • 1829

Comandante Guillermo Galvey led an expedition to the District of Benguet and led another 45 expeditions after.

  • 1846

Galvey established a comandancia at La Trinidad, which was named after his wife.
  • 1891

Another mission was founded at La Trinindad, and others were established at about the same time at Kopangan and Daklan (three miles north of Bodok).

  • 1898

All missions were deserted when the Spanish priests were compelled to leave the Philippine Islands.

  • November 22, 1900

Baguio became a town through ACT NO. 48, and ACT NO. 1397, which superseded it on September 14, 1905.
  • November 23, 1900

By virtue of ACT NO. 49, a civil government was established by the Philippine Commission for the province of Benguet, with Baguio as the new capital. A simplified form of government was adopted for the towns of Baguio, Trinidad, Galiano, Itogon, Tublay, Atok, Kapangan, Balakbak, Palina, Ampusungan, Loo, Buguias, Kabayan, Adaoay, Bokod, Daklan, Sablan, Kibungan and Ambuklao.
  • June 1, 1903

The Philippine Commission made Baguio as the summer capital of the Philippines.
  • 1907

The missions were re-established by the Congregation of the Immaculate Heart of Mary Fathers.
Baguio, Bontoc and Bauko missions were established.
  • 1908

Itogon and La Trinidad missions were established.
  • August 18, 1908

Benguet, along with Bontoc, Ifugao, Amburayan, Lepanto, Kalinga and Apayao, was made one of seven sub-provinces of Mountain Province.

  • September 1, 1909

Baguio became a chartered city by virtue of ACT NO. 1963.
  • March 27, 1916

ACT NO. 2661 made applicable to the city of Baguio, Mountain Province that part of ACT NO. 2657, known as the "Administrative Code," contained in Chapter 50, sections 2520 to 2554, inclusive, known as "The Charter of the City of Baguio."
  • April 26, 1945

American forces liberated the city.
  • February 10, 1989

By virtue of Republic Act 6710, September 1 of each year is designated as Baguio Charter Day and is a Non-Working Holiday in the City of Baguio in order to commemorate the Anniversary of the said City.
  • September 1, 2009
 Baguio City celebrated its Centennial anniversary.
  • As of 2013

Baguio City has 20 administrative districts.
