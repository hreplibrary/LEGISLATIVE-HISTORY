HISTORY

BASILAN

The following text/information are lifted from the book of Lee W. Vance entitled Tracing your Philippine Ancestors which was published in Provo, Utah by Stevenson’s Genealogical Center in 1980 and Symbols of the State by the Department of Interior and Local Government of the Republic of the Philippines in 1998.

 

Basilan is located at the southern part of Zamboanga peninsula of Mindanao in the southwestern extremity of the Philippines. It is separated from Mindanao by the Basilan Strait on the northeast, the Celebes sea on the south, the Moro Gulf on the east, and the Sulu Sea on the west.

Basilan has a land area of 1,234.2 square kilometers. In 2007, it had a population of 408,520. At present, Basilan is composed of Basilan City and Isabela which is the nominal capital of the province plus nine other municipalities.

Following is a timeline1,2 of events that led to the creation and development of the province of Basilan:

T I M E

E V E N T
  • 1637

The Spaniards under Governor-General Sebastian Hurtado de Corcuera conquered Lamitan on Basilan Island.
  • 1844

The Spanish fortified the military garrison at Basilan under the supervision of governor of Zamboanga.
  • 1863

Basilan was separated from Zamboanga and became the independent Sixth District of Mindanao with its capital at “Isabela de Basilan.”
  • 1903

Basilan became part of the civil province of Zamboanga by Americans. Isabela, San Pedro, Santa Barbara, and Nipa Balas were the towns of Basilan at this time. Later, it was divided into Isabela, Lamitan, and Maluso.
  • July 1, 1948

By virtue of Republic Act 288, the entire island and its dependent islands of Basilan became a chartered city.
  • December 27, 1973

By virtue of Presidential Decree 356, Basilan restored the status of being a province.

At the same time, the poblacion of the municipality of Isabela became the City of Basilan, and the former municipal districts of Lamitan and Maluso became municipalities. Isabela municipality was made the provincial capital.
  • December 2, 1974

By virtue of Presidential Decree 593, the boundaries of City of Basilan, Isabela, Maluso and Lamitan municipalities were defined.

New municipalities of Lantawan, Sumipsip, Tipo-tipo, Tuburan, Tapiantana, Malamawi and Pilas.
  • December 11, 1975

Basilan City was abolished and annexed to Isabela municipality. Pilas was annexed to Lantawan and Tapiantana was combined with Sumipsip. Both Pilas have been reorganized as separate municipalities and additional municipality of Malamawi has been organized.

As of 2010, Basilan constitutes a lone district that includes the municipalities of Lamitan, Maluso, Sumisip, tipo-Tipo, Tuburan, and City of Isabela.
