HISTORY

LANAO DEL SUR


The following text/information are lifted from the book of Lee W. Vance entitled Tracing your Philippine Ancestors which was published in Provo, Utah by Stevenson’s Genealogical Center in 1980 and Symbols of the State by the Department of Interior and Local Government of the Republic of the Philippines in 1998.

 

The province of Lanao del Sur is located in southern Philippines in the northwestern part of Mindanao Island.  It entirely surrounds the basin of Lake Lanao, the second-largest lake in the country.  It is bounded on the north by its sister province, Lanao del Norte, by Bukidnon on the east, Maguindanao and North Cotabato on the south, and Illana Bay to the west.

The plateaus of Lanao had several primitive mountain tribes – the Dumagats, Manobos, Bagobos and Negritos – as its earliest inhabitants.  They were followed by a second wave of settlers who were Malaysians-derived Mohammedans, who moved inland from the coast to build an empire centered around the shores of Lake Lanao.  These were the Maranaos, whose former glory and power are dimly mirrored in their modern towns.  Although these Moros formerly dominated all of Mindanao and Sulu, Lanao del Sur is the last Philippine province in which these Muslims still comprise a majority.

Lanao formed a single province until its division into northern and southern provinces in 1959.  Therefore, Lanao del Sur shares its history up to that year with Lanao del Norte.

Following is a time line1, 2 of events that led to the creation and development of the province of Lanao del Sur:

T I M E

E V E N T
  • August 19, 1940

Today there are 36 municipalities and one chartered city, Marawi.  Marawi City was originally chartered as “Dansalan City”
  • October 17, 1946

The Municipality of Tubod was created by virtue of  REPUBLIC ACT 58
  • June 16, 1948

REPUBLIC ACT 283 amended R.A. 58

  • September 15, 1950

Dansalan City was inaugurated after a decade
  • 1956

Dansalan City was renamed Marawi City
  • May 10, 1956

REPUBLIC ACT 1415 is the act that created the municipality of Bacolod in the province of Lanao.

  • May 22, 1959

Lanao del Sur province came into existence with the approval of Republic Act No. 2228. The new province then comprised the municipality of Malabang, and the municipal districts of Pualas, Tatarikan, Tubaran, Binidayan, Bayang, Ganassi, Butig, Lumbatan, Masiu, Madamba, Madalum, Bacood-Grande, Tugaya, Balindong, Marantao, Saguiaran, Kapai, Wao, Ramain, Babong, Molundo, Taraka, Lumba-a-Bayabao, Poon-a-Bayabao, and Tamparan.
  • 1959

Marawi City was designated the provincial capital
  • June 19, 1960

REPUBLIC ACT 2883 amended subparagraphs 22 and 23 for the provinces of Lanao and Leyte, respectively, pages 142 and 143, Paragraph A, Title D, Section One of REPUBLIC ACT NO. 2093.
  • June 22, 1963

By virtue of REPUBLIC ACT 3624, the name of the Municipal District of Tatarikan was changed to Pagayawan.
  • June 19, 1965

By virtue of REPUBLIC ACT 4260, the name of Lake Lanao to Lake Sultan Alonto.
  • June 18, 1966

By virtue of REPUBLIC ACT 4790, the Municipality of Dianaton was created.
  • July 5, 1969

By virtue of REPUBLIC ACT 5978, the municipality of Balabagan in the province of Lanao Del Sur, was created.

  • June 17, 1972

REPUBLIC ACT 6434 repealed R.A. 4260, and restoring the old name of Lake Lanao in the province of Lanao Del Sur.

  • June 17, 1972

REPUBLIC ACT 6491 created the municipality of Piagapo.

  • February 8, 1982

BATAS PAMBANSA BILANG 168 was approved which created the municipality of Kapatagan.

  • 2010

Lanao del Sur has 2 districts. The 1st district is composed of the municipalities of Buadiposo-Buntong, Bubong, Bumbaran, Ditsaan-ramain, Kapai, Lumba-Bayabao, Maguing, Marantao, Masiu, Mulondo, Piagapo, Poona Bayabao (Gata), Saguiaran, Tagoloan, Tamparan Taraka, and Wao. The 2nd district is composed of the municipalities of Bacolod-Kalawi (Bacolod Grande), Balabagan, Balindong (Watu), Bayang, Binidayan, Butig, Calanogas, ganassi, Kapatagan, Lumbatan, Lumbayanague, Madalum, Madamba, Malabang, Marogong, Pagayawan (Tatarikan), Pualas, Sultan Dumalondong, Sultan Gumander, Tubaran, and Tugaya.
