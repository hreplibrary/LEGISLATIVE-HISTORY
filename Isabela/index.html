HISTORY

ISABELA
The following text/information are lifted from the book of Lee W. Vance entitled Tracing your Philippine Ancestors which was published in Provo, Utah by Stevenson’s Genealogical Center in 1980 and Symbols of the State by the Department of Interior and Local Government of the Republic of the Philippines in 1998.

The province of Isabela, considered the 2nd largest province on Luzon Island, is the most central of those provinces lying on the Pacific coast of northern Luzon in the northern Philippines. It is encircled by the provinces of Cagayan on the north; Kalinga Apayao, Mountain Province and Ifugao on the west; Nueva Vizcaya and Aurora (a sub-province of Quezon) to the south; while the vast Pacific Ocean sparkles beyond its long eastern shore.  

The early history of the territory now comprising Isabela is practically identical with that of Cagayan, of which it was formerly a part. It is believed that in the pre-Spanish period, this area was known to the Chinese and Japanese.

Isabela’s spiritual as well as civil conquest came from Cagayan to the north.

Isabela originally comprised three separate administrative districts of Cagayan province; Irraya (from Cabagan to Ilagan), Diffun (from Gamu to Cauayan), and Paniqui (from Cauayan to the Nueva Vizcaya border). (Velasco & Untivero, 1975)

The towns of Cabagan and Tumauini, together with a few other Rancherias or settlements, were taken from Cagayan, and the towns of Ilagan, Gamu, Angadanan, Camarag (now Echague), Carig and Palanan were detached from Nueva Vizcaya to form the new province, which was named after Queen Isabela II of Spain. The Comandancia of Saltan, formerly part of Nueva Vizcaya, was detached and made depend[e]nt upon Isabela province. (Galang, 1957 as cited in Vance, 1980).

The revolutionary forces of the Philippine Republic took Isabela without resistance from the Spanish in 1898, and set up a short-lived provincial government.

The Americans captured General Emilio Aguinaldo, president of the Republic, in Palanan, Isabela, concluding a long chase from Bulacan across the mountains in Northern Luzon.

A brief military government ruled the province until this date, when the Americans established a civil government for the province (Philippine Commission Act 210, enacted August 24, 1901, but effective Sept. 10, 1901)

Isabela has 4 districts. The 1st district is composed of the municipalities of Cabagan, Delfin Albano, Divilacan, Maconacon, Palanan, San Pablo, Santa Maria, Santo Tomas, and Tumauini, and the City of Ilagan. The 2nd district is composed of the municipalities of Aurora, Benito Soliven, Burgos, Gamu, Mallig, Naguilian, Quezon, Quirino, Roxas, San Manuel, and San Mariano. The 3rd district is composed of the municipalities of Alicia, Angadanan, Cabatuan, Luna, Reina Mercedes, San Guillermo, San Mateo, and Cauayan City. The 4th district is composed of the municipalities of Cordon, Dinapigue, Echague, Jones, Ramon, San Agustin, San Isidro, and Santiago City.
