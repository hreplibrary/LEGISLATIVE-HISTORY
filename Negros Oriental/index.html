HISTORY

NEGROS ORIENTAL

The following text/information are lifted from the book of Lee W. Vance entitled Tracing your Philippine Ancestors which was published in Provo, Utah by Stevenson’s Genealogical Center in 1980 and Symbols of the State by the Department of Interior and Local Government of the Republic of the Philippines in 1998.

The province of Negros Oriental occupies the eastern and southern portions of Negros Island in the central Visayan region of the Philippines.  A mountain range running along the island’s center separates Negros Oriental from its sister province to the north and west, Negros Occidental.  The rest of the province is bounded by the sea, with the Tanon Strait (with the island province of Cebu beyond it) to the east, and the Mindanao Sea to the south.  Until 1971, the island of Siquijor, off its southeastern coast, formed a sub-province of Negros Oriental.

Negros Island was formerly called “Buglas”, but when the early Spaniards first explored it, they found many Negrito settlements.  Hence, they renamed it the island of “Negros”.
Although modern Occidental and Oriental Negros share a common island, their administration and early history was separate; the western half of the island originally having been administered from Iloilo (Arevalo) and the eastern half from Cebu.  Consequently, their histories are treated separately.

Following is a time line 1,2 of events that led to the creation and development of the province of Negros Oriental:


DATE

EVENT
  • 1521

The earliest westerners set foot on Negros and some of Magellan’s expedition were sent there to explore the island
  • 1575

Early missions were established in Binalbagan
  • 1580

first parish was founded at Tanjay, and included (in addition to that community) the towns of Dumaguete, Siaton, Marabago (now Bacong) and Manalongon
  • 1584

Early missions were established in Ilog
  • 1628

the Jesuits took over the Augustinians
  • 1734

Earlier, Negros Occidental originally fell under the provincial jurisdiction of Iloilo (or Arevalo).  But in 1734, it was combined with the eastern half of Negros to form a single politico-military comandancia, or district.  The capital of Negros was originally at Ilog, but was moved almost immediately to Himamaylan
  • 1768

expulsion of the Jesuits from the Philippines, the missions and parishes were turned over to the secular clergy
  • 1848

the Recollects took over their care

  • 1849

The capital of Negros was transferred to Bacolod
  • 1856

Negros Island’s status was elevated to that of a province, when a heavy influx of settlers (mainly from Panay) arrived to work in the newly flourishing sugar industry in Negros
  • 1890

Negros Province was split to form the two politico-military provinces of Negros Occidental and Negros Oriental. At this time, the province of Negros Oriental comprised the towns of Amlan, Ayungon, Ayuquita, Bacong, Bais, Bayanan (Bayawan), Canoan, Dauin, Dumaguete, Guihulngan, Manjuyod, New Valencia, Siaton, Tanjay, Tayasan, Tolon and Zamboangita

  • 1896

outbreak of the Philippine Revolution, local revolutionary groups were organized.
  • November 1898

The Spanish surrendered. .  A provisional government was set up immediately for the province.  But its leaders, including Governor Juan Araneta, a former war hero, soon decided to reunite the two provinces in Negros to form the new “Estado Cantonal de Negros”, or “Regional State of Negros”.
  • December 19, 1898

A general election was held on the island to elect representatives to the legislative assembly.
  • March 1899

the arrival of U.S. troops
  • March 4, 1899

Bacolod was surrendered to the Americans
  • April 20, 1901

By virtue of ACT 120, the provisions of the provincial government act and the municipal code to the province of Oriental Negros were extended.

  • April 20, 1901

Civil government was restored. Negros was re-divided to form two separate provinces again, as under the last decade of Spanish rule.  Negros Oriental’s capital was at Dumaguete, as before under the Spanish by virtue of Philippine Commission Act 120.
  • April 2, 1903

By virtue of ACT 715, the 24 municipalities of the province of Oriental Negros were reduced to 14.
  • October 8, 1907

Suiquijor Island, previously a part of Bohol province under Spanish rule, was annexed as a sub-province to Negros Oriental. Its seat was at Canoan, now part of modern Larena by virtue of Philippine Commission Act 1753.
  • February 28, 1914

By virtue of ACT NO. 2354, the office of lieutenant-governor was made elective in the subprovince of Oriental Negros …,  and for other purposes
  • March 6, 1924

By virtue of ACT NO. 3143, the subprovince of Siquijor was granted the right to elect a member to represent the interests of the subprovince in the provincial board of Oriental Negros, and for other purposes.
  • November 13, 1931

By virtue of ACT No.  3863, the name of the municipality of Talingting, subprovince of Siquijor, province of Oriental Negros, was changed to that of Enrique Villanueva
  • May 11, 1940

By virtue of COMMONWEALTH ACT NO. 531, the name of the barrio of Maloconan, municipality of Tolong, province of Oriental Negros, was changed to that of San Francisco
  • June 14, 1948

By virtue of Republic Act 252, the name of the municipality of Luzurriaga, province of Negros Oriental, was changed to that of "Valencia"
  • May 9, 1955

By virtue of Republic Act 1212, the municipality of San Jose, province of Negros Oriental was created
  • June 16, 1955

By virtue of Republic Act 1303, the barrios of Kalumboyan and Villareal in the municipality of Bayawan, province of Oriental Negros were created
  • June 21, 1959

By virtue of REPUBLIC ACT 2496, the municipality of Mabinay in the province of Negros Oriental was created
  • June 21, 1959

By virtue of REPUBLIC ACT 2498, the barrios of Luz, Linantuyan and Hilaitan in the municipality of Guihulungan, province of Negros Oriental were created
  • June 21, 1959

By virtue of REPUBLIC ACT 2526, the name of the municipality of Payabon, province of Negros Oriental, was changed to municipality of Bindoy
  • June 19, 1960

By virtue of REPUBLIC ACT 2839, the barrio of Bugay in the municipality of Bayawan, province of Negros Oriental was created
  • June 19, 1960

REPUBLIC ACT 2882 amended item (13) on page 151, subparagraph 38 for the province of Negros Oriental, under paragraph C, title A of section 1 of REPUBLIC ACT 2301

By virtue of Republic Act 5421, the municipality of Basay in the province of Negros Oriental was created
  • June 21, 1969

By virtue of Republic Act 5568, the name Barrio Sampong in the municipality  of Bayawan, province of Negros Oriental, was changed to Barrio San Roque
  • September 17, 1971

Siquijor was separated from Negros Oriental and made a regular province, with its capital at Larena by virtue of Republic Act 6398
  • September 12, 1972

The provincial capital was transferred from Larena to Siquijor municipality
  • February 9, 1983

By virtue of BATAS PAMBANSA BLG. 336, the municipality of Salvador Benedicto in the province of Negros Occidental was created
  • 2010

Negros Oriental has 3 districts. The 1st district is composed of Ayungon, Bindoy (Payabon), Guihulngan, Jimalalud, La Libertad, Manjuyod, Tayasan, Vallehermoso, and Canlaon City. The 2nd district is composed of the municipalities of Amlan, Mabinay, Pamplona, San Jose, and Sibulan, Dumaguete City and Tanjay City. The third district is composed of the municipalities of Bacong, Basay, Dauin, Santa Catalina, Siaton, Valencia, Zamboanguita, and Bayawan City.
