HISTORY

AGUSAN DEL NORTE

The following text/information are lifted from the book of Lee W. Vance entitled Tracing your Philippine Ancestors which was published in Provo, Utah by Stevenson’s Genealogical Center in 1980 and Symbols of the State by the Department of Interior and Local Government of the Republic of the Philippines in 1998.

 

Agusan del Norte is located in the southern portion of the Philippine Archipelago, in the northeast part of the island of Mindanao. It is bounded on the north by Butuan Bay and Surigao del Norte, on the east by Surigao del Sur and Agusan del Sur, on the south by Agusan del Sur, and on the west by Misamis Oriental and Butuan Bay.  The center of economic activity and government services is the capital city of Butuan.

Agusan del Norte has a land area of 2,730.24 square kilometers. In 2007, it had a population of 314,027.

Following is a timeline1,2 of events that led to the creation and development of the province of Agusan del Norte:

DATE

EVENT
  • Prior to 1521

When the Europeans first arrived in Agusan, portions of the province were already inhabited by natives trading with the Chinese and Portuguese.

  • 1521

Masao (Butuan), the early capital of Agusan area, was believed the site of the first mass in the Philippines by the Magellan expedition.
  • 1538

Magellan’s expedition was followed by Portuguese explorer, De Castro.
  • 1543

De Castro’s expedition in the area was followed by Villalobos.
  • 1565

Legazpi’s expedition in the area.
  • 1596

The first permanent missions were established by Jesuit missionaries who arrived in Butuan.
  • 1620

The Recollects established missions along the upper portions of the Agusan River in the interior of the area at Linao, now called Bunawan, Agusan del Sur.

  • 1622

Jesuits, who established missions along the banks of the lower Agusan River, were replaced by the Augustinian Recollects.
  • 1640

Recollect missions were raided by Moros.
  • 1753

Recollect missions were raided by Moros again.
  • 1860

With the establishment of a politico-military government in Mindanao, Agusan del Norte, combined with Agusan del Sur, Surigao del Norte, Surigao del Sur and part of Davao del Norte and Davao Oriental, comprised the East District of Mindanao. This district extended from Butuan Bay in the northwest to Caraga Bay, in modern Davao Oriental, in the southeast.  It included those inland settlements under Spanish influence, along an indefinite and slowly progressing boundary which moved as Spanish colonization proceeded slowly inland from the coast. 

  • 1870

The combination of Mindanao, Agusan del Norte with Agusan del Sur, Surigao del Norte, Surigao del Sur and part of Davao del Norte and Davao Oriental was called the district of Surigao.
  • 1875

Jesuit missionaries returned to Agusan.
  • 1898

Agusan had been formed into a politico-military commandancia under the administrative jurisdiction of the province of Surigao and was officially named Butuan.
  • 1901

Agusan including the territory of modern provinces of Agusan del Norte and Agusan del Sur was made subprovince of Surigao.
  • 1907

By virtue of ACT 1693, the province of Agusan was created with Butuan and Bukidnon as sub-provinces. Butuan sub-province included the rest of the territory of Agusan. But in the ACT, it does not designate the municipalities taken from Misamis or Surigao, the former Spanish pueblos listed hereafter lay within the territory described:  Butuan, Nasipit, Cabadbaran, Tolosa, Jabonga, Tubay, La Paz, Talacagon and Verbena.  These were combined to form three municipalities – Butuan, Cabarbaran and Talacagon –by annexing Nasipit to Butuan; Tolosa, Jabonga and Tubay to Cabarbaran (Cabadbaran); and Verbena and La Paz to Talacogon.

  • September, 1914

By virtue of Act 2408, Agusan was designated as one of the seven special provinces under the Department of Mindanao and Sulu comprising modern Agusan del Sur and Agusan del Norte in one province. Its capital continued to be Butuan.

  • 1935

Agusan province constituted five municipalities and 49 municipal districts including Butuan, Cabadbaran, Jabonga, Nasipit and Talacogon; municipal districts: Axpitia, Bahbah, Balete, Baquingquing,  Basa, Baylo, Borbon, Bunaguit,  Banawan, Concordia, Corinto, Cuevas, Ebro, Esperanza, Gracia, Guadalupe, Halapitan, Langasian, La Paz, Las Nieves, Libertad, Loreto, Los Arcos, Maasin, Mambalili, Mampinsahan, Maygatasan, Milagros, Novele, Nuevo Sibagat, Nuevo Trabajo, Patrocinio, Prosperidad, Remedios, Rosario,  Sagunto, Salvacion, San Ignacio, San Luis, San Isidro, Santa Ines, Santa Josefa, Santo Tomas, Trento, Tudela, Verdu, Verbena, Violanta, Waloe.
  • 1947

Agusan it had been made a “semi-regular” province, and the two additional municipalities of Buenavista and Tubay had been created, and Azpitia municipal district disorganized. 

  • June 21, 1959

By virtue of Republic Act (RA) 2517, the municipality of Bunawan was created.
By virtue of RA 2518, the municipality of San Francisco was created.

  • June 18, 1960

The municipality of Prosperidad was created.
  • June 17, 1967

The passage of RA 4979 dividing Agusan into the two provinces of Agusan Del Norte and Agusan Del Sur.

  • January 8, 1968

RA 4979 was proclaimed. At this time, Agusan del Norte was comprised of the municipalities of Buenavista, Cabadbaran, Carmen, Jabonga, Kitcharao, Las Nieves, Nasipit, Tubay, and the capital at Butuan City.  Since then the two municipalities of Magallanes and Santiago have been organized.

  • As of 2010

Agusan del Norte constitutes two legislative districts. Listed are the municipalities under each district:
1st District: Municipalities of Nieves, and Butuan City.
2nd District: Municipalities of Buenavista, Cabadbaran, Carmen, Jabonga, Kitcharao, Magallanes, Nasipit, Remedios T. Romualdez, Santiago, and Tubay.
