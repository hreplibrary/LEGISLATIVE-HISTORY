HISTORY

ORIENTAL MINDORO
The following text/information are lifted from the book of Lee W. Vance entitled Tracing your Philippine Ancestors which was published in Provo, Utah by Stevenson’s Genealogical Center in 1980 and Symbols of the State by the Department of Interior and Local Government of the Republic of the Philippines in 1998.

The province of Oriental Mindoro occupies the eastern portion of Mindoro Island, located just slightly northwest of the center of the Philippine Archipelago, and just south of the island of Luzon.  It is bounded on the east by its sister province, Occidental Mindoro, and the waters off is coasts are the Verde Island Passage to the north, and the Mindoro Strait to the east.

Following is a timeline1,2 of events that led to the creation and development of the province of Oriental Mindoro:

DATE

EVENT
  • Prior to 1950

Occidental and Oriental Mindoro formed the single province of Mindoro, so their history is the same.

  • 1570

When Juan de Salcedo and Martin de Goiti first explored the island while enroute to Manila, they found two trading junks loaded with general merchandise anchored in Baco River. The Spaniards must have felt they had made a rich find by discovering Mindoro, for they called it “Mina de Oro”, or “gold mine”, which later was shortened to Mindoro.  Its previous name had been “Mait”.
  • 1571

Don Miguel Lopez de Legazpi, then in the process of transferring his national capital from Panay to Manila, stopped at the northern coast of Mindoro, and succeeded in bringing the people under Spanish sovereignty.
  • 1572

Christianity first came to Mindoro via the Augustinians who first arrived and opened a mission at Baco. But the natives revolted to them and imprisoned the friars, who were soon after forced to leave. 

  • 1578

Two Franciscans were assigned to the province of Balayan and Mindoro.
  • 1580

Jesuits were assigned to the province
  • 1581

Mindoro became part of the territorial jurisdiction of “Balayan” (or “Bombon”) province, now known as Batangas province, when it was created
  • Early 17th Century

Mindoro was separated from Balayan (Batangas) and made into a corrigimiento with Puerto Galera as its capital.  Its territory then included Mindoro and its small dependent islands, and also Marinduque, Ylin, and the Lubang Islands. 

  • 17th and 18th Centuries

Mindoro’s history at this time was one of nearly continuous struggle against Moro pirates, who pillaged and burned entire towns, and murdered or enslaved its inhabitants.  The Moros established strongholds at Mamburao and Balete (abandoned by their original inhabitant s) from which they sailed forth to attack the settlements of Mindoro, Luzon and the Visayas, razing entire districts and enslaving those captives who were not put to the sword.
  • 1673

Recollects were assigned to the province. The early centers of missionary activity were established in Baco, Calavite, Naujan, Abra de Ilog, Mangarin and Calapan.
  • 1778

A successful expedition led by Don Jose Gomez captured the Moro stronghold at Mamburao, effectively eliminating Moro control over the island.  The grateful natives, gradually forgetting their fears of the conquered Moros, returned to the abandoned settlements along Mindoro’s coasts.
  • End of the 18th Century

The Spanish government, realizing at the last the imperative necessity of more effective methods, began a determined campaign against the Moro pirates of Mindoro. 

  • Probably in the 19th Century

The Lubang Islands were annexed to Cavite province.
  • 1837

The capital was transferred from Puerto Galera to Calapan
  • 1839

Mindoro had already been made a politico-military province.
  • 1899

Mindoro remained nominally under the Philippine Revolutionary government after the evacuation of the Spaniards
  • August 1901

The American troops landed in Calapan and occupied the island.
  • June 23, 1902

Civil government was extended to Mindoro when it, together with the Lubang Islands (separated from Cavite), was annexed to Marinduque province by virtue of Philippine Commission Act 423
  • April 28, 1904

Act 1131 made the Governor of the Province of Mindoro a Justice of Peace throughout the whole of that province.

On the same day, the municipality of Bongabon was incorporated as a barrio of the municipality of Pinamalayan through Act 1135
  • November 22, 1902

Mindoro was organized as a separate “regular” province from Marinduque.  It included the Lubang Islands within its jurisdiction, and its provincial capital was transferred back to Puerto Galera from Calapan by virtue of Philippine Commission Act 500.
  • April 28, 1904

Bongabon was annexed to Pinamalayan by virtue of Philippine Commission Act 1135
  • January 4, 1905

The remaining fifteen municipalities of Mindoro were reduced to eight by consolidating Looc with Lubang, Palauan and Abra de Ilog with Mamburao, Puerto Galera with Calapan, Pola with Pinamalayan, and Mansalay and Mangarin with Bulalacao by virtue of Philippine Commission Act 1280
  • September 14, 1905

Mindoro’s status was changed from a regular province to a “special” province, and the capital was again transferred back to Calapan by virtue of Philippine Commission Act 1396. On the same day, the former municipalities were changed in status to townships and Rancherias or settlements by virtue of Philippine Commission Act 1397.
  • July 2, 1907

The island of Maestre de Campo was annexed to Mindoro province from Romblon by virtue of Philippine Commission Act 1665
  • 1919

the townships were restored to their status as municipalities and municipal districts by virtue of Philippine Legislature Act 1824
  • March 5, 1919

ACT NO. 2824 extended to the provinces of Mindoro the provisions of Chapters 63 and 64 of the Administrative Code of 1917 …
  • February 20, 1921

By virtue of Philippine Legislature Act 2964, the evolution of Mindoro province was completed when it was restored as a “regular” province, with its capital remaining at Calapan
  • March 6, 1924

The municipality of Concepcion, on Maestre de Campo Island, was annexed to Mindoro from Romblon by virtue of Philippine Legislature Act 3131
  • March 6, 1924

The segregation of the municipality of Concepcion, on the island of Maestre de Campo, from the province of Romblon, and its annexation to the province of Mindoro was authorized by virtue of ACT NO. 3131
  • December 7, 1927

By virtue of ACT No.  3415, the districts of Bongabong and Puerto Galera, province of Mindoro, were reorganized as municipalities
  • December 8, 1928

By virtue of ACT NO.  3498, the municipality of Bulalacao and the municipal district of Baco, in the province of Mindoro was reorganized.
  • September 8, 1936

COMMONWEALTH ACT NO. 28, the name of the Barrio of Payompon, municipality of Sablayan, province of Mindoro, was changed to that of Buenavista.
  • September 8, 1936

COMMONWEALTH ACT NO.29, the name of the barrio of Gumalpong, municipality of Baco, province of Mindoro, was changed to that of Santa Rosa.

  • 1939

The whole island has been under the care of the Divine World Fathers (SVD).

  • June 8, 1940
 By virtue of CA 573, the name of Barrio Macaucang in the Municipality of Naujan was changed
  • June 13, 1950

The separation of the eastern and western halves of Mindoro and their organization into the two provinces of Oriental and Occidental Mindoro, occurred when Republic Act 505 was approved. At that time, the province of Oriental Mindoro consisted of the municipalities of Baco. Bungabo, Bulalacao, Calapan (the capital), Mansalay, Naujan, Pinamalayan, Pola, Puerto Galera, Roxas and San Teodoro.  The province of Occidental Mindoro consisted of the municipalities of Abra de Ilog, Looc, Lubang, Mamburao (the capital), Paluan, Sablayan, San Jose and Santa Cruz.

  • June 21, 1957

By virtue of REPUBLIC ACT 1704, certain barrios in the municipality of Mansalay, province of Oriental Mindoro were created.
  • June 21, 1957

By virtue of REPUBLIC ACT 1727, the names of certain barrios in the municipality of Sablayan, province of Occidental Mindoro were changed.
  • June 21, 1959

By virtue of REPUBLIC ACT 2514, the municipality of Bansud in the province of Oriental Mindoro was created
  • June 19, 1960

By virtue of REPUBLIC ACT 2788, the name of the municipality of Bulalacao in the province of Oriental Mindoro was changed to San Pedro
  • June 19, 1960

Certain barrios in the municipality of Naujan, province of Oriental Mindoro were created by virtue of REPUBLIC ACT 2828
  • June 18, 1961

By virtue of REPUBLIC ACT 3356, the barrio of San Antonio in the municipality of Puerto Galera, province of Oriental Mindoro was created
  • June 22, 1963

By virtue of REPUBLIC ACT 3609, the municipality of Socorro in the province of Oriental Mindoro was created
  • June 9, 1966

By virtue of REPUBLIC ACT 4651, the municipality of Gloria in the province of Oriental Mindoro was created
  • June 21, 1969

By virtue of Republic Act 5570, the name  of  the  barrio  Bulbulan  in  the municipality of Gloria, province  of  Oriental  Mindoro,  was changed to Sta. Maria.
  • June 21, 1969

By virtue of Republic Act 5571, the name of barrio Lubag in the municipality of Gloria, province of Oriental Mindoro, was changed to Barrio Macario Adriatico.
  • June 21, 1969

By virtue of Republic Act 5572, the name of barrio  Calamundingan  in  the municipality of Gloria, province  of  Oriental Mindoro was changed to Barrio Lucio Laurel.

The name of Barrio Bating an  in  the municipality of Gloria, province  of  Oriental Mindoro was changed to Barrio Andres Bonifacio by virtue of REPUBLIC ACT 5573.

An act changing the name of Barrio Langang in the municipality of Gloria, province  of  Oriental Mindoro,  to Barrio Santa Theresa by virtue of REPUBLIC ACT 5576.

An act changing the name of Barrio Dalagan in  the municipality of Gloria, province  of  Oriental  Mindoro,  to Barrio San Antonio by virtue of REPUBLIC ACT 5577.
  • June 21, 1969

An act changing the name of the municipality of San Pedro in the province of Oriental Mindoro by virtue of REPUBLIC ACT 5646.
  • 2013

At present, the province is composed of 2 districts. The 1st district is composed of the municipalities of Baco, Naujan, Pola, Puerto Galera, San Teodoro, Socorro, Victoria, and City of Calapan. The 2nd district is composed of the municipalities of Bansud, Bongabong, Bulalacao, Gloria, Mansalay, Pinamalayan, and Roxas
