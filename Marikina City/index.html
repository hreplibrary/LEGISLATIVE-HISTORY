HISTORY

MARIKINA CITY

The following text/information are lifted from the book of Lee W. Vance entitled Tracing your Philippine Ancestors which was published in Provo, Utah by Stevenson’s Genealogical Center in 1980 and Symbols of the State by the Department of Interior and Local Government of the Republic of the Philippines in 1998.

 

Marikina City is located on the north-east of Metro Manila. It is bounded on the north by the Municipality of San Mateo, Rizal on the east by the City of Antipolo, on the south by the City of Pasig and on the west by the City of Quezon.

Marikina City has a land area of 21.50 square kilometers. In 2007, it had a population of 424,610.

Following is a timeline1, 2 of events that led to the creation and development of Marikina City:

DATE

E V E N T
  • 13th Century

Ancient settlements were already thriving in Paranaque, Pasig, Cainta and Taytay.  These settlements were believed to be known to Chinese, Japanese and Indian traders.
  • 1570

The Augustinians arrived at the valley, particularly in Chorillo, Barangay Barangka.
  • 1571

Juan de Salcedo visited Taytay and Cainta and brought these towns under Spanish sovereignty.
  • 1630

The town was established by the followers of St. Ignatius of Loyola, the Jesuit Orders, who blazed the trail in the quest for spiritual transformation among the country’s inhabitants. The town was known as Jesus dela Pena in honor of the Infant Child Jesus.

The first Catholic mass was celebrated in the town at Barrio Jesus dela Pena.
  • April 16, 1630

The ecclesiastical control and supervision of Marikina area were transferred to the Jesuits by Fray Pedro de Arce.
  • 1639

A Chinese uprising caused great rioting throughout Rizal.  The Chinese burned the churches of Taytay, San Mateo and Pasig. The Spaniards were able to quell the uprising but not without great loss of life and property.
  • 1762

British troops under the command of Captain Thomas Backhouse invaded Pasig, defeated the Spaniards and occupied Taytay and Cainta.
  • 1764

The British withdrew from the islands.
  • 1787

A municipality status was granted to the town under the Province of Rizal with Don Benito Mendoza as its first Gobernadorcillo.

The area was called Mariquina then.
  • 1815

The town of Pateros was founded.
  • 1853

“Distrito de los Montes de San Mateo”, or District of the San Mateo Mountains was formed.  Cainta, Taytay, Antipolo and Bosoboso were annexed to this new district from Tondo province.  Angono, Binangonan, Cardona, Morong, Baras, Tanay, Pililla and Jalajala were annexed to the district from Laguna province.  Morong was made the capital of the District.
  • 1859

The name Distrito de Los Montes de San Mateo was changed to Morong District.  At about the same time, Tondo province was renamed Manila province.  At about this time, Navotas was founded.
  • 1898

American military forces controlled the district.
  • 1898-1899

Mariquina became the capital of the province of Manila when the Philippines achieved its independence.
  • June 11, 1901

The province of Rizal was created by virtue of ACT No. 137 by the First Philippine Commission which during the time was acting as the unicameral legislative body in the island of Luzon.

  • 1903

Rizal’s various municipalities were combined as follows - Montalban with San Mateo; Taguig and Muntinglupa with Pateros; Malibay with Pasay; Las Pinas with Paranaque, Novaliches with Caloocan; Navotas with Malabon, San Felipe Neri (Mandaluyong) with San Juan del Monte, Cainta and Angono with Taytay; Bosoboso and Teresa with Antipolo, Binangonan, Baras and cardona with Morong; and Quisao and Jalajala with Pililla. 
A month later Binangonan was separated from Morong and Angono was annexed to it.  At the same time, Muntinglupa was separated from Pateros, and annexed as a barrio to Binan, Laguna and later annexed (still as a barrio) to Taguig, itself newly formed from the former pueblos of Pateros, Taguig and Muntinglupa.
  • 1910

The town was officially named Marikina by the first Philippine Commissioner Trinidad Pardo de Tavera with the hispanized q giving way to a more vernacular k.
  • November 7,1975

By virtue of Presidential Decree No. 824, Metropolitan Manila Area was established and Marikina became one of the seventeen (17) component cities and municipalities.

  • November 26, 1996

By virtue of Republic Act (RA) No. 8223, the municipality of Marikina was converted into a highly urbanized city to be known as the City of Marikina.
  • December 15, 2006

By virtue of RA 9364, the amendment of Section 10 and 53 of RA 8223 otherwise known as the Charter City of Marikina was approved. 

  • April 10, 2007
 Barangays Fortune and Tumana were created by virtue Republic Acts 9431 and 9432, respectively.
  • May, 2007

By virtue of RA 9364, Marikina City was divided into 2 congressional districts serving 2 representatives.
  • As of 2010

Marikina City constitutes two legislative districts. Listed below are the barangays of each district:
1st District: Barangays of Barangka, Calumpang, Jesus dela Peña, Malanday, San Roque, Sta. Elena, Santo Niño, Tañong, and Industrial Valley.
2nd District: Barangays Nagka, Parang, Concepcion Uno, Concepcion Dos and Marikina Heights.
