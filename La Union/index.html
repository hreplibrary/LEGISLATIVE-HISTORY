HISTORY

LA UNION
The following text/information are lifted from the book of Lee W. Vance entitled Tracing your Philippine Ancestors which was published in Provo, Utah by Stevenson’s Genealogical Center in 1980 and Symbols of the State by the Department of Interior and Local Government of the Republic of the Philippines in 1998.

The province of La Union is located in the western coast of north-central Luzon in the northern Philippines.  It is bounded on the north by Ilocos Sur, on the east by Benguet, on the south by Pangasinan, and on the west it faces the sparkling South China Sea.

Prior to the arrival of the Spanish, the La Union area was already settled, and was apparently known to Chinese and Japanese traders.  Purao (later renamed Balaoan), Bauang and Agoo were thriving town.

Following is a timeline1,2 of events that led to the creation and development of the province of La Union:

Augustinian missionaries first arrived in La Union

Spanish conquistador Juan de Salcedo was the first to explore La Union where he met strong resistance from the natives at Purao (now Balaoan).

  • Prior to 1854

Ilocos Sur extended southward down to Balaoan, and Pangasinan extended northward up to Bacnotan.  Pangasinan ceded its northern towns of San Juan, San Fernando, Bauang, Naguilian, Aringay, Agoo, Bacnotan and Santo Tomas [Philippine Commission Act 935 (Oct 9, 1903), amended by Phil. Comm. Act 1003 (Nov. 21, 1903)] to comprise the southern towns of La Union at the time of its creation.  Ilocos Sur gave up Bangar, Balaoan and Namacpacan (now Luna) to form La Union’s northern towns

  • April 11, 1854

The province of La Union was created, by uniting (hence the name) part of Ilocos Sur with the northern part of Pangasinan. 

  • May 1898

During the second part of the Philippine Revolution, La Union fell to the insurgents when they occupied San Fernando
  • November 19, 1899

The Americans displaced the short-lived revolutionary provincial government when they occupied the province after winning the battle at Aringay river.
  • August 15, 1901

The American military rule of the province was replaced by a civil provincial government established by the Americans (Philippine Commission Act 203, enacted Aug 165, 1901)
  • 1902

La Union ceded a narrow strip of territory comprising modern Sudipen, Santol and San Gabriel municipalities to Amburayan sub-province of Lepanto-Bontoc province
  • October 9, 1903

the fifteen municipalities of La union were reduced to twelve by combining Caba and Galinao with Aringay, and Rosario with Santo Tomas (Philippine Commission Act 935 (Oct 9, 1903), amended by Phil. Comm Act 1003 (Nov 21, 1903). These twelve towns have grown and increased to the present total of twenty municipalities, with their capital at San Fernando.
  • October 18, 1906

By virtue of ACT 1543, the Municipality of Namacpacan was changed to Luna.
  • 1908

This was transferred to the jurisdiction of Mountain Province when it was created, and remained with it until Amburayan sub-province was abolished
  • February 4, 1920

Its territory divided between La Union and Ilocos Sur
  • December 3, 1927

By virtue of ACT  3386, the municipal districts of Sudipen, Santol, San Gabriel, and Pugo were organized into regular municipalities.
  • As of 2013

La Union has 2 districts. The 1st district is composed of the municipalities of Bacnotan, Balaoan, Bangar, Luna, Santol, San Gabriel, San Juan, Sudipen, and San Fernando City. The 2nd district is composed of the municipalities of Agoo, Aringay, Bagulin, Bauang, Burgos, Caba, Naguilian, Pugo, Rosario, Santo Tomas, and Tubao.
