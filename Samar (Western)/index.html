HISTORY

SAMAR


The following text/information are lifted from the book of Lee W. Vance entitled Tracing your Philippine Ancestors which was published in Provo, Utah by Stevenson’s Genealogical Center in 1980 and Symbols of the State by the Department of Interior and Local Government of the Republic of the Philippines in 1998.

The province of Samar occupies the southwestern part of Samar Island, which lies in the eastern extremity of the central Philippines.  It is bounded on the north by Northern Samar, on the east by Eastern Samar, on the south by Leyte Gulf, and on the west by the Samar Sea.  The island of Samar on which the province is located is the third-largest island of the Philippine Archipelago.

Samar province shares its early history with the neighboring provinces of Eastern Samar and Northern Samar.

Following is a time line 1,2 of events that led to the creation and development of the province of Samar:

DATE

E V E N T
  • March 15, 1521

Ferdinand Magellan sighted the island of Samar, which was then inhabited by “Zamal” natives.  This gives Samar the distinction of being the first island in the country to be discovered by the Spaniards.
  • 1596 - 1729

Jesuit missionaries established missions and parishes in Catbalogan (1596), Palapag (before 1617), Capul, Loaong and Paranas (before 1629), Catubig, Pambujan, Catarman and Bobon (before 1649); and Gandara (1729)
  • Early Spanish period – 1735

Samar or Ibabao as it was known, was under the jurisdiction of Cebu together with Leyte or Tendaya.
  • 1735

Leyte and Samar were combined into one provinc with its capital at Carigara Leyte.
  • 1768

Samar was separated from Leyte and organized as a province, with its capital at Catbalogan.

  • 1785

The Franciscans established a parish at Calbayog.   The Jesuits were expelled from the island in 1768 so the parishes remained under Franciscan care until 1941 when they were turned over to the secular clergy.

  • 1860 - 1898

Samar was given the status of politico-military province.

  • December  10, 1898

During the Philippine Revolution, a provincial government was set up by the revolutionary forces under General Lukban.

  • January 27, 1900

Nine American warships  arrived at Maqueda Bay in Catbalogan.  The Filipino forces, instead of surrendering after the 12-hour American ultimatum, set Catbalogan on fire and fled to the hills. 

  • June 17, 1902

The revolutionary forces were subdued, and the Americans established a civil government for Samar.
  • June 19, 1965

Pursuant to REPUBLIC ACT 4221, dated June 19, 1965, Samar  was divided into Easter, Western and Northern Samar. Western Samar consisted of the municipalities of Catbalogan (the capital), Basey, Calbiga, Daram, Gandara, Santa Margarita, Hinabangan, Jiabong, Marabut, Motiong, Pinacdao, Santa Rita, Talalora, Tarangan, Villareal, Wrigh, Zumarraga, Matuginao, San Jose de Buan, San Sebastian, Almagro and Santo Nino.

  • June 21, 1965

“Western Samar” was renamed “Samar” on June 21, 1969 by virtue of R.A. 5650 dated, June 21, 1969.

  • 2010

Samar province now consists of the municipalities of Almagro, Gandara, Matuguinao, Pagsanghan, San Jorge, Santa Margarita, Santo Niño, , Tagapul-an, Basey, Calbiga, Catbalogan, Daram, Hinabangan, Jiabong, Marabut, Motiong,Paranas, Pinabacdao, San Jose de Buan, San Sebastian, Santa Rita, Talalora, Villareal, Zumarraga and the City of Calbayog.
