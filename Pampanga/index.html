HISTORY

PAMPANGA
The following text/information are lifted from the book of Lee W. Vance entitled Tracing your Philippine Ancestors which was published in Provo, Utah by Stevenson’s Genealogical Center in 1980 and Symbols of the State by the Department of Interior and Local Government of the Republic of the Philippines in 1998.

The province of Pampanga lies in south-central Luzon amid a flat plain.  It borders Manila Bay to the south, Bataan and Zambales to the west, Tarlac and Nueva Ecija to the north, and Bulacan to the southeast.

The very heart of Luzon’s Philippine civilization in pre-Spanish days seems to have been the Pampanga region.  Manufacturing and commerce had been carried on for centuries by the Pampangans, prior to the arrival of the Spanish, and the region was probably well known to Chinese, Japanese and Hindu merchants.  The inhabitants of the region were fearless and independent, and did not take kindly to the Spaniards.  Among the major settlements of that day were Bacolor, Macabebe, Arayat, Betis, Lubao and Candaba.

  • 2013

Pampanga has 4 districts. The 1st district is composed of the municipalities of Mabalacat City, Magalang, and Angeles City. The 2nd district is composed of the municipalities of Floridablanca, Guagua, Lubao, Porac, Santa Rita, and Sexmoan. The 3rd district is composed of the municipalities of Arayat, Bacoor, Mexico, Santa Ana, and City of San Fernando. The 4th district is composed of the following municipalities: Apalit, Candaba, Macabebe, Masantol, Minalin, San Luis, San Simon, and Santo Tomas.
