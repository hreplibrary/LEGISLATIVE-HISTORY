HISTORY

KALINGA
The following text/information are lifted from the book of Lee W. Vance entitled Tracing your Philippine Ancestors which was published in Provo, Utah by Stevenson’s Genealogical Center in 1980 and Symbols of the State by the Department of Interior and Local Government of the Republic of the Philippines in 1998.

The province of Kalinga lies between the Ilocos provinces and the Cagayan Valley in the northernmost part of Luzon Island.  It is bounded on the north by a narrow strip of Cagayan, on the east by Cagayan and Isabela, on the south by Mountain Province, and on the west by Abra and Ilocos Norte.

The mountainous territory of Kalinga was so inaccessible that it remained largely uncivilized and undeveloped in Spanish days.  It was formerly part of the ancient province of Nueva Segovia (Cagayan), which included nearly all of the northeastern Luzon.

Following is a timeline1,2 of events that led to the creation and development of the province of Kalinga:

DATE

EVENT
  • 1610

A mission was established by the Dominicans at Futul (Pudtol, Apayao) to proselyte the Isnegs (Itnegs), often called “Mandayas” by the Spaniards.
  • 1612

A mission was established by the Dominicans at Capinatan.

  • 1688

The mission of San Jose de Traga was founded 16 miles up the Chico River from Tuao, in Kalinga.  But these missions and the others following them generally lasted only for a brief period due to the hostile environment and inhabitants.

  • 1839

The territory roughly equivalent to modern Kalinga was annexed to the newly created province of Nueva Vizcaya remaining with Cagayan province.
  • 1880

The Spanish authorities began a concerted effort to subjugate and Christianize the peoples of the inner mountainous regions of Luzon.
  • 1889

As part of this plan, politico-military comandancias were established in Kalinga under the name of “Itaves”, with O’Dona as its capital
  • 1890

Comandancias were established in Apayao under the name of “Apayos”, with its capital named as Abulog (modern Abulug, in Cagayan province).
  • 1896

The Spaniards withdrew from this area with the outbreak of the Philippine Revolution. Therefore, this mountain country was largely uninvolved and practically unaffected by the revolution and the subsequent American Takeover. 

  • 1899

All Spanish missionaries were withdrawn, and the missions were left vacant for eight years.
  • February 6, 1901

When the Americans organized a civil provincial government for Cagayan, Kalinga and Apayo again became part of that province (Philippine Commission Act No. 83, enacted Feb 6, 1901)
  • 1907

The missionaries of the Immaculate Heart of Mary (CICM Fathers) were sent to the province by the Roman Catholic Church.

  • May 9, 1907

Kalinga was separated from Cagayan and became a sub-province of the former province of Lepanto-Bontoc; its sub-provincial capital was at Tabuk.  At the same time, Apayao became a sub-province of Cagayan, with its seat at Tauit. (Philippine Commission ACT NO. 1642, enacted May 9, 1907)
  • May 9, 1907

By virtue of ACT NO. 1642, two subprovinces to be known as the subprovince of Kalinga and the subprovince of Apayao were established.
  • August 18, 1908

Both of the sub-provinces of Kalinga and Apayao were annexed to the newly created Mountain province. The other sub-provinces of Mountain Province at that time included: Benguet, Amburaya, Lepanto, Bontoc and Ifugao (Philippine Legislature ACT NO. 1876, enacted Aug 18, 1908)
  • 1925

Parishes were founded in Lubuangan

  • 1927

Parishes were founded in Naneng (Tabuk) in Kalinga and Kabugao in Apayao, followed by ten more in later years. (Catholic Directory of the Philippines, 1976 as cited in Vance, 1980)

  • June 16, 1950

By virtue of REPUBLIC ACT 533, the Municipal District of Tabuk, Sub-province of Kalinga, Mountain Province was converted into a regular municipality.
  • June 18, 1966

The two  sub-provinces were separated from Mountain Province and combined to form a new province named Kalinga-Apayao, enacted by virtue of REPUBLIC ACT NO. 4695.
  • June 19, 1971

By virtue of REPUBLIC ACT NO. 6183, the name of the municipality of Liwan, province of Kalinga-Apayao, was changed to the municipality of Rizal.
  • February 14, 1995

By virtue of REPUBLIC ACT NO. 7878, the sub-provinces of Kalinga and Apayao were converted into regular provinces to be known as the province of Kalinga and the province of Apayao, amending for the purpose R.A. No. 4695.

  • March 23, 2007

By virtue of REPUBLIC ACT NO. 9404, the municipality of Tabuk was converted into a component city.

 
  • As of 2013

The current municipalities of Kalinga are: Balbalan, Lubuagan, Pasil, Pinukpuk, Rizal (Liwan), Tabuk, Tanudan, and Tinglayan.
