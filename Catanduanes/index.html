HISTORY

CATANDUANES

The following text/information are lifted from the book of Lee W. Vance entitled Tracing your Philippine Ancestors which was published in Provo, Utah by Stevenson’s Genealogical Center in 1980 and Symbols of the State by the Department of Interior and Local Government of the Republic of the Philippines in 1998.

The province of Catanduanes is located on the eastern side of the Philippines, just off the eastern tip of Camarines Sur of Bicol Peninsula. It is bounded on the north, east and south by the Pacific Ocean and on the west by the Maqueda Channel, separating it from the Bicol Peninsula. 

Catanduanes has a land area of 1,492.16 square kilometers. In 2007, it had a population of 232, 757.  

Following is a timeline 1,2 of events that led to the creation and development of the province of Catanduanes:

DATE

EVENT
  • 1573

The island was first explored by Juan de Salcedo, who found it already settled by Malays presumably descended from the Borneans who settled in Panay in the early 13th century.  
  • 1582

Four encomiendas were formed as part of the province of “Vicor and Camarines” on the island, and ruled from the capital of Nueva Caceres (Naga, Camarines Sur).
  • 1591

Catanduanes formed part of the province “Vicor and Camarines”.
  • 1596

The Christianization of Catanduanes begun by Franciscan missionaries from the Bicol mainland.
  • 1600

The old Bicol province was split and Ibalon (Albay) province formed, Catanduanes was assigned to its jurisdiction and ruled from its capital, Albay (Legazpi).

At this time, Caramoan parishes were also established.
  • 1650

Pandan parishes were established.
  • 1661

Viga parishes were established.
  • 1683

Payo (now named Panganiban) parishes were established.
  • 1755

Moros overran the island of Catanduanes and burned the towns including ecclesiastical and municipal records.

The parish at Virac, the eventual capital and diocesan seat was founded.

  • 1885

Catanduanes still was not a separate province.
  • 1898

Catanduanes had been made a separate politico-military province with its capital at Virac.
  • July 16, 1901

By virtue of the Philippine Commission 169 was the island was annexed to Albay as municipalities thereof. Again it became part of its mother province.

  • April 19, 1905

When Philippine Commission Act 1336 was enacted, Catanduanes was made a sub-province of Albay.
  • October 31, 1906

By virtue of the Phil. Commission Act 1557, major municipal consolidations occurred for Baras was joined to Bato, Bato lost “the visita of Cabugao” to Virac, Payo and Bagamanoc were annexed to Viga, and Caramoran was joined to Pandan.

  • As of 2010

Catanduanes constitutes a lone district that includes the municipilaties of Bagamanoc, Baras, Bato, Caramoran, Gigmoto, Pandan, Panganiban, San Andres, San Miguel, Viga and Virac.
