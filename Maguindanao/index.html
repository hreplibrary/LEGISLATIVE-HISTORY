HISTORY

MAGUINDANAO

The following text/information are lifted from the book of Lee W. Vance entitled Tracing your Philippine Ancestors which was published in Provo, Utah by Stevenson’s Genealogical Center in 1980 and Symbols of the State by the Department of Interior and Local Government of the Republic of the Philippines in 1998.

 

The province of Maguindanao lies on the southwestern coast of Mindanao Island, in the southern Philippines.  It is bounded on the north by Lanao del Sur, on the east by North Cotabato, on the south by Sultan Kudarat, and on the west by the Illana Bay of the Moro Gulf.

Maguindanao shares its history with its sister provinces of North Cotabato, Sultan Kudarat and South Cotabato.

From long before the beginning of recorded history, the approximate territory belonging to the four provinces of North Cotabato, South Cotabato, Maguindanao and Sultan Kudarat comprised the single ancient province of Cotabato.  It was the largest province of the Philippine Archipelago, occupying the entire southwestern quarter of Mindanao Island.

Ancient relics and jewelry, traditions and folklore of the natives, and their stubborn resistance to Spanish authority, indicate that the Cotabato region of Mindanao had been occupied b primitive mountain tribes, and by Mohammedan Moros, for many centuries before the first Spaniard ever set foot on Philippine soil.  Cotabato village was settled originally when the Manobos (or Manubas or Man-subas, the river dwellers), lived in the Cotabato area.

The name “Maguindanao,” later shortened to “Mindanao”. Means “that which was inundated”, and was the original name given to the town of Cotabato, which was periodically flooded by the adjacent Cotabato River.  “Cotabato”, on the other hand, is derived from the Moro term “Kuta”, meaning fort or fortification.  A stone fort, called “Kota wato” by the Muslims, formerly stood on a hill overlooking the town of Cotabato.

Following is a time line1,2 of events that led to the creation and development of the province of Maguindanao:

DATE

EVENT
  • End of the 15th Century

Islam was introduced into the area and the Sultanate of Maguindanao was founded by Sharif Mohammed Kabungsuwan near the end of the Fifteenth Century.

  • 1525

The first Spanish expedition into this area was led by Garcia Jofre Loaisa from Spain.
  • October 1526

Loaisa reached the coasts of Mindanao (called “Bendanao” by Urdaneta but no attempt to occupy the region was made at that time due to lack of provisions, munitions and men, and the hostile attitude of the native Moros.
  • Late 16th Century

Because of the arrival of new Christian settlers, new Christian churches were established by the Jesuit missionaries, but soon left.  

  • 1639

Pedro Almonte established a small presidio at Buhayon, but this post was soon abandoned.
  • 1640

The Moros of Cotabato were left to themselves, in practically undisputed control of the region, recognizing no authority except that of their datus or sultan, and obeying no laws but their own until the mid-19th century.
  • 19th Century

Jesuit missionaries returned after 3 centuries and established a Christian center at Tamontaka.  They made a little headway with the Muslims, but found better success with the pagans.  Their mission work was disrupted when Spain lost the Philippines, and only one Spanish Jesuit remained for the whole province of Cotabato. 

  • 1851

Spanish military authorities had discovered the strategic position of Polloc (now in Parang, Maguindanao) and attacked and occupied Polloc, converting it into a Spanish naval base, and from that point extending Spanish control into the adjacent area.
  • 1854

Polloc was made a politico-military district under the jurisdiction of Zamboanga.
  • 1858

When Davao was made a separate new district of Mindanao, the area of southern Cotabato surrounding Sarangani Bay fell under the jurisdiction of Glan, then a politico-military comandancia under Davao.
  • 1861

Three separate campaigns were made against the Moros.
  • 1862

The pueblo of Cotabato was founded, and made the capital of the new district of Cotabato.  The same year, a Spanish military base was established at Tamontaka, followed by the occupation of the adjacent interior towns and the establishment of military garrisons in them. 

  • 1872

Cotabato was more advanced than any other region in Mindanao, and was the temporary capital of the whole island for three years.

  • 1887

The three pueblos of Cotabato, Polloc and Tamontaka had been organized.
  • 1898

By the end of the Spanish regime, the Cotabato politico-military district, or the “5th District of Mindanao”, included the dependent politico-military comandancia of Polloc, & the military posts of Malabang, Reina Regente, Taceran, Babia, Illana, Baras and Lebac.
  • Early 1899

The Philippine Revolution had a little effect in Cotabato until Spain ceded the Philippines to America, thus compelling all Spaniards to evacuate from the province.
  • June 1, 1903

To fill the void, a native government was established under Roman Vilo for the Christian towns, and the arrival of Moro government was also organized under Datu Piang.  The Moros remained largely independent, but the government of the Christian towns was later turned over to the U.S. Military until this date.

  • Cotabato was made one of the five districts of the newly-created Moro Province by virtue of Philippine Commission Act 787, enacted June 1, 1903.
  • 1914

Moro Province was dissolved, and its districts, including Cotabato, were organized as separate provinces under the general supervision of the new Department of Mindanao and Sulu of the central national government.

  • 1939

The Land Settlement Act was implemented. Thousands of settlers began to enter Cotabato, coming from Luzon and the Visayas, and settling along the coastal and river plains inland as far as Pikit.  New municipalities and barrios grew faster than they could be organized.

  • 1942

Japanese invasion of the country. Cotabato town was burned, so as not to provide shelter for the Japanese, thus becoming a “ghost town”.
  • April 17, 1945

Resistance forces were established, and Cotabato was finally liberated on this date.
  • 1947

There were only three organized municipalities in the whole province:  Cotabato, Midsayap and Dulawan (Datu-Piang).
  • June 12, 1954

By virtue of REPUBLIC ACT 1034, the name of barrio of Banawa, in the municipality of Kabagan, province of Cotabato, was changed to Banawag.
  • June 12, 1954

By virtue of REPUBLIC ACT 1035, THE NAME OF THE MUNICIPALITY OF DULAWAN was changed to Datu Piang.
  • June 15, 1954

By virtue of REPUBLIC ACT 1107, the name of the municipality of Buayan, in the province of Cotabato, was changed to General Santos.
  • June 10, 1955

By virtue of REPUBLIC ACT 1248, the municipality of Upi was created.
  • 1957

There was only one remaining organized municipality – the capital, Cotabato -- but 37 municipal districts had been organized.  Problems of sparse population, lack of revenue, lack of roads and transportation, and vast territory seemed insurmountable under the then existing organizational system, so the partitioning of Cotabato began.
  • May 7, 1959

By virtue of REPUBLIC ACT 2189, the municipality of Maitum, province of Cotabato, was created.
  • June 20, 1959
 By virtue of REPUBLIC ACT 2364, the City of Cotabato was created.
  • June 21, 1959

By virtue of REPUBLIC ACT 2509, the municipality of Ampatuan, province of Cotabato, was created.

  • June 21, 1959

By virtue of REPUBLIC ACT 2587, the name of the municipality of Lambayong, province of Cotabato, was changed to Sultan sa Barongis.
  • June 19, 1960

By virtue of REPUBLIC ACT 2835, certain barrios in the municipality of Midsayap, province of Cotabato, were created.
  • June 18, 1961

By virtue of REPUBLIC ACT 3357, certain barrios in the municipality of Nuling, province of Cotabato, were created.
  • June 18, 1961

By virtue of REPUBLIC ACT 3419, the municipality of Buldon, province of Cotabato, was created.
  • June 18, 1961

By virtue of REPUBLIC ACT 3420, the municipality of Surallah in the province of Cotabato, was created.
  • June 22, 1963

REPUBLIC ACT 3664 amended REPUBLIC ACT 3420
  • June 22, 1963

By virtue of REPUBLIC ACT 3721, the municipality of Magpet in the province of Cotabato, was created.
  • July 18, 1966

South Cotabato was separated to form a new, independent province by virtue of Republic Act 4849, approved July 18, 1966.  South Cotabato’s town then included Norala, Surala, Banga, Tantangan, Koronadal, Tupi, Polomolok, Kiamba, Maitum, Maasim, Tampacan, and Glan, and the city of Rajah Buayan (General Santos) with the capital at Koronadal.

  • May 8, 1967

By virtue of REPUBLIC ACT 4868, the municipality of Lutayan, province of Cotabato, was created.
  • May 8, 1967

By virtue of REPUBLIC ACT 4869, the municipality of president Roxas in the province of Cotabato, was created
  • June 21, 1969

By virtue of REPUBLIC ACT 5645, the municipality of Alamada in the province of Cotabato, was created.
  • June 21, 1969

By virtue of REPUBLIC ACT 5647, the name of the municipality of Nuling in the province of Cotabato, was changed to Sultan Kudarat.
  • June 21, 1969

By virtue of REPUBLIC ACT 5960, the municipality of Bagumbayan in the province of Cotabato, was recreated.
  • November 22, 1973

Since the smaller area aided development, and was governmentally more manageable, it was decided to split the remaining portion of Cotabato into smaller provinces.  This was accomplished in November 22 1973, when Cotabato was divided into the three provinces of Maguindanao, North Cotabato, and Sultan Kudarat by virtue of PD 341.

  • 1978

 Maguindanao was part of the representation of Region XII.
  • March 25, 1982

By virtue of BATAS PAMBANSA BILANG 211, the seat of government of the province of Maguindanao from the municipality of Sultan Kudarat was returned to the municipality of Maganoy, repealing for the purpose P.D. No. 1170.
  • 1984-1986

Maguindanao was first granted its own representations in the Regular Batasang Pambansa. Two assemblymen were elected at-large.
  • 1986

Maguindanao was divided into two legislative districts.
  • August 30, 1991

By virtue of REPUBLIC ACT 7152, the municipality of Arakan in the province of Cotabato was created.
  • 2007

The first district of Maguindanao became the Lone District of Shariff Kabunsuan and Cotabato City, while the second district became the Lone District of Maguindanao. The first district was then composed of Cotabato City and the municipalities of Barira, Buldon, Datu Blah T. Sinsuat, Kabuntalan, Matanog, Northern Kabuntalan, Parang, Sultan Kudarat, Sultan Mastura, and Upi. 

  • July 16, 2008

The creation of Shariff Kabunsuan was voided by the Supreme Court since and reverted to the jurisdiction of the province of Maguindanao.
  • 2013

Maguindanao is composed of the following municipalities: Ampatuan, Buluan, Datu Paglas, Datu Piang, Gen. S.K. Pendatun, Mamasapano, Pagagawan, Pagalungan, Shariff Aguak (Maganoy), South Upi, Sultan sa Barongis, Talayan, and Talitay.
