HISTORY

ILOILO

The following text/information are lifted from the book of Lee W. Vance entitled Tracing your Philippine Ancestors which was published in Provo, Utah by Stevenson’s Genealogical Center in 1980 and Symbols of the State by the Department of Interior and Local Government of the Republic of the Philippines in 1998.

The province of Iloilo is situated on the southeastern portion of Panay Island in the Western Visayas region of the central Philippines.  Mountain ranges separate it from Capiz on the north and Antique on the west.  The province includes several smaller islands that dot the southeastern coast.  The largest of these is Guimaras Island, which forms a sub-province of Iloilo.

Following is a time line 1,2 of events that led to the creation and development of the province of Iloilo:

DATE

EVENT
  • 13th century

A group of Malaysians from Borneo, including 10 datus and about a thousand of their followers sailed from their homes desiring to escape the oppression and cruelties of the ruling maharajah of Borneo, and to set up a new life under a less oppressive government. 

  • 1213

They landed in a place called Sinogbuhan (traditionally in modern San Joaquin municipality),Taghurokad (Saturday) of Daghug Kahoy (February), of the year 1213, they bought part of the island for one gold sadok for the Negrito King Marikudo, and a gold necklace for his queen, and other treasures.  The Negritos later moved to the highlands of the island’s interior
  • 1580-1583

Governor-General Ronquillo enlarged Arevalo and made it the capital of an alcaldia or province named “Panay”, which included the islands of Panay, Guimaras and Negros (at least the western half)
  • 1565

Miguel Lopez de Legazpi established the first Philippine center of Spanish at Cebu.  But due to the hostile conditions there, and the continual menace of Portugese “pirates”, Legazpi transferred his encampment to the settlement of Panay on the northern coast of Panay Island, now in modern-day Capiz.  The Spaniards named the island “Isla de Pintados” because the natives were fond of tattoos
  • 1570

Martin de Goiti’s expedition explored the island and found many well-established settlements which later were organized into towns, including Oton and Arevalo (now a district of Iloilo City)
  • 1571

Legazpi did not remain long in Panay and transferred his capital to Manila
  • 1572

The Augustinians founded early Christian missions and parishes in the towns of Oton and Dumangas

Capiz became a separate politico-military district and was the first to be split off from the ancient province of Panay
  • 1734

The western half of Negros Island was separated from Panay province, and the eastern half was separated from Cebu and organized into a single politico-military district
  • 1790

Antique was organized as a politico-military district from parts of Iloilo and Capiz provinces
  • 1798

the districts of Antique and Negros Island became full-fledged provinces
  • 19th century

The politico-military district of Concepcion was created, comprising the northeastern portion of Iloilo & the towns of Carles, Balasan, Estancia, Batad, San Dionisio, Concepcion (the capital of the district), Sara, Ajuy, Lemery, Alaminos and San Juan. 

  • 1856

The port of Iloilo was opened to international trade, thus increasing its prosperit and the influx of foreign visitors and immigrants into the Visayan region.
  • 1898

Philippine revolutionary forces occupied Iloilo City on Christmas Day
  • 1899

American warships shelled the city’s defenses.
  • 1901

The district of Concepcion was dissolved and annexed to Iloilo by the Americans.

  • February 2, 1901

The revolutionaries who set fire to the city and withdrew when the Americans landed fought for nearly two years, but surrendered to the Americans on this date.
  • April 11, 1901

The Americans established a civil government for the province, replacing the interim American military command; Iloilo, now reduced from a city to a municipality, was declared the capital, as previously under the Spanish.
  • April 4, 1903

The 51 municipalities of Iloilo were reduced to 17 But since that time, these have been subdivided to form the present total of 47 municipalities and one city, Iloilo City.  Three of these municipalities comprise the island province of Guimaras, off the southeast coast of Iloilo, which was organized in 1966 with its seat at Jordan by virtue of Philippine Commission Act 719.

Iloilo has 6 districts. The 1st district is composed of the municipalities of Guimbal, Igbaras, Miagao, oton, San Joaquin, Tigbauan, and Tubungan. The 2nd district is composed of the municipalities of Alimodian, Leganes, Leon, New Lucena, Pavia, San Miguel, Santa Barbara, and Zarraga. The 3rd district is composed of the municipalities of Badiangan, Bingawan, Cabatuan, Calinog, Janiuay, Lambunao, Maasin, Mina, and Pototan. The 4th district is composed of the municipalities of Anilao, Banate, Barotac Viejo, Dingle, Dueñas, Dumangas, San Enrique, and Passi City. The 5th district is composed of the municipalities of Ajuy, Balasan, Barotac Nuevo, Batad, Carles, Concepcion, Estancia, Lemery, San Dionisio, San Rafael, and Sara. Iloilo City is a lone district.
