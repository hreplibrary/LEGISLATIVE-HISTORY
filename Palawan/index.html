HISTORY

PALAWAN
The following text/information are lifted from the book of Lee W. Vance entitled Tracing your Philippine Ancestors which was published in Provo, Utah by Stevenson’s Genealogical Center in 1980 and Symbols of the State by the Department of Interior and Local Government of the Republic of the Philippines in 1998.

The long, narrow province of Palawan lies west of the main portion of the central Philippine Archipelago, pointing like a dagger towards the northeastern corner of the Malayan island of Borneo.  It is the largest province in the nation, and is comprised of 1,769 islands, the most important of which are the main island of Palawan, and the islands of Busuanga, Culion, Linapacan, Cuyo, Dumaran, the Cagayanes group, and Balabac Island.  The South China Sea lies to the west, and the Sulu Sea lies to the east.

By the end of Spanish rule, Palawan (or Paragua, as it was called then) had been divided again into three politico-military provinces.  “Calamianes” Province included the Calamianes and Cuyos island groups; Linapacan, Dumaran and Agutaya islands; and the northern part of Palawan Island.  It included the pueblos of Cuyo (the capital), Culion and Agutaya.  “Paragua” Province included the rest of Palawan, or Paragua, Island not already part of Calamianes, and the pueblos of Puerto Princes (the capital), Bacuit, Danlig, Dumaran, Talindan and Taytay.  The third province was “Balabac”, which included the island of the same name, with its capital at the sole pueblo of Balabac.

Following is a timeline1,2 of events that led to the creation and development of the province of Palawan:

DATE

EVENT
  • 12th and 13th centuries

Comparatively little authoritative information is written about Palawan’s ancient history, but it is believed that this area was probably settled b the ancestors of the primitive Palawans, Tagbanuas, and Batacs, even before Malay settlers from the Madjapahit Empire in Indonesia filtered into these islands
  • 1521

Members of Magellan’s expedition may have landed here
  • 1591

Calamianes was an encomienda under the jurisdiction of Balayan province, now known as Batangas.
  • 1622

Augustinian missionaries were the first to preach in the region, landing first in Cuyo then proceeding to the other nearby islands.  Early missions were founded in Cuyo, Taytay and Agutaya.
  • 1692

Missions were also founded in Cagayancillo, with others following much later. Ferocious Moro raids attempted to undo the process of Christianization, and several times succeeded in driving the missionaries away for long periods of time, if they escaped with their lives.  The Recollects attempted to re-establish the missions, and finally succeeded when they built accompanying forts garrisoned wit soldiers for protection.
  • Early 18th century

The Spaniards entered the Moro territory, built a fort and stationed a garrison of nearly 700 soldiers at the town of Taytay on the northern end of Palawan Island.  From that time, Taytay became a bulwark of Spanish influence and Christianity.
  • 1730 and 1735

The Moros made desperate attempts to capture the fort, but failed. Several other minor attempts also failed to dislodge the Spanish influence there.
  • 1749

The Sultanate ceded the southern part of Palawan to the Spanish, permitting them to establish their authority over the whole island.  Shortly after the ratification of the treaty, the Spaniards attempted to establish settlements in the newly acquired territory; but a considerable number of the settlers died of fever (probably malaria), and the effort was abandoned.
  • 1818

Practically all the territory of modern Palawan had been organized as a single province named “Calamianes”, with its capital at Taytay.
  • 1858

Calamianes was divided to form the two provinces of Castilla and Asturias.  Castilla comprised the Calamianes Island group and the northern part of Palawan Island, with its capital at Taytay.  Asturias included the rest of Palawan Island, and had its capital at Puerto Princesa.  At the same time, Balabac Island was made a separate province of the same name, with its capital at the town of Principe Alfonso. 

  • 1872

The entire island of Palawan was organized as a politico-military province, then called “Paragua”, with Puerto Princesa as its capital
  • 1885

The sparse population of Palawan inhibited its development, so a settlement scheme was inaugurated.
  • 1896

The Philippine Revolution had little direct effect in Paragua, although the Spanish authorities did deport a large number of prisoners in Palawan to Balabac for alleged complicity with the Katipunanan or other subversive activities.  After Spain’s defeat, the American military took over the short-lived provisional revolutionary government.
  • June 23, 1902

A Civil government for the province of Paragua was established by the Americans by virtue of Philippine Commission Act 422. It then included practically the same territory as the former province of Castilla or Calamianes, the Cuyos and Calamianes island groups, Dumaran I. and the part of Palawan Island north of the 10th parallel of latitude.  Its capital was located at Cuyo.  Significantly, it did not include Puerto Princesa, which was the seat of “Southern Paragua”, a district which at that time remained under a U.S. military government.
  • May 14, 1903

The provincial boundaries were further revised to include all of Paragua, or Palawan, Island, together with essentially all of the other islands and territory it has today.  The capital was transferred from Cuyo back to Puerto Princesa by virtue of Philippine Commission Act 747.
  • June 1905

Paragua province was renamed “Palawan” by virtue of Philippine Commission Act 1363
  • June 28, 1905

By virtue of ACT NO. 1363, the name of the province and island of Paragua was changed to that of Palawan
  • September 14, 1905

Palawan was made a “special” province, its towns being simplified townships, until it was restored to “regular” provincial status later by virtue of Philippine Commission Act  Nos. 1396 and 1397

Palawan has 2 districts. The 1st district is composed of the municipalities of Agutaya, Araceli, Busuanga, Cagayancillo, Coron, Culion, Cuyo, Dumaran, El Nido, Kalayaan, Linapacan, Magsaysay, Roxas, San Vicente and Taytay. The 2nd district is composed of Aborlan, Balabac, Bataraza, Brooke’s Point, Narra, Quezon, Rizal, Sofronio Española, and Puerto Princesa City.
