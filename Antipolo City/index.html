HISTORY

ANTIPOLO CITY

The following text/information are lifted from the book of Lee W. Vance entitled Tracing your Philippine Ancestors which was published in Provo, Utah by Stevenson’s Genealogical Center in 1980 and Symbols of the State by the Department of Interior and Local Government of the Republic of the Philippines in 1998.

Antipolo City is located in the province of Rizal, about 25 kilometers east of Manila. Antipolo is landlocked; bounded to the north by San Mateo and Rodriguez; to the northeast by Gen. Nakar in neighboring Quezon Province; to the southeast by Tanay; to the south by Angono, Taytay and Teresa; and to the west by Cainta and Marikina City in Metro Manila.

Antipolo City has a land area of 306.10 square kilometers. In 2007, it had a population of 633,971.

Following is a timeline1,2 of events that led to the creation and development of the province of Antipolo City:

DATE

EVENT
  • 13th Century

Ancient settlements were already thriving in Paranaque, Pasig, Cainta and Taytay.  These settlements were believed to be known to Chinese, Japanese and Indian traders.
  • 1571

Juan de Salcedo visited Taytay and Cainta and brought these towns under Spanish sovereignty.
  • 1578

Franciscan missionaries arrived and built a church in Boso-Boso.
  • 1591

Franciscans were replaced by Jesuits who organized the village into a parish.
  • March 25, 1626

The Marian image of the Our Lady of Peace and Good Voyage or the Virgin of Antipolo, which was brought in from Acapulco, Mexico by Governor- General Juan Niño de Tavera, and enshrined in the Antipolo Cathedral.
  • 1639

A Chinese uprising caused great rioting throughout Rizal.  The Chinese burned the churches of Taytay, San Mateo and Pasig. The Spaniards were able to quell the uprising but not without great loss of life and property.
  • 1650

The village of Antipolo was organized into a town and became part of Tondo Province.

  • 1689

The town of Cainta was founded in 1689 while Marikina was founded the following year.
  • 1762

British troops under the command of Captain Thomas Backhouse invaded Pasig, defeated the Spaniards and occupied Taytay and Cainta.
  • 1764

The British withdrew from the islands.
  • 1815

The town of Pateros was founded.
  • 1853

The Tondo Province was divided and Antipolo became part of the District of San Mateo de los Montes.

  • 1859

The name Distrito de Los Montes de San Mateo was changed to Morong District.  At about the same time, Tondo province was renamed Manila province.  At about this time, Navotas was founded.
  • 1864

The Recollects took over Antipolo. It was during these years that the Virgin of Antipolo gained a following of devotees.
  • 1898

American military forces controlled the Morong district.
  • June 4, 1899

The town served as the capital of Morong until it was occupied by Americans. The Revolutionary Government then transferred Morong’s capital to Tanay.
  • 1901

Americans established Province of Rizal which included towns of Morong District and Manila Province.

  • 1903

Rizal’s various municipalities were combined as follows - Montalban with San Mateo; Taguig and Muntinglupa with Pateros; Malibay with Pasay; Las Pinas with Paranaque, Novaliches with Caloocan; Navotas with Malabon, San Felipe Neri (Mandaluyong) with San Juan del Monte, Cainta and Angono with Taytay; Bosoboso and Teresa with Antipolo, Binangonan, Baras and cardona with Morong; and Quisao and Jalajala with Pililla. 

A month later Binangonan was separated from Morong and Angono was annexed to it.  At the same time, Muntinglupa was separated from Pateros, and annexed as a barrio to Binan, Laguna and later annexed (still as a barrio) to Taguig, itself newly formed from the former pueblos of Pateros, Taguig and Muntinglupa.
  • December 24, 1908

The Manila Railroad Company inaugurated a railway service to Antipolo.
  • 1919

Teresa became an independent municipality.
  • March 6-7, 1945

The bombing during the Second World War destroyed the Antipolo church.
  • March 12, 1945

The American and Filipino soldiers and local guerrillas liberated the town.
  • October 15, 1945

A temporary church was built and the Virgin of ANtipolo was returned from the Quiapo Church.
  • June 15, 1952

Hinulugang Taktak was proclaimed a National Park by Pres. Elpidio Quirino.
  • January 14, 1954

The Bishops of the Philippines proclaimed the Cathedral of Antipolo as the official shrine of the Virgin of Antipolo.
  • April 27, 1984

Barangays Beverly Hills, Bagong Nayon, San Juan, Barangay Sta. Cruz, Muntindilaw, San Luis,and Inarawan were constituted through BP 787, 789, BP 790, BP 791, 792, BP 793, and BP 794, respectively.
  • April 4, 1998

Republic Act (RA) No. 8508, Antipolo was converted from a municipality into a component city of Rizal Province.
  • March 22, 2001

By virtue of RA 9047, the number of representatives of the City of Antipolo to the Sangguniang Panlalawigan of Rizal from the present one (1) Sanggunian member to two (2) members, amending for the purpose RA 8508 was increased.

  • December 22, 2003

RA 8508 or the “act converting the municipality of Antipolo into a component city to be known as the City of Antipolo” was further amended by virtue of RA 9232.
  • As of 2013

Antipolo City constitutes two legislative districts. Listed are the barangays under each district:
1st District: Barangays Bagong Nayon, Beverly Hills, Dela Paz, Mambugan, Mayamot, San Isidro, Santa Cruz, and Munting Dilao.
2nd District: Barangays Calawis, Cupang, Dalig, Inarawan, San Jose (Poblacion), San Juan, San Luis, and San Roque.
