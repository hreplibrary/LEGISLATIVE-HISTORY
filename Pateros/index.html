HISTORY

 

MUNICIPALITY OF PATEROS

The following text/information are lifted from the book of Lee W. Vance entitled Tracing your Philippine Ancestors which was published in Provo, Utah by Stevenson’s Genealogical Center in 1980 and Symbols of the State by the Department of Interior and Local Government of the Republic of the Philippines in 1998.

Pateros is the only municipality in Metro Manila. Pateros is bordered by Pasig City to the north, Makati City to the west, and Taguig City to the south.

Pateros has a land area of 2.1 square kilometers. In 2007, it had a population of 61,940.

Following is a timeline1,2 of events that led to the creation and development of the municipality of Pateros:

DATE

E V E N T
  • 13th Century

Ancient settlements were already thriving in Paranaque, Pasig, Cainta and Taytay.  These settlements were believed to be known to Chinese, Japanese and Indian traders.
  • 1571

Juan de Salcedo visited Taytay and Cainta and brought these towns under Spanish sovereignty.
  • 1587

Christian missions established parishes in Taguig and was established as a separate “pueblo” (town) of the then province of Manila.
  • 1590

San Juan became a small encomienda after being a village.

Formerly a barrio of Santa Ana de Sapa, which was ruled by King Lacantagean and his wife Bouan, the town derived its name from the patron sint San Juan de Bautista (Saint John the Baptist), and “Del Monte,” often annexed to it, was named after the hilly structure of the land.

  • 1602

The Dominican friars built a retreat house for their immediate use, where aging convalescing friars stayed.

Later, the Dominicans constructed a convent sanctuary and a stone church dedicated to the Holy Cross, a church destined to be ravaged during the Spanish occupation.
  • 1639

A Chinese uprising caused great rioting throughout Rizal.  The Chinese burned the churches of Taytay, San Mateo and Pasig. The Spaniards were able to quell the uprising but not without great loss of life and property.
  • 1689

The town of Cainta was founded in 1689 while Marikina was founded the following year.
  • 1762

British troops under the command of Captain Thomas Backhouse invaded Pasig, defeated the Spaniards and occupied Taytay and Cainta.
  • 1764

The British withdrew from the islands.
  • Before 1770

Pateros was only a barrio of Pasig until the Spanish Governor-General in the Philippines issued a decree making Pateros an independent municipality.
  • 1783

San Juan became independent of Santa Ana but it was still then a barrio.
  • 1815

The town of Pateros was founded.
  • 1853

“Distrito de los Montes de San Mateo”, or District of the San Mateo Mountains was formed.  Cainta, Taytay, Antipolo and Bosoboso were annexed to this new district from Tondo province.  Angono, Binangonan, Cardona, Morong, Baras, Tanay, Pililla and Jalajala were annexed to the district from Laguna province.  Morong was made the capital of the District.
  • 1859

The name Distrito de Los Montes de San Mateo was changed to Morong District.  At about the same time, Tondo province was renamed Manila province.  At about this time, Navotas was founded.
  • 1864

The Recollects took over Antipolo. It was during these years that the Virgin of Antipolo gained a following of devotees.
  • 1889

Filipino forces including Taguig “revolutionaries” dislodged and American position in the hills of Taguig, now a portion of Pateros and Fort Bonifacio. They were defeated eventually by the Americans with superiority in the armaments and training.
  • 1898

American military forces controlled the Morong district.
  • June 11, 1901

Rizal province was created by fusing the former province of Manila, except the City of Manila, with the former district of Morong.  Pasig was declared the provincial capital.
Pateros became one of the towns in the newly created province of Rizal, by virtue of General Order No. 40, Act No. 137 of the Philippine Commission.
  • 1903

Rizal’s various municipalities were combined as follows – Montalban with San Mateo; Taguig and Muntinglupa with Pateros; Malibay with Pasay; Las Pinas with Paranaque, Novaliches with Caloocan; Navotas with Malabon, San Felipe Neri (Mandaluyong) with San Juan del Monte, Cainta and Angono with Taytay; Bosoboso and Teresa with Antipolo, Binangonan, Baras and cardona with Morong; and Quisao and Jalajala with Pililla. 

A month later Binangonan was separated from Morong and Angono was annexed to it.  At the same time, Muntinglupa was separated from Pateros, and annexed as a barrio to Binan, Laguna and later annexed (still as a barrio) to Taguig, itself newly formed from the former pueblos of Pateros, Taguig and Muntinglupa.
  • March 22, 1905

After naming the combined Pateros, Taguig and Munitinlupa under Pateros, the municipality was renamed Taguig by virtue of Act No. 1308.

  • February 29, 1908

Pursuant to Executive Order No. 20, Pateros was separated from Taguig.
  • January 1, 1909

Pateros regained status as a municipality by virtue of Executive Order No. 36.
  • November 7, 1975

Pateros became part of the new Metropolitan Manila Area through Presidential Decree No. 824.
  • As of 2010

The Municipality of Pateros constitutes Barangays Pususan, Tuktukan, Sta. Ana, Kalsada, Palingon, Ligid Tipas, Ibayo Tipas, Napindan, Bambang, Wawa, Hagonoy, Lower Bicutan and Bagumbayan in one district.
