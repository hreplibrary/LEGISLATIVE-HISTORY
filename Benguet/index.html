HISTORY

BENGUET

The following text/information are lifted from the book of Lee W. Vance entitled Tracing your Philippine Ancestors which was published in Provo, Utah by Stevenson’s Genealogical Center in 1980 and Symbols of the State by the Department of Interior and Local Government of the Republic of the Philippines in 1998.

Benguet is located in the mountains of north-coast of Luzon Island. It is bounded on the north by Ilocos Sur and Mountain Province, on the east by Ifugao and Nueva Vizcaya on the South of Pangasinan, and on the west by Ilocos Sur and La Union.

Benguet has a land area of 2,826.59 square kilometers. In 2007, it had a population of 372,533.

Following is a timeline1, 2 of events that led to the creation and development of the province of Benguet:

DATE

EVENT
  • 1642

The “land of the Igorots” was visited by Alonso Martin Quirante, Comandant of Pangasinan.
  • 1755

An Agustinian friar held the first mass at La Trinidad.
  • 1756

Agustinians established a mission in Tondo, near Tuba, making occasional visits from parent parish at Agoo (La Union) but only lasted for three years.
  • 1829

Comandante Guillermo Galvey led an expedition to the District of Benguet and led another 45 expeditions after.

  • 1846

Galvey established a comandancia at La Trinidad, which was named after his wife.
  • 1891

Another mission was founded at La Trinindad, and others were established at about the same time at Kopangan and Daklan (three miles north of Bodok).

  • 1898

All missions were deserted when the Spanish priests were compelled to leave the Philippine Islands.

  • November 23, 1900

A civil government was established by the Philippine Commission for the province of Benguet, with Baguio as the new capital. A simplified form of government was adopted for the towns of Baguio, Trinidad, Galiano, Itogon, Tublay, Atok, Kapangan, Balakbak, Palina, Ampusungan, Loo, Buguias, Kabayan, Adaoay, Bokod, Daklan, Sablan, Kibungan and Ambuklao.
  • 1907

The missions were re-established by the Congregation of the Immaculate Heart of Mary Fathers.
Baguio, Bontoc and Bauko missions were established.
  • 1908

Itogon and La Trinidad missions were established.
  • August 18, 1908

Benguet, along with Bontoc, Ifugao, Amburayan, Lepanto, Kalinga and Apayao, was made one of seven sub-provinces of Mountain Province.

  • 1909

Baguio became a chartered city.
  • 1910

Kiangan mission was established.
  • 1920

The boundary of Benguet was changed with the abolition of the provinces of Amburayan and Lepanto. Bakun was annexed to Benguet from the former sub-province of Lepanto.
  • 1950

By virtue of REPUBLIC ACT 531, the Municipal District of La Trinidad, Sub-province of Benguet, Mountain Province was converted into a regular municipality to be known as the Municipality of La Trinidad.
  • 1951

By virtue of REPUBLIC ACT 616, the Municipal District of Itogon, Sub-province of Benguet, Mountain Province was converted into a regular municipality to be known as the Municipality of Itogon.
  • 1955

By virtue of REPUBLIC ACT 1302, the Municipal District of Mankayan, Sub-province of Benguet, Mountain Province was converted into a municipality.
  • June 18, 1966

By virtue of Republic Act 4695, the provinces of Benguet, Mountain Province, Ifugao, and Kalinga Apayao were created dividing the former Mountain Province into four.
Benguet province included the municipalities of La Trinidad (the provincial capital), Tuba, Sablan, Itogon, Tublay, Atok, Bokod, Kabayan, Kapanga, Bakun, Kibungan, Manyakan and Buguias.

  • As of 2013

Benguet constitutes a lone district that includes Municipalities of Atok, Bakun, Bokod, Buguias, Itogon, Kabayan, Kapangan, Kibungan, La Trinidad, Mankayan, Sablan, Tuba, and Tublay.
