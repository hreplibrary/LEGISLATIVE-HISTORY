HISTORY

CAGAYAN DE ORO CITY


The following text/information are lifted from the book of Lee W. Vance entitled Tracing your Philippine Ancestors which was published in Provo, Utah by Stevenson’s Genealogical Center in 1980 and Symbols of the State by the Department of Interior and Local Government of the Republic of the Philippines in 1998.

Cagayan de Oro City is a highly urbanized city. It is bounded on the north by the Majalajar Bay, on the south and east by the Province of Bukidnon, and on the west by the town of Manticao. The village was originally a part of the Kingdom of Sultan Kudarat and its early settlers were of Malayan origin.

Following is a time line 1,2,3,4  of events that led to the creation and development of Zamboanga Sibugay:

DATE

EVENT
  • 377 A.D.

The area was first inhabited around 377 A.D. (the late Neolithic period), island natives lived in a settlement then known as Himologan  (now known as Huluga), eight kilometers from present day Cagayan de Oro. The natives were polytheistic animist and paid tributes to Sultan Muhammad Dipatuan Kudarat, the Sultan of Maguindanao.
  • 1622

At the time when the first Spanish missionaries came in, the people of Cagayan had tributary relation to Kudarat, the Muslim Sultan of Maguindanao empire in Cotabato.
  • 1626

Fray Agustin de San Pedro persuaded the chief of Himologan, Datu Salangsang, to transfer his settlement down river, to the present-day Gaston Park.
  • 1738

Spanish dominance was felt in Cagayan.
  • 1818

Misamis gained status of province in 1818, one of its four districts was the Partidos de Cagayan.
  • 1871

"Partidos" became a town and was made permanent capital of Misamis.
  • February 27, 1872

the Spanish Governor-General Carlos Maria de La Torre issued a decree declaring Cagayan the permanent capital of Segundo Distrito de Misamis. During this era, the name of the town was known as Cagayan de Misamis.
  • 1883

the town became seat of the Spanish government in Mindanao for the provinces of Misamis Oriental, Misamis Occidental, Bukidnon, Lanao del Norte.
  • 1898

The war years in Cagayan were prompted by the presence of the Americans.
  • December 1898

Before the Spaniards evacuated Misamis, the Spanish Governor organized a committee of five Filipinos, and turned over the government to them.

  • January 2, 1899

General Emilio Aguinaldo appointed two commissioners in order to hold elections and established a new government in Cagayan de Oro and Surigao.
  • January 10, 1899

Cagayan de Misamis joined the Philippine government of Emilio Aguinaldo and celebrated its independence from Spain. It was the second time the Aguinaldo government was declared and the new Philippine flag raised on the Mindanao island.
  • June 4, 1900

The Americans were initially and successfully repulsed by the local forces led by Major Apolinar Velez at the historic battle of Macahambus.
  • March 31, 1900

the Americans occupied Cagayan de Misamis.
  • April 7, 1900

battle erupted in the town center led by General Nicolas Capistrano and Filipino resistance fighters. This would later be known as the Battle of Cagayan de Misamis. The Americans won the war.
  • December 22, 1941

Japanese Air Force bombed Camp Evangelista.
  • May 1, 1942

Detachment sailed from Iloilo on this day and landed on the coast of Cagayan de oro Bay.

  • May 10, 1945

The Allied Forces occupied and captured Cagayan de Oro from the Japanese invaders.
  • June 15, 1950

President Elpidio Quirino signed REPUBLIC ACT NO. 521, which granted the status of a chartered city to the municipality of Cagayan de Oro.
  • June 15, 1950

With the passage of R.A. 521, the town of Cagayan de Oro became a city.
  • June 18, 1964

RA 3969 amended certain sections of R.A. 521, otherwise known as "Charter of Cagayan De Oro City".
  • June 18, 1966

RA 4663 created the Cagayan de Oro Port Authority to administers, manage, develop, improve, integrate, coordinate and mobilize terminal facilities in the area.
  • November 22, 1983

Cagayan de Oro was declared a highly-urbanized city by the Ministry of Local Government.
  • February 22, 2007

R.A. 9371 provided for the apportionment of the lone legislative district of the City of Cagayan De Oro.
  • 2010

Cagayan de Oro is politically subdivided into 80 barangays. These are grouped into two congressional districts: 24 barangays in the 1st District (West) and 56 barangays in the 2nd District (East), with Cagayan de Oro River as the natural boundary. The city has a 57 urban barangays and 23 rural barangays all in all.
